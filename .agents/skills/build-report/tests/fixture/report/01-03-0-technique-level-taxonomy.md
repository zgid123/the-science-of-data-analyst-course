## 1.3. Technique-Level Taxonomy in the Deep-Learning Era

Section 1.2 classified PAD systems by the source of their evidence and the role of the user. Inside the PAD component, the techniques that turn a captured sample into a decision form a second, finer taxonomy. The most recent systematic review of the field is "Presentation Attack Detection: A Systematic Literature Review" by Pooshideh, Beheshti, Qi and Farhood of Macquarie University together with Simpson, Gatland and Soltany of Locii Innovation [34]. It was published in ACM Computing Surveys, volume 57, issue 1, article 25, pages 1–32, online on 7 October 2024 [34]. It reviews face PAD as intensively studied over the last two decades across datasets, evaluation methods and attack types [34], and it compares representative methods on widely used datasets together with their strengths and weaknesses [34].

Over the same period the techniques themselves changed. A survey of deep-learning face anti-spoofing in IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence states that traditional methods based on hand-crafted features became unreliable because of their limited representation capacity [35]. A survey of face PAD with RGB cameras groups the techniques by the cue each exploits: liveness cues such as motion and blood pulse, texture cues, 3D geometric cues, multiple cues combined, and newer machine-learning methods [30]. This cue-based view organises the subsections that follow.

Building on it, Section 1.3.1 covers texture and frequency cues, Section 1.3.2 covers motion and physiological cues, and Section 1.3.3 covers deep classifiers, whose auxiliary supervision also carries the 3D geometric cue. Section 1.3.4 then examines cross-database generalisation, the problem that all families share, and Section 1.3.5 joins the system-level and technique-level taxonomies in one view.
