## 1.1. Definitions

Research on attacks at the biometric sensor became comparable once the field adopted one shared vocabulary. The term PAD was adopted in ISO/IEC 30107-1:2016, a joint standard of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC), and it is now largely accepted by the research community [1]. Surveys of the field follow the normative vocabulary of the standard wherever appropriate, so that results from different groups describe the same events in the same terms [2].

The ISO/IEC 30107 series provides this vocabulary in several parts. The first editions comprise Part 1 (framework, 2016), Part 2 (data formats, 2017) and Part 3 (testing and reporting, 2017) [2]. The second edition of Part 3 was published on 10 January 2023 by the joint technical committee ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 37 Biometrics [3]. In addition, Part 4 (first edition 2020, second edition 2024) defines a profile for testing mobile devices, and the FIDO Alliance biometric certification builds on it together with Part 3 [4].

Part 3 is the operative document for evaluation. It sets out principles and methods for the performance assessment of PAD mechanisms, rules for reporting test results, and a classification of known attack types in its Annex A [3]. Its scope covers attacks that take place at the biometric capture device during presentation, while overall system-level security and vulnerability assessment lie outside it [3]. The full texts are sold, and developers and test laboratories purchase their own copies [5]. The definitions below are therefore quoted from peer-reviewed publications that reproduce the standard's wording verbatim.

Building on this foundation, Section 1.1.1 defines presentation attacks and the instruments used in them, Section 1.1.2 introduces the error rates that measure PAD performance, Section 1.1.3 describes how the effort behind an attack is rated, and Section 1.1.4 draws the boundary between presentation attacks and digital injection attacks.
