## 5.3. Implications for Practice

For an organisation that deploys face verification in 2026, the conclusions translate into five requirements for a PAD solution and its supplier:

(1) **Certified PAD at a defined level.** A certification at ISO 30107 Level 2 or an equivalent level, which Vietnamese banking regulation now requires [98], tested by an accredited laboratory such as iBeta under ISO/IEC 30107-3 [20].
(2) **Tested injection attack detection.** Protection of the data stream between camera and processing, tested against CEN/TS 18099 [22]; iBeta has offered such testing since 4 June 2026 [88], and FIDO certification requires documented injection protection [79].
(3) **Demographic evidence.** Results across skin tone, age and gender, as the FIDO programme tests them [78].
(4) **Results under change.** Cross-dataset and unseen-attack results under independent protocols, since accuracy within one dataset predicts little about the field [35], [105].
(5) **Licence and certification status of research models.** A research model such as FSFM is released under the CC BY-NC 4.0 licence for research use, and as of 3 October 2026 no FSFM-based product holds an iBeta confirmation letter, a FIDO certification or a NIST FATE PAD result [107]; such a model enters production through a certified product built on it.

Taken together, PAD is ready to be required and still open to research. Its definition, its metrics and its certification route are settled, while detection that holds under new data, new devices and attacks that bypass the camera is where the next advances of the field will be measured.
