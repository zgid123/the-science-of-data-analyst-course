### 3.2.1. Test Levels and Protocol

iBeta tests at three points of a biometric system. A PAD subsystem test evaluates the PAD component only and reports APCER, BPCER and the associated non-response rates, whereas a full system test reports the IAPMR (named IAPAR in the 2023 edition, Section 3.1.1) together with the FNMR and the false match rate [20]; a data capture test forms the third type [20]. The levels are iBeta's own definition: "although similar to the FIDO Alliance Levels A & B, there are significant differences" [20].

The attacks are produced in the laboratory. iBeta's testers select six PAI species "as uniformly as possible" and create the artefacts from biometric characteristics provided by cooperative volunteer subjects [20]. At Level 1, with six subjects or six species, the testing requires 48 hours [20]. Table 1.3 in Section 1.1.3 lists the time, expertise, equipment, cost and attack-success limits of each level. The rule for bona fide errors has tightened over time: "These error rates were initially unconstrained but were later capped at 20%, and then reduced to 15% for Level 1 and Level 2", while Level 3 requires a BPCER or FNMR of no more than 10% (Table 1.3) [20]. A passing system therefore stops attacks while keeping the rejection of genuine users within these caps.

Level 3 is the most recent addition. iBeta introduced Level 3 testing based on ISO/IEC 30107-3 in June 2025, initially for face, with custom hyper-realistic masks and more time per attempt, to address attacks "carried out with significant background knowledge and funding" [86]. The Level 3 tester has at least 16 hours of research on presentation attacks and "has participated in at least 10 other PAD tests with the target modality" [20]. The first Level 3 letters followed in 2026, after a year without Level 3 results [87]: the first posted Level 3 letter is dated 23 January 2026, and the latest 17 September 2026 [20].

The laboratory's scope now extends beyond presentation attacks. iBeta offers PAD testing to ISO/IEC 30107-4 for mobile devices and a separate injection attack detection service [82]. According to a trade report, this service launched on 4 June 2026 against CEN/TS 18099, with testing up to Level 3: Level 2 covers virtual webcam and emulator injections, Level 3 covers camera service and kernel manipulation, and a test based on the international standard is planned once ISO/IEC 25456 is published [88]. As a result, the same laboratory now tests both routes of attack that Table 1.4 distinguishes.
