# 5. Conclusion and Research Directions

PAD has reached an unusual state for a security technology. Its definitions, metrics and test methods are standardised, certified by accredited laboratories and written into national law [73], [20], [98], yet the property that matters most in deployment, reliable detection under conditions a detector has not seen before, remains unsolved [35], [105]. Detectors reach very low error rates on the attacks and data they were built for, and they lose much of that accuracy when the dataset, the capture device or the attack type changes [31], [35].

At the same time, the attacks that scale best no longer pass through the camera. Injection attacks deliver manipulated video directly into the data stream, so a presentation attack detector alone no longer secures remote identity verification [10], [21], [67]. Assurance, finally, comes less from the accuracy a method reports than from independent testing: certification levels, government benchmarks and open models that others can evaluate [20], [31], [105].

The main conclusions and their evidence, the problems they leave open and their consequences for practice follow in Sections 5.1 to 5.3.
