## 2.2. Era 2: Deep Classifiers with Auxiliary Supervision

Section 2.1.3 closed with the cross-dataset failure of hand-crafted features. The early deep models that replaced them were trained as binary classifiers, and "Many of them struggle to grasp adequate spoofing cues and generalize poorly" [45]. The second era, from about 2018 to 2020, answered this with auxiliary supervision, which guides learning "toward discriminative and generalizable cues" [45]. Section 1.3.3 introduced the supervision signals; this section follows the papers, groups and benchmarks that made them the standard.

Three research groups led the era. Liu, Jourabloo and Liu of Michigan State University introduced depth and rPPG supervision at CVPR 2018 [45] and then the SiW-M benchmark of unseen attacks in 2019 [56]. Yu, Zhao and colleagues of the University of Oulu, with Mininglamp Academy of Sciences, Aibee and Northwestern Polytechnical University, introduced central difference convolution (CDC) and the Central Difference Convolutional Network (CDCN) in 2020 [57]. The National Laboratory of Pattern Recognition of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, with partners, built the large multi-modal datasets CASIA-SURF [58] and CASIA-SURF CeFA [59] and organised the ChaLearn challenges with them [60]. The two landmark papers carry 593 and 592 citations in Crossref as of 2 October 2026 [45], [57].

The data grew in step with the models. The Era-1 datasets of Table 2.3 held 15 to 50 subjects, whereas SiW holds 165 subjects [45], CASIA-SURF 1,000 subjects and 21,000 videos [58], and CeFA 1,607 subjects [59]. SiW-M widened the attack range from the one or two types of earlier work to 13 types [56]. Participation grew as well: more than 300 institutions joined the 2019 challenge [61], and 340 teams joined the development stage in 2020 [60].

The era also exposed its own limit, the cross-dataset gap described in Section 1.3.4 [35]. On the unseen attacks of SiW-M, the 2018 auxiliary model averaged 23.6% ACER [56] and the later CDCN++ still averaged 12.7% [57]. A 2021 review of the competitions concluded that "generalized face PAD is still far away from a solved research problem" [61], which set the agenda for Era 3.

Building on this overview, Section 2.2.1 presents auxiliary depth and rPPG supervision, Section 2.2.2 presents CDC, and Section 2.2.3 presents the benchmarks and challenges of the era.
