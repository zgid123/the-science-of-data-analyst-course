## 3.2. Independent Commercial Testing: iBeta

The standards of Section 3.1 became a market requirement through independent test laboratories, and iBeta Quality Assurance is the laboratory most closely identified with PAD testing. It is a software quality assurance company in Aurora, Colorado, whose testing services include biometrics alongside web, mobile, load and accessibility testing [81]. Its biometric laboratory is accredited by NIST under the National Voluntary Laboratory Accreditation Program (NVLAP) with lab code 200962-0 and audited against the laboratory standard ISO/IEC 17025:2017 [82]. After a NIST audit in September 2023, its accredited scope covered ISO/IEC 30107-3:2023 together with PAD testing to Parts 1, 3 and 4 and performance testing to ISO/IEC 19795, effective through 30 September 2024 [83]. The audits recur, and an audit in November 2021 had already added ISO/IEC 19795 [84]. The laboratory also holds biometric accreditations from the FIDO Alliance, Android and Mastercard [82].

What iBeta issues is a confirmation letter rather than a certificate. ISO/IEC 30107-3 is included in its NVLAP scope, and "the specific test procedures, processes, and report templates, as developed by iBeta, are audited and approved as part of the NIST administration of the NVLAP" [20]. A letter states that the tested solution conforms with the testing and reporting requirements of the standard, and iBeta stresses that this "does not translate to a certification of the vendor product" [20]. The laboratory first used the word certification, and the NIST audit of March 2019 corrected it [20]. Each letter names the product version and "the exact device configuration" that was tested [20], and iBeta posts letters only with the vendor's written consent and without a fee [20]. In market reports the letters are still often called certifications [85].

The first posted letter is dated 20 August 2018 and confirms a Level 1 result for FaceTec [20]. From that date, the letters turned the error rates of Section 1.1.2 and the levels of Section 1.1.3 into a document that vendors can show to customers. Section 3.2.1 describes the test protocol behind a letter, and Section 3.2.2 follows the growth in the number of letters.
