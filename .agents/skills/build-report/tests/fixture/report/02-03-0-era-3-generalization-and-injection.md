## 2.3. Era 3: Generalisation and the Shift to Digital Injection

Era 2 ended with strong results inside known datasets and an open problem outside them. The third era, from about 2020 to 2026, pursued two strands in parallel: one changed how detectors are trained, and the other changed what they must defend against.

The first strand made generalisation the explicit training goal. Domain generalisation methods, beginning with MADDG in 2019 and SSDG in 2020, learn features that hold across several source datasets [44], [42]. Large pretrained models followed: FLIP, presented in 2023, initialises a ViT with vision-language weights and reports the best average result on the O/C/I/M protocol [66]. In one comparison table, the average HTER on the four unseen-dataset tasks of the O/C/I/M protocol fell from 11.28% for SSDG-R in 2020 to 3.01% for FLIP in 2023 [66]. Generalisation nevertheless remains the motivating problem, since "Existing FAS methods have poor generalizability to unseen spoof types, camera sensors, and environmental conditions" [66].

The second strand moved the threat model away from the sensor. In January 2022, the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) recorded that "the number of attacks with masks and video injection has been increasing", and noted that interactive deepfake puppets "can now be created in real-time" [67]. It named the deepfake attack delivered by video injection or high-fidelity screens "the last frontier" [67]. In 2024, the AWS paper of Section 1.1.4 stated that real-time generative models had "heightened the risk of digitally generated attacks" and that existing models "struggle with digital injection attacks" [10]. The industry analyst Gartner forecast in February 2024 that, by 2026, deepfake attacks on face biometrics will lead 30% of enterprises to stop treating face-based identity verification as reliable in isolation [68].

Building on these two strands, Section 2.3.1 follows the domain generalisation methods and Section 2.3.2 follows the shift to injection and deepfake attacks and the response it triggered.
