## 3.5. Synthesis of the Policy Track

The four sections of this part describe four different kinds of institution, and Table 3.8 compares them by role, output and the part of ISO/IEC 30107 they rely on.

**Table 3.8. Four institutions on the policy track of PAD**

| Institution | Kind | Role | Main output | Relation to ISO/IEC 30107 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 37 | International standards body | Defines vocabulary, framework and test methodology [72], [73] | ISO/IEC 30107 Parts 1–4, 2016–2024 [73] | Author of the series [73] |
| iBeta Quality Assurance | NVLAP-accredited test laboratory | Tests products on vendor request [82], [20] | 273 PAD confirmation letters, 2018–2026 [20] | Tests to ISO/IEC 30107-3 under its accredited scope [20], [83] |
| NIST | National metrology and evaluation agency | Benchmarks many algorithms on sequestered data [31] | IR 8491: 82 algorithms from 45 developers (2023) [31] | Uses the Part 1 definition and Part 3 metrics [9], [31] |
| SBV | Central bank and regulator | Makes biometric authentication with PAD mandatory for online banking [92], [95] | Decision 2345 (2023), Circulars 17, 18, 50 (2024) and 77 (2025) [92], [96], [97], [95], [98] | Requires PAD based on ISO 30107 and certification at ISO 30107 Level 2 or equivalent [95], [98] |

Read in sequence, the four institutions turned one vocabulary into a binding obligation. The standard first defined what a presentation attack is and how to measure its detection [73]. The laboratory then turned that methodology into confirmation letters that vendors can show to customers, with the yearly count rising from 2 in 2018 to 56 in 2025 [20]. The government benchmark measured the field as a whole and found wide variation between algorithms and attack types [31]. Finally, the central bank made biometric authentication mandatory for transfers above a threshold [92] and, from 2025, required PAD against photos, videos and 3D masks with third-party certification [95], [98].

The policy track also kept pace with the technical track of Part 2. When the injection threat of Section 2.3.2 grew, the standards answered with CEN/TS 18099 [22], the laboratory with injection attack testing [88], and the FIDO programme with requirements for injection protection [79]. The benchmark, in contrast, places injection attacks outside its scope [31]. As a result, the requirements that a production method must meet in 2026 include certified PAD at a defined level [98], testing across demographic groups [78], and protection against injection [79], which is the context for the method examined in Part 4.
