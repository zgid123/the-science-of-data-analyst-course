### 3.3.1. Main Findings

The central finding of IR 8491 is variation. "PAD accuracy varies widely across algorithms, use cases (impersonation vs. evasion), and PA types", and only a small percentage of the algorithms evaluated demonstrated notable detection capabilities [31]. No algorithm covered every attack: in the words of the lead author, "Some developers' algorithms could catch two or three types, but none caught them all" [91].

Detection is mature for some attack types and open for others. For print and replay attacks, protective face masks and flexible silicone masks, several developers reached an APCER of at most 0.01 at a BPCER of 0.01, and a BPCER of at most 0.01 at an APCER of 0.01 [31]. Other attack types were detected well by only one developer, as for attack type 5, and some remained high-error for every algorithm [31]. On the undisclosed attack type 4, even the best single algorithm missed 13% of attacks in the impersonation task and 62% in the evasion task at a BPCER of 0.01 [31]. Zooming in on a print or replay attack, so that the frame of the medium and the attacker's hands are hidden, raised the errors of all algorithms, in some cases sharply [31]. This result matches the observation of Section 2.2.1 that a detector can rely on cues such as a screen bezel rather than on the face itself.

Three further observations point to practical improvements. Many algorithms made fewer errors on video than on a still image from the same session, although "The existence and extent of PAD error reduction in videos versus stills is highly algorithm-dependent" [31]. A simple sum-rule fusion of four accurate algorithms beat the best single algorithm in all impersonation attack categories and in most evasion categories [31]. In addition, algorithms detecting impersonation generally performed better than those detecting evasion on the same attacks, and a first-order check found no consistent elevation of BPCER for any demographic group [31].

NIST also states the boundary of its own evidence. The study "tested PAD capabilities under narrow conditions" and did not evaluate injection attacks, hardware-based PAD or active challenge-response PAD, and an offline test includes no live image acquisition or interaction with real users [31]. As of 2 October 2026, the FATE PAD track is closed to submissions and IR 8491 remains its latest posted result; NIST will announce a reopening on its page and mailing list [90]. The benchmark therefore documents the state of passive, software-based PAD in early 2023, and Section 3.4 follows how one country made such a PAD gate a legal requirement.
