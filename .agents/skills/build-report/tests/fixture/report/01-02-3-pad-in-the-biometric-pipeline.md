### 1.2.3. PAD in the Biometric Pipeline

Both axes describe a component that sits inside a larger system. A generic biometric system captures a sample, after which a signal processing unit performs pre-processing, quality checking, feature extraction and template creation, and a comparator scores the probe against the stored reference and compares the score with a preset threshold to reach the decision [25]. ISO/IEC 30107-1, whose current edition dates from August 2023, considers attacks "at the capture device during the presentation and collection of the biometric characteristics" [33]. It standardises the terms of PAD while leaving countermeasures, algorithms and sensors to implementers [33]. The capture device deserves this attention because it is the most exposed point of a biometric system: an attacker needs no knowledge of the system's inner functioning to launch an attack there [6].

PAD joins this pipeline in one of two ways, serially or in parallel with the comparator [25]. In the serial design, the PAD subsystem makes its decision first, and only samples judged to come from a living person reach face recognition; in the parallel design, the data enter the face recognition system and the PAD subsystem at the same time, and their outputs are fused [26]. Figure 1.1 draws the serial gate, which makes the role of PAD easiest to follow, and the parallel design differs from it only in where the two decisions are combined.

Injection attacks call for a check at a different point. Because an injection attack bypasses the sensor and inserts digital content directly into the data stream [10], its detection belongs on the link between the capture device and the processing stages. CEN/TS 18099 addresses this link, as Section 1.1.4 describes [23]. Deployed systems implement controls at both points. Face ID randomises the sequence of its 2D and depth captures to counter digital and physical spoofs [28], and Windows Hello offers a setting that disallows external Hello cameras entirely [29], a capture-side control against untrusted camera paths. The Windows Hello recognition engine then runs four distinct steps after capture [29], in line with the generic pipeline above.

As a result, a complete design places PAD at the capture device, as a gate before comparison or as a parallel subsystem whose output is fused, and places injection attack detection on the path from the camera to processing. Section 1.3 turns from this system view to the techniques that implement the PAD component itself.
