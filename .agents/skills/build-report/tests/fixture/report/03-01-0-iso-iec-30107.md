## 3.1. International Standardisation: ISO/IEC 30107

The policy track begins with the committee that wrote the shared vocabulary of Section 1.1. ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 37 Biometrics was established in August 2002, after the approval of a proposal submitted by the American National Standards Institute [72]. It was created to "harmonize and accelerate formal international biometric standardization", and its scope covers generic biometric technologies that support interoperability and data interchange among applications and systems [72]. As of August 2024, SC 37 works through six working groups; its Working Group 3 is active in developing PAD standards, and its Working Group 5 standardises testing and reporting methodologies and metrics [72]. Biometric security testing and evaluation methodology belong to the sister committee SC 27 [72], which is why the Common Criteria evaluation of PAD in Section 1.1.3 is a separate standard.

The PAD series answered a concrete barrier to adoption. The first edition of ISO/IEC 30107-1 states that "the potential for attacks on a system through the biometric sensor has limited the use of biometrics in applications which are unsupervised by an agent of the system owner, such as remote authentication over untrusted networks" [73]. Published by SC 37 on 14 January 2016, Part 1 provides "a foundation for PAD through defining terms and establishing a framework through which presentation attack events can be specified and detected" [73]. Part 3 then supplied the testing methodology, and its second edition of 10 January 2023 is the current SC 37 document for evaluating PAD [3], [74].

Building on this origin, Section 3.1.1 lists the parts and editions of the series, and Section 3.1.2 follows the standards and certification schemes that build on it.
