### 3.1.1. Parts and Editions

The ISO/IEC 30107 series is a multi-part document in four parts: Framework, Data formats, Testing and reporting, and Profile for testing of mobile devices [75]. Three of the four parts have been revised since their first publication, and Table 3.1 lists every edition as of 2 October 2026.

**Table 3.1. Parts and editions of ISO/IEC 30107 (as of 2 October 2026)**

| Part | Title | Edition and publication date | Status |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Framework | Edition 1: 14 January 2016 | Withdrawn 10 August 2023 [73] |
| 1 | Framework | Edition 2: 10 August 2023 | Current [73] |
| 2 | Data formats | Edition 1: 15 December 2017 | Current [73] |
| 3 | Testing and reporting | Edition 1: 23 August 2017 | Withdrawn 10 January 2023 [73] |
| 3 | Testing and reporting | Edition 2: 10 January 2023 | Current [73], [3] |
| 4 | Profile for testing of mobile devices | Edition 1: 12 June 2020 | Withdrawn 7 February 2024 [73] |
| 4 | Profile for testing of mobile devices | Edition 2: 7 February 2024 | Current [73] |

Each part has a distinct role. Part 1 defines the terms and the framework [73]. Part 2 "defines data formats for conveying the mechanism used in biometric presentation attack detection and for conveying the results of presentation attack detection methods" [73]. Part 3 sets out the testing and reporting methodology, and it is "directed at vendors or test laboratories seeking to conduct evaluations of PAD" [74]. Part 4 is a profile that specifies requirements for testing PAD "on mobile devices with local biometric recognition and on biometric modules integrated into mobile devices" [73].

Part 3 carries the metrics, and their names changed between editions. The 2017 edition defined APCER, BPCER and IAPMR, the proportion of impostor attack presentations that succeed [2]. The 2023 edition technically revises the 2017 text: it uses the name IAPAR for the full-system metric [74], adds the relative IAPAR (RIAPAR), which combines IAPAR with the FRR at a fixed threshold [74], adds an Annex C on the roles in PAD testing, and makes general technical clarifications [74]. This is why Section 1.1.2 lists IAPAR as the newer name of IAPMR.

Part 3 also connects PAD testing to the wider standards system. Its normative references include the Common Criteria standard ISO/IEC 15408-1 and the biometric performance testing standard ISO/IEC 19795-1 [74]. Because the texts are sold, developers and test laboratories purchase their own copies [5], and the certification schemes of Section 3.1.2 make the requirements of Part 3 concrete for the products they test.
