### 3.2.2. Growth of Confirmation Letters

The number of posted letters shows PAD testing moving from a research metric to a routine step in product release. iBeta's own posted table holds 273 PAD confirmation letters from 20 August 2018 to 17 September 2026, issued to about 150 distinct vendor names [20]. By the end of June 2024 it held 147 letters from about 89 vendors, 19 of them dated in the first half of 2024 [20]. Because letters are posted only with vendor consent [20], all counts are lower bounds, and one vendor can hold several letters for different products or levels. Table 3.3 gives the count per year.

**Table 3.3. iBeta PAD confirmation letters per year (posted table, as of 2 October 2026)**

| Year | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 (to 17 September) | Total |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Letters | 2 | 10 | 20 | 29 | 35 | 32 | 47 | 56 | 42 | 273 |

Source: count of the posted table [20].

A widely repeated figure measures something different. The dataset vendor Axon Labs reported in October 2024 that "As of the first half of 2024, iBeta has certified 85 companies over the past six years." and that 18 certifications were issued between January and June 2024 [85]; Wikipedia repeats this as 85 certificates [89]. The figure counts companies rather than letters, which is why it is lower than the 147 letters of the posted table for the same period [20].

Growth accelerated in 2025. An analysis of the published letters found that "During 2025, iBeta published 55 confirmation letters covering 40 companies, representing roughly 20 percent growth compared to the prior year" [87]. The letters split nearly evenly between Level 1 and Level 2, and identity verification and know-your-customer providers make up just over half of the companies [87]. The posted table as of October 2026 shows 56 letters dated in 2025 [20]. The first Level 3 letters followed in 2026 [20].

Read together, the counts show three shifts: more letters each year, a growing share of the harder levels, and a market led by remote identity verification, the setting in which Section 2.3.2 located the injection threat. Section 3.3 turns from this vendor-paid testing to the government benchmark that compares many algorithms at once.
