## 2.1. Era 1: Hand-Crafted Texture and Motion Cues

The first era of face PAD built its detectors from features designed by hand. Early works used hand-crafted descriptors such as LBP and adopted traditional classifiers such as support vector machines and linear discriminant analysis [45]. Chingovska et al. grouped the methods of this period into three categories: motion-based, texture-based and image-quality-based methods [30]. Section 1.3 described how these cues work; this section traces when they appeared, who developed them and why they gave way to deep learning.

Shared evaluation turned the early methods into a field. The first public competition on countermeasures to face spoofing took place at the 2011 International Joint Conference on Biometrics, organised by Chakka, Anjos and Marcel of the Idiap Research Institute [46]. It answered a clear gap, since "There seems to exist no comparative studies of different techniques using the same protocols and data" [46]. Six teams from universities around the world took part [46], using Idiap's Print-Attack database, which Anjos and Marcel published at the same conference together with a baseline countermeasure [47]. The entries combined motion, texture analysis and liveness detection, and the organisers concluded that the results "suggest the investigation of more complex attacks" [46]. The second competition followed at the International Conference on Biometrics in June 2013, again led by Idiap, with authors from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Fraunhofer IGD, the University of Oulu, the University of Campinas and other institutions [48].

The era is best dated by the paradigm that dominated it rather than by a hard boundary. Hand-crafted methods led the field until about 2017, and their landmark colour-texture journal paper appeared as late as 2016 [37]. Deep learning, however, entered earlier: Yang, Lei and Li of the Chinese Academy of Sciences posted the first end-to-end deep method on 24 August 2014 [49], an 8-layer shallow convolutional network [35], and reported an "over 70% relative decrease of Half Total Error Rate (HTER)" on two datasets [49]. The two paradigms therefore overlapped between 2014 and 2017, and Era 2 began when the deep approach became the standard one.

Building on this outline, Section 2.1.1 follows the texture descriptors, Section 2.1.2 the blink- and motion-based liveness cues, and Section 2.1.3 the datasets of the era and the cross-dataset failures that ended it.
