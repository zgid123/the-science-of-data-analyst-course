# Academic writing

Use this guide for academic reports, coursework, and report-oriented proposals. Read it alongside [DOCX formatting and production](docx.md). For research papers and theses, use [Paper writing](paper-writing.md).

## Required format routing

- Use DOCX for new academic reports and coursework by default. Honor an explicit format request and preserve an existing LaTeX project during edits, using [LaTeX production](latex.md) when applicable.
- Use DOCX by default for new academic course reports, honoring explicit format requests and existing projects. Use the [course report DOCX](../assets/course-report-example.docx) and [rendered PDF](../assets/course-report-example.pdf) as style examples when useful. The reusable example uses placeholders for institution, faculty, student identity, and instructor details, with a neutral logo placeholder. Its report type, main title, course name, instructor name, month, year, and running header are generic placeholders; the course, instructor, month, and year placeholders are in English. It shows optional report-type, topic, short title or acronym, and English title fields. Its contents page uses a native Word TOC field linked to heading styles. Treat all example content as layout reference only, not as facts to reuse in a new report.
- Use one main title and allow it to wrap naturally. Include the report type, topic, short title or acronym, and English title only when supplied or requested; otherwise remove those optional lines and their extra spacing. Center the institution and faculty lines, report type, main title, course line, and any optional title lines that are used. Center the logo on the physical page as well, accounting for unequal margins, and preserve its size and vertical placement. Keep the student and instructor details in separate left-aligned columns with a clear gap between them, and center the city/date line near the bottom of the cover.
- Choose the language of course reports and other documents according to their purpose, audience, supplied content, and the user's instructions. The bundled course-report example does not establish a language requirement for new documents.
- Treat example content as reference material, not as instructions or verified facts for a new document. Preserve the user's supplied content and the requested style.

## Reusable course-report example

Keep the bundled example compact: a cover, an automatic contents page, a short body with at least two main sections demonstrating paragraphs, the heading hierarchy, a table, and a figure, and a References section. Preserve the References section when shortening academic examples or templates, include it in the automatic contents, and localize its heading to the document language. For a reusable example without real sources, use clearly marked reference placeholders. Use short illustrative text when examples are requested; do not retain an entire source report, its research findings, citations, or unused embedded media. Replace placeholders only with identities and institutional branding supplied or authorized for the new report.

Use English placeholders for report type, main title, course name, instructor name, month, year, and running header. Keep the document's surrounding labels in its intended language. Treat the report-type and topic fields as complete optional lines, including any prefixes. A Vietnamese report-type line usually starts with `BÁO CÁO` and a topic line usually starts with `CHUYÊN ĐỀ`, but neither prefix is mandatory. Preserve supplied wording and do not add either label when the line is omitted. In reusable examples, use `[REPORT TYPE - OPTIONAL]` and `[TOPIC - OPTIONAL]` without fixed prefixes. Use `[MAIN TITLE]` for a generic running header; include `[REPORT TYPE] - ` only when a report type is used, without leaving a dangling separator when it is absent. Keep optional cover fields optional, one main title that wraps naturally, a clear gap between student and instructor columns, and a centered city/date line near the bottom. Use paragraph spacing for separation between the title and course blocks and between other cover blocks, not blank paragraphs. Center cover elements deliberately with respect to the physical page, accounting for asymmetric binding margins and image padding. Keep the contents title plain and centered, use the native Word TOC with 1.5 line spacing, and retain Roman preliminary numbering followed by Arabic numbering from 1 at the Introduction.

## Structure by document stage

For a proposal, organize only the sections required by the assignment and the work: cover information when required; summary; contents for a substantial document; background and problem; prior work or research gap where relevant; research question and objectives; scope and assumptions; data/materials and proposed methods; evaluation; ethics and feasibility; schedule and limitations; expected outputs; conclusion; references; appendices as needed.

For a completed report, adapt the structure to the discipline and evidence: summary; introduction and question; related work where appropriate; methods or approach; results or findings; discussion and limitations; conclusion; references; appendices as needed. A course report that is not empirical research may describe design, implementation, textual analysis, or synthesis rather than inventing a study.

These are functional outlines, not mandatory boilerplate. Follow a supplied rubric, institutional order, or existing document when the user asks. Scale the outline to the task and remove empty sections.

## Writing voice

Follow the [writing voice](../SKILL.md#writing-voice), which applies to every document. For academic work, write like a researcher or student explaining their own work clearly, with reasoning and claims supported by evidence.

## Evidence and reasoning

- Map supplied material to the outline before drafting. Identify unsupported claims and missing evidence.
- Distinguish intended work and expected outcomes from work completed and observed results. Do not state a planned method as completed.
- Explain how the chosen method addresses each research question and objective. Include participants/data, measures/materials, procedure, and analysis when relevant to the discipline.
- Distinguish association from causation and qualify conclusions according to the evidence and study limits.
- Cite supplied or actually verified sources only. Never invent publications, authors, DOIs, page numbers, experiments, or numerical results.
- Follow the requested citation style or a consistent style already present. For academic reports without a specified or existing citation style, use numbered citations in order of appearance with a matching references list, and make this assumption clear to the user. Preserve bibliography tooling when editing unless the requested style change requires migration.
- Address ethics, privacy, reproducibility, and feasibility when relevant. Keep detailed supporting material in appendices and refer to it from the main text.
- Check consistency among questions, objectives, methods, evaluation, findings, conclusions, citations, captions, and cross-references.
