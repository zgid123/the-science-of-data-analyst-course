# Paper writing

Use this guide for research papers and theses, including drafting, revision, structure, and formatting. Apply the shared [writing voice](../SKILL.md#writing-voice) and read the production guide for the requested format for source setup and visual checks. For academic reports and coursework, use [Academic writing](academic-writing.md).

## Format and style

Research papers and theses use the same style selection. Choose appearance independently of file format:

1. Follow explicit user requirements and the controlling university or publication template. Establish the venue, year, and submission stage for actual submissions. Report a conflict and follow the user's latest direction.
2. Preserve an existing project's style and bibliography workflow unless a change is requested.
3. For a new paper or thesis, use [ICIS](icis-style.md) by default; use [ACM](acm-style.md) or [IEEE](ieee-style.md) when requested or required by the target template. Load only the selected guide.

Default to English and LaTeX for both papers and theses, delivering editable source, required assets/bibliography, and compiled PDF. Honor explicit language and format requests. For DOCX, retain the selected style and use [DOCX production](docx.md); for LaTeX, use [LaTeX production](latex.md). Choosing DOCX does not switch a paper or thesis to the default DOCX report style.

Use the selected profile's shared typography and citation conventions. Differences for theses concern chapter-capable implementation and required front matter, numbering, and single-column structure, as documented by that profile; they do not introduce another style choice. These are Alpha's structural adaptations, not official conference thesis standards. University requirements take priority.

## Structure and argument

- Establish the research question, audience, contribution, discipline, and document stage from supplied material before drafting. Map existing content to an outline and identify missing evidence.
- For a research proposal, organize the problem, background and research gap, question and objectives, scope, proposed methodology, evaluation, ethics and feasibility, expected contribution, schedule when needed, and references. Describe planned work as planned.
- For a completed paper, organize the introduction and contribution, relevant prior work, methodology or approach, results, discussion and limitations, conclusion, and references. Adapt the structure to the discipline and actual evidence; do not invent an empirical study for conceptual or design work.
- For a thesis, retain required cover information, author details, abstract, contents, chapters, references, and appendices. Follow the institution's chapter order and front matter requirements. Apply the selected shared style to chapter headings, chapter counters, and preliminary-to-main pagination.
- Scale these outlines to the task and supplied template. Do not add empty boilerplate, draft body content during format-only work, or impose thesis front matter on a paper. Keep a References section in skeletons, using clearly marked placeholders when no sources are supplied.

## Evidence and citations

For paper discovery, critical reading, literature synthesis, research design, or substantive manuscript development, optionally use Research if it is available in the installed skill catalog. Its absence does not block this workflow: use supplied material and available search/reading tools with the safeguards below. Keep formatting-only work in Documents and preserve existing content and references.

For research work, establish the question and eligibility criteria, log searches actually performed, screen and deduplicate sources, then connect claims to the evidence inspected. Compare findings and limitations rather than listing papers as votes. Scale effort to the requested document; do not claim an exhaustive search or universal novelty from a shortlist.

- Explain how the methodology addresses each research question and objective. Describe data or participants, materials, measures, procedure, and analysis where relevant.
- Distinguish intended work and expected outcomes from completed work and observed results. Qualify conclusions according to the evidence and limitations, and distinguish association from causation.
- Cite supplied or actually verified sources only. Never invent publications, authors, DOIs, page numbers, experiments, numerical results, identities, or affiliations. Mark missing information clearly.
- Label publication status and what was inspected separately: full text, selected sections, abstract, or metadata. Match claims to that access; missing abstract details are unknown, not established flaws. Check accessible corrections or retraction notices when verifying sources, apply corrections, and exclude retracted work from supporting evidence; disclose unavailable checks.
- Exclude IEEE Access from new research support and citations unless the user explicitly overrides that preference. Match the publication venue with normalized capitalization and whitespace; known repository/preprint versions of the same publication stay excluded. Other IEEE venues and IEEE citation style remain eligible. Do not infer venue from a domain, title, DOI prefix, or another paper's references. Keep unresolved venue decisions pending, and disclose exclusions that limit the evidence.
- During substantive revision, flag claims relying on excluded sources and replace their support only with verified eligible evidence; otherwise qualify or remove the unsupported claim. During formatting-only work, preserve existing wording, citations, and bibliography.
- Follow the selected citation profile: APA 7 author-year with an alphabetical bibliography for ICIS, IEEE numbered citations in citation order for IEEE, and the ACM class or venue citation mode and reference format for ACM. Preserve existing bibliography tooling unless the requested style requires migration.
- Address ethics, privacy, reproducibility, and feasibility when relevant. Keep detailed supporting material in appendices and link it from the main text.
- Check consistency among questions, objectives, methodology, evaluation, findings, conclusions, citations, captions, and cross-references.

## Writing and delivery checks

Follow the [writing voice](../SKILL.md#writing-voice) for new prose and preserve supplied content during format-only work.

Use [LaTeX production](latex.md) or [DOCX production](docx.md) for build, field-refresh, and visual verification according to the output format. Check every rendered page against the selected paper or thesis profile. For an actual submission, verify the current venue and submission-stage requirements as directed by the selected style guide. Successful compilation alone does not establish submission compliance. If compilation or rendering is unavailable, provide the editable source and state exactly which PDF and visual checks remain incomplete.
