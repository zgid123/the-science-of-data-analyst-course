Does behavior add to early scores?

All fitted comparisons include module context and use the same test population. Scores assume a 14-day release delay; the score baseline includes score availability count. Why more features do not always help

Negative differences mean behavior-only ranked risk better. Day-60 validation favored combined (AP 0.731 versus 0.680), but later-cohort test reversed that result. The module comparison and feature shifts below show heterogeneous transport; BBB early scores changed from mean 72.0 to 59.0 and its due opportunities fell from three to two. BBB also lost CMAs and changed its first TMA weight from 5% to 0%. These changes are plausible explanations, not causal proof. The 28-day release assumption barely changes combined day-60 AP. What would educators need to review?

False positives later Pass/Distinction; false negatives later Fail/Withdrawn. Validation quantiles select thresholds; test outcomes do not. Separate outcome recalls use each final-outcome group's count, with flagged successful students representing false positive rates.

How much support time remains?

The 14-day support window is proposed, not stakeholder approved. Withdrawal intervals use observed future unregistration days; course-end intervals for Fail are proxies and cannot establish time before failure or score publication.

Which assumptions change the result?

28-day mark release challenges the primary 14-day assumption. Seven-day logging delay hides recent submissions and scores. Failure-only omits future withdrawals and refits Fail versus Pass/Distinction; it evaluates a selected retrospective non-withdrawal population. Including previous 2013J students broadens the test population with unchanged models and threshold. Sensitivities did not select the primary model.

Who is missed or falsely flagged?

Fixed 20% validation-capacity reference, combined model. Disability and age are audit fields, not predictors. Each group's eligible test enrollment count is the denominator; small groups are unstable. This is an exploratory error audit, not fairness certification.

Do shown probabilities match observed risk?

At day 60, the highest risk bin averaged 95.6% predicted risk but only 59.1% observed risk (n=937). Probabilities are substantially overconfident. This fixed finding is a day-60 reference; the table below follows the cutoff selector.
