# Speech script, full version – Early Identification of Disengaging Students in Online Courses

Team N04 · MTH045 – The Science of Data Analytics · Milestone 5 final presentation · full deck, 94 slides, no
slide limit. The main / appendix split and the final slide count follow from this version (appendix-split.md).

**Big idea (core message).** Staff notice a drifting student only after the early weeks, when help is easiest, and the
obvious sign, a missed task, comes too late for most non-completers; on two years of open course data, an early-sign
rule that also counts inactivity gives staff a factual reason beside each name now, while a better ranking and the
effect of contact still need one designed test and a randomised pilot.

**SCQA.** Situation: a Vietnamese university expanding online and blended learning holds LMS, enrolment and result
records, and withdrawal is a regulated quality measure. Complication: staff notice late, can contact only a few
students, no open Vietnamese course data exist and no dataset records a contact. Question: can records dated by week 4
tell staff, in time and within capacity, whom to contact and why, and how much can the evidence honestly claim?
Answer: in part, each claim at its own level (Slide 4).

**Arc.** Hook and answer first (1–5) → introduction, business understanding and the questions (6–21) → the data:
requirements, selection, description, fit and quality (22–38) → preparation: the add-drop rule, the early-sign rule,
the pipeline and leakage control (39–46) → method and evaluation design (47–58) → status and evidence: what we ran,

## Slide 2 – The Surprising Number   [Hook]   [Line: main]

## Slide 4 – Our Answer in Brief   [Abstract]   [Line: main]

**Visual:** none; a key-terms box, three number cards and a "still open" strip (structure: the answer before the evidence).
**Speaker notes:** Here is the whole talk on one slide. Four terms first. Non-completion is a fail or a withdrawal. The cut-offs are day 13 for the week-3 list and day 27 for the week-5 list, and the add-drop period ends at day 14 on our data. So staff get a ranked list with a factual reason beside each name. Still open: the risks hold as ranks, not as probabilities, and whether contact helps. Here is how the talk builds it.

## Slide 7 – Withdrawal Is a Regulated Measure   [1 Introduction]   [Line: detail]
**Lead:** Vietnamese regulation caps withdrawal, so a university needs an exact record of each student's status and an early view of who is drifting away.
**On the slide:** Three number tiles: 10% cap on the annual withdrawal rate (Circular 01/2024); 15% cap on the first-year rate, above which the enrolment quota may not rise; 30% of a full-time or work-study program may be taught in online classes (2021 training regulation). Card: Circular 83/2026, in force from 15 November 2026, replaces Circular 01/2024, keeps both caps, and no longer counts approved leave or transfers as withdrawal. Card: what follows for our project, completion means passing and withdrawal follows Circular 83/2026, so the baseline is recounted under its rule; the stand-in data record neither leave nor transfer. Basis line: the regulations are cited through news reports, secondary sources.
**Visual:** three number tiles, the two caps in the accent colour (comparison of thresholds), then two cards.
**Speaker notes:** A university with records but no questions might still wait; regulation is why it cannot. Circular 01/2024 caps the annual withdrawal rate at 10 percent and the first-year rate at 15 percent, and a university above the first-year cap may not raise its enrolment quota. Circular 83/2026, in force from 15 November 2026, keeps both caps and no longer counts approved leave or transfers as withdrawal. At the same time, a program may now teach up to 30 percent of its volume online. Together these rules make withdrawal a measured quality indicator and require an exact record of each student's status. That is why our completion measure follows Circular 83/2026, and why we note that our stand-in data cannot separate leave or transfer from withdrawal. Next, what published research says about timing.

**Visual:** three numbered lines in large type, the first in orange, and the navy take-home box (none beyond type).
**Speaker notes:** So here is what we hope you take away, whoever runs the list. First, watch for silence, not only missed tasks: by week 4, fewer than one in five of the students who will fail or withdraw have missed a task, so an early list has to look at whether students are still active; a week without activity is the earlier sign. Second, size the list to the people who will call: about six names per twenty students is where a contact still finds a student at risk more often than chance, and every name should come with the reason it is there, so that staff can check it before they act. Third, read the score as a rank and prove contact before scaling: the model orders students well, but its risks are not reliable probabilities in every course, and only a randomised pilot can show whether, and for whom, early contact raises completion.
