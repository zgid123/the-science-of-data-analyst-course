## Slide 52 – Judging a List by Capacity   [9 Evaluation]   [Line: main]
**Lead:** A list is judged by how many later non-completers it reaches against the most a list of its length could reach.
**On the slide:** Worked example (illustration, not data): 20 students, 9 later non-completers, a list of 5 holding 4 of them: recall 4 of 9, normalised recall 4 of 5, lift (4 of 5) ÷ (9 of 20) = 1.8. Definitions: capacity k, 3 or 5 contacts per 20 students, main operating point 5 per 20 at day 27; recall at capacity; normalised recall, with the ceiling of 46% to 100% of a run's non-completers in the training runs; lift, 1 = chance; why not Milestone 1's "precision of at least 50%": a random list already reaches about 45% precision. Framework: the TRIPOD+AI reporting guideline (discrimination, calibration, usefulness for the decision).
**Visual:** worked example of one run, 20 person icons with the list boxed (worked example), definitions beside it.
**Speaker notes:** A list's length is set by capacity, not by a probability threshold, so we judge it by how many later non-completers it reaches. Look at the worked example: twenty students, nine of whom will not complete, and a list of five. Four of the nine are on the list, so recall is four of nine. But a list of five can hold at most five, so normalised recall is four of five; one would mean the best list possible. In the training runs that ceiling ranges from 46 to 100 percent of a run's non-completers, which is why raw recall would compare unlike runs. Lift compares the list with the run's base rate, so one means chance. This follows the TRIPOD+AI reporting guideline. Next, the whole evaluation plan.

## Slide 54 – The AQ2 Success Criterion   [9 Evaluation]   [Line: detail]

**Visual:** a number line from −5 to 20 points with a dashed line at 5 and three illustrative estimates with intervals (worked example).

## Slide 56 – Gate S1 and the Calibration Condition   [9 Evaluation]   [Line: main]
**Lead:** One gate decides whether a model replaces the rule's order, and probabilities are shown only if calibration holds.
**On the slide:** Decision: model list against rule-ranked list on all 13 test runs, day 27, 5 per 20 → gate S1: pooled normalised recall above the rule list's; 95% paired two-level bootstrap interval of the difference above 0; one-sided sign-flip test over the 13 per-run differences, p below 0.05 → pass: the model's order, reported with the number of extra non-completers listed; fail: the rule's order stays. S2, precision at capacity: reported, not a gate, since recall, precision and lift at capacity re-express the same listed non-completers. S3, calibration: calibration-in-the-large between −0.2 and 0.2 and slope between 0.8 and 1.2, otherwise ranks and reasons only, never recalibrated on 2014. Key: S1–S3 = the success criteria of the predictive model.
**Visual:** decision diagram (sequence): the comparison, the gate with its three tests, two exits.
**Speaker notes:** Here is the gate, S1, the single success criterion for AQ3. On all thirteen test runs, at day 27 with five contacts per twenty students, the model's pooled normalised recall must exceed the rule list's. Beating it means two things: the paired two-level bootstrap interval of the difference lies above zero, and a one-sided sign-flip test over the thirteen per-run differences gives p below 0.05. The gate sets no minimum gain, so a pass would be reported with the number of extra non-completers listed. If the model fails, the rule's order stays, which is an acceptable outcome. Precision, S2, is reported but decides nothing. Calibration, S3, decides only whether staff see probabilities; otherwise they see ranks and reasons. A good list must also be fair.

## Slide 57 – Fairness and Rule FR1   [9 Evaluation]   [Line: main]
**Lead:** The list is audited for equal recall across groups, and a clear gap sends the group to human review, not to an automatic verdict.
**On the slide:** Group attributes stay out of the predictors and serve only the audit. Criterion: equal recall at capacity. Rule FR1 (fairness review rule): flag a group for human review when its recall is more than 10 points below the other students' and the Holm-adjusted one-sided interval of the gap lies below 0. No flag means no evidence of a gap at that precision, not proof that the list is fair. Confirmatory groups in the 2014 test at day 27: declared disability 1,488; most deprived band (0–10%) 1,486; missing deprivation band 610; aged 55 or over 118, gap reported, "insufficient data, not cleared". FR1 threshold bar at 10 points. Key: deprivation band; Holm adjustment.
**Visual:** three number tiles for the group sizes (comparison) and a bar marking the 10-point review threshold.
**Speaker notes:** A list can be accurate overall and still miss one group, so the design audits fairness with one criterion: equal recall at capacity. The main harm of a list is missing a student who struggles, so a struggling student should have the same chance of being listed whatever the group. Rule FR1 flags a group for human review when its recall falls more than ten points below the other students' and the Holm-adjusted interval of the gap lies below zero. The confirmatory groups are declared disability, 1,488 test enrolments, the most deprived band, 1,486, and the missing band, 610. Students aged 55 or over, 118, are too few and stay not cleared. No flag means no evidence of a gap, not proof of fairness. What else protects the evaluation?
