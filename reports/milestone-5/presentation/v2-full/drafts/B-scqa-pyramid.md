# Draft B – Full speech script, SCQA with the Minto pyramid (Milestone 5 final presentation, team N04)

## Header

**Method.** SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) opens the talk, and the Minto pyramid principle orders
everything after it: the governing answer comes first (Slide 3), the deck then falls into five groups, each group
proves one key line of the pyramid, and each slide proves one point of its key line with an assertion-evidence layout
(a one-sentence lead as the assertion, a table, chart or worked example as the evidence). Source lineage follows the
deck-context discipline: every number on a slide or in the notes carries the section of the Milestone 5 report it comes
from, and nothing is rounded again.

**SCQA.**

- **Situation.** A Vietnamese university is expanding online and blended courses on its learning platform and already
  holds activity, enrolment and result records; withdrawal is a regulated quality measure (annual cap 10%, first-year
  cap 15%) [M5 §1].
- **Complication.** Students who stop taking part are noticed late (lecturers around week 5 or 6, the program manager
  after the semester, the support office at the academic warning list), while the window for effective support is two
  to four weeks; staff can follow up only a few students a week, and no open dataset of Vietnamese courses exists
  [M5 §1, §1.1, §1.2].
- **Question.** Can records the university already holds name, early and within staff capacity, the students to
  contact, with a factual reason for each name, and how much of that can we claim today?
- **Answer (governing thought).** On OULAD, the open stand-in, nearly half of the enrolments still registered at
  day 14 do not complete (45.3%), most non-completers have not yet missed a task by week 4, and five early signs dated
  by week 4 go with later non-completion in both years; so an early list must combine missed tasks with inactivity and
  show its signs as reasons staff check, while whether a model beats that rule and whether contact helps are fixed
  tests still to run: one held-out test on 2014 and a randomised pilot [M5 Abstract, §11.2, §11.3, §12.1].

**Big idea (one sentence).** The early signs are real and measurable before week 5, but they are reasons to look,
not proof that contact works, so the university should start with a simple, checked rule and test the rest.

**Grouping logic.** The five key lines are MECE by the kind of claim they defend: *why* we ask (need), *with what*
(data), *how* we judge (method), *what we found* (evidence), and *what it allows and does not allow* (use and limits).
Within each group the slides follow the analytics lifecycle order the course brief prescribes (Parts 1 to 14), so the
deductive pyramid and the chronological brief order coincide: problem → data → preparation → method → evidence →
assessment → use → conclusions. Each key line opens with its main slide and ends with a bridge to the next line.

**Arc.** Hook (noticed late) → answer up front → map → need (goal, KPIs, decisions, questions) → data (requirements,
selection, fit, quality, preparation) → method (strategy, evaluation, status) → evidence (traceability, AQ1, AQ2,
AQ3/AQ4 expected) → use (D1, D2, D3) → limits and future work → AI reflection → conclusions → take-home → questions.

## Slide 44 – Judging a List by Capacity   [9 Evaluation design]   [Line: main]
**Lead:** A list's length is set by staff capacity, so we judge it by how many later non-completers it reaches against the most it could reach.
**On the slide:** Capacity k = 3 or 5 contacts per 20 students; main operating point: 5 per 20 at day 27. Recall at capacity = share of a run's non-completers on the list.
