# Draft B – Speech script, Milestone 5 final presentation (SCQA and pyramid)

## Method notes (SCQA / pyramid)

**Framework.** SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) carries the whole talk; the pyramid principle (answer
first, then the supporting groups) organises each part. The `data-storytelling` arc maps onto it: the hook is the
answer in brief (Slide 2), the context is the situation (Slides 3–6), the rising action is data, preparation and method
(Slides 7–16), the climax is the evidence (Slides 17–22), the resolution is the assessment and use (Slides 23–27), and
the call to action is the pilot (Slides 27–29). From `pptx-deck-context`: one message per slide, a source tag behind
every number. The brief's rule that slide titles stay short topic headings overrides action titles, so each slide's
action statement sits in its **Lead** line, and each part opens with its answer slide or answer sentence before the
supporting slides (pyramid inside the part).

**SCQA statement.**

- **Situation.** A Vietnamese university that is expanding online and blended learning already holds LMS, enrolment
  and result records, and withdrawal is a regulated quality measure with annual caps [M5 §1].
- **Complication.** Students who stop taking part are noticed at week 5 or 6 or after the semester, while the window
  for effective support is two to four weeks; staff can contact only a few students a week; no open dataset of
  Vietnamese courses exists, and no dataset records a contact [M5 §1, §1.1, §4.1, §5.1].
- **Question.** Can records dated by week 4 tell staff, in time and within capacity, whom to contact and why, and how
  much of that can the evidence honestly claim?
- **Answer.** In part, and with the claim set by each question's level. On the stand-in data OULAD, 45.3% of the
  enrolments registered at day 14 did not complete, under one in five non-completers had missed a task by week 4, and
  all five early signs go with later non-completion in both years, a missed task by 40.8 and 39.3 points [M5 §11.2,
  §11.3]. So an early-sign rule with an activity part gives staff a factual reason beside each name today; whether a
  model ranks better is one designed test away, and whether contact helps needs a randomised pilot [M5 §11.4, §12.1].

**Pyramid per part.** Each part opens with its governing message, then two to five supporting slides:

| Part | Slides | Governing message (top of the pyramid) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Opening | 1–2 | Early records give a reason for each name now; ranking and contact still need testing |
| Problem | 3–6 | Students are noticed late, and three decisions need four questions answered at their own level |
| Data | 7–9 | We chose the data by the questions; OULAD alone passed, with named gaps |
| Preparation | 10–12 | The data forced two rules, and every feature is dated by its cut-off |
| Method | 13–16 | Simple methods against baselines, judged under capacity, by one gate and one fairness rule |
| Evidence | 17–22 | AQ1 and AQ2 answered on both years; AQ3 and AQ4 end at the design |
| Assessment | 23–25 | We state exactly what is measured, what is designed and what has no data |
| Use | 26–27 | Each decision receives what its risk allows; the pilot tests contact |
| Conclusions | 28–30 | Firmest first, then the open question, then thanks |

## Big idea and arc

**Big idea (core message).** Records a university already holds can show, by week 4, which students are drifting and
why, but only if activity counts beside missed tasks; that gives staff a factual reason beside each name now, while a
better ranking and the effect of contact remain to be tested.

**Arc.**

1. Problem (Milestone 1): students are noticed late; staff need a short list with a reason, for three decisions and
   four questions.
2. Data (Milestone 2): requirements first; OULAD is the only open dataset that passes, a stand-in with named gaps.
3. Preparation (Milestone 3): profiling forced the day-14 add-drop rule and an early-sign rule with an activity part;
   every feature is dated by its cut-off.
4. Method (Milestone 4): simple methods against baselines, lists judged under capacity, gate S1 and fairness rule FR1,
   frozen before any test.
5. Evidence, assessment and use (Milestone 5): AQ1 and AQ2 answered on 2013 and 2014; AQ3 and AQ4 are designs; the
   results serve D1–D3 at their level and lead to one test and a randomised pilot.

## Slide 14 – Lists Under Capacity   [Method]   (~50 seconds)

**Lead:** Because staff can contact only a few students, a list is judged by how many later non-completers it reaches
against the most its length allows.

**On the slide:**
- Capacity k: 3 or 5 contacts per 20 students; main operating point 5 per 20 at day 27.
- The early-sign rule names 28.8% of enrolments at day 27, more than a list of 15% or 25% can hold, so the names must
  be ranked.
- Recall at capacity = share of a run's non-completers on the list.
- Normalised recall = recall divided by the most a list of length k can reach; that ceiling ranges from 46% to 100%
  between training runs.
- Lift = share of non-completers on the list divided by the run's base rate.
- Why not "precision of at least 50%": non-completion is about 45%, so a random list already comes close.

**Visual:** a schematic run of 20 icons with a list of 5 (worked example of the definitions, no data values), showing
the reachable ceiling and the list's hits; key: filled = later non-completer, outlined box = on the list.

**Speaker notes:** Staff can contact three or five students per twenty, so a list is short. The early-sign rule alone names 28.8 percent of enrolments at day 27, more than a list can hold, which is why
the names must be ranked. Recall is the share of a run's non-completers on the list. But a short list has a ceiling, which varies
from 46 to 100 percent between runs. So we divide by it:
normalised recall. Lift compares the list with the run's base rate. Milestone one asked for precision of fifty
percent; with a base rate near forty-five, a random list nearly meets that, so we replaced it. Next, how we decide.

**Sources:** k, operating point, 46%–100% [M5 §9]; 28.8%, 15% or 25% [M5 §8]; base rate, precision [M5 §3; App. III
Table A.8].

## Slide 15 – Evaluation Plan and Gate S1   [Method]   (~50 seconds)

**Lead:** Every answer has a success criterion fixed before any code ran, and the model must beat the rule once, on
runs it has never seen.

**On the slide:**
- AQ1: two independent aggregation paths agree to the enrolment; every rate carries its denominator and interval.
- AQ2: adjusted risk difference of at least 5 points, interval excluding 0, same direction in at least two-thirds of
  the modules; p-values take no part in the verdict.
- AQ3, gate S1: pooled normalised recall above the rule list's, paired bootstrap interval above 0, sign-flip test
  p below 0.05.
- Development: leave-one-run-out folds on the 9 runs of 2013; a challenger replaces the primary model only with a gain
