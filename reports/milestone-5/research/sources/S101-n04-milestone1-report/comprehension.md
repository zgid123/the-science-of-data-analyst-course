# S101 – N04 (2026). Milestone 1 report: business understanding and analytical questions

## Summary in my own words

The Milestone 1 proposal sets the project's business frame for a scenario Vietnamese university that is expanding online and blended learning. From AI-run interviews with five simulated stakeholders and a validation round, it states the problem (disengaging students are noticed late), the Business Goal, a SMART Goal with assumed baseline 70% and target 75% by the end of 2027–2028, one primary and two secondary KPIs, and four analytical questions at the four analytics levels. It classifies three decisions D1–D3 by level, programmability and reversibility, and gives preliminary data requirements, naming OULAD as a possible open stand-in. Every figure about the university is a scenario assumption.

## Key facts

- Scenario program size is assumed – "about 1,200 students in a faculty of about 4,000 (both scenario assumptions)"
- Problem – "students who stop taking part are noticed late and only some of them are noticed at all"
- Regulation – "limits the share of students who withdraw to 10% a year overall and 15% in the first year of study"
- Business Goal – "improve successful course completion in the program's online and blended courses by identifying students who stop taking part early enough to support them within the semester"
- SMART baseline assumed – "The proposal therefore assumes a baseline completion rate of 70%."
- Pilot size assumed – "The pilot courses are about four of the program's online and blended courses, with about 600 enrolments in total"
- Early-sign rule (Milestone 1 form) – "a student who has missed two or more assessed tasks, or submitted them late, by the end of week 4"
- Capacity – "The three officers of the support office can follow up about 30–40 students a week between them"
- Review points – "Contact lists reach staff at two review points: week 3, before the second or third assignment deadline, and week 5"
- AQ4 limits in Milestone 1 – "reach a precision of at least 50%, and keep first-year recall within ten points of later-year recall"
- D3 is close to one-way – "Close to one-way: it changes the university's data practice and students' trust"
- Interviews were AI on both sides – "Generative AI took both sides of each conversation."
- Decision levels follow Gorry and Scott Morton (1971; cited in S101 as [24]) – "following Anthony's levels as presented by Gorry and Scott Morton [24]"
