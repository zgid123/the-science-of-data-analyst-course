# S909 – Miao, F., & Holmes, W. (2023). Guidance for generative AI in education and research. UNESCO

## Summary in my own words

UNESCO's first global guidance on generative AI (GenAI) in education and research, drafted by Fengchun Miao and Wayne Holmes. It argues for a human-centred approach that protects human agency, and stresses that GenAI output is fluent but cannot be trusted to be accurate: it fabricates facts ("hallucination"), reproduces dominant views and errors go unnoticed unless the user knows the topic. It therefore asks researchers, teachers and learners to evaluate every output critically and never accept it at face value, with human review matched to the stakes of the use. For our report it grounds the Milestone 5 rule that AI may support but not replace evidence and reasoning, and the AI Decision Log's verification column.

## Key facts

- Publisher and year – "Published in 2023 by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization,"
- Authorship – "the drafting of the publication was led by Fengchun"
- Accuracy – "GenAI cannot be trusted to be accurate."
- Errors are hidden – "the errors will go unnoticed unless the user has a solid knowledge of the topic in question."
- Fabrication – "it is well-known that GPTs make up some things that do not exist in real life."
- Human evaluation needed – "more sophisticated outputs need skilled human input and must be critically evaluated before they are used."
- Critical stance – "Learners, teachers and researchers should never accept the information provided by the GenAI at face value and should always critically assess it."
- Not an authority – "GenAI can never be an authoritative source of knowledge on whatever topic it engages with."
- Unreliable source – "Understand the role of GenAI as a fast but frequently unreliable source of information."
- Human agency – "core humanistic values that promote human agency, inclusion, equity, gender"
- Over-reliance – "to prevent their overreliance on and addiction to the generated content."
- Quoted provider guidance (OpenAI, GPT-4 report, cited in the guidance) – "Great care should be taken when using language model outputs, particularly in high-stakes contexts"
