# Reader test of the Vietnamese report (stage 5)

Reader: a lecturer and program coordinator at a Vietnamese university, fluent in data analysis, new to this project,
reading only the `.vi.md` main text (00-1, 01-00 to 14-00) plus the abbreviation and glossary pages. Evidence codes and
citations in square brackets were ignored. Date: 2026-11-09.

**Overall judgement.** Yes, a reader of the field could explain the document afterwards: the problem (late detection of
disengaged students), the three decisions D1–D3, the four questions, the OULAD stand-in, the 45,7% non-completion rate,
the five early signs led by "missed one assessment" (+40,8 points), and the fact that AQ3–AQ4 are designs only all come
through by Section 11. But the first reading is slow and often requires the glossary. The abstract is the hardest page:
it uses "kỳ mở module", "còn hiệu lực tại ngày 14", "thời điểm chốt dữ liệu", "Level 1" and "dấu hiệu sớm" before any of
them is explained, so a reader of the abstract alone could not say what population the 45,7% describes. Throughout, the
text leans on internal labels (C03–C17, B2, S1, FR1, V01–V15, G1–G5, DQ08, "Level 1/2", "GREEN/YELLOW/RED") that are
either never defined in the main text or defined only in a later section or the glossary, and on calques from English
("kỳ mở" for presentation/run, "đi kèm" for "is associated with", "về sau không hoàn thành", "không bao giờ được",
"có hiệu chỉnh phạt", "mức vượt tỷ lệ nền"). Several sentences pack three or four ideas joined by semicolons, and a few
statements look contradictory on first reading (2014 outcomes "giữ kín" yet 2014 counts used in AQ1; "mọi mắt xích đều
có bằng chứng" next to GAP cells; 3,19% vs 7,39% duplicate clicks; "Trả lời một phần" next to "Đạt cả năm tiêu chí").
The Vietnamese is grammatical; the difficulty is density, undefined labels and English sentence order, not language
errors.
