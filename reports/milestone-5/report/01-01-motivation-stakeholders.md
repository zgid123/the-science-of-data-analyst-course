## 1.1. Motivation and Stakeholders

The problem is worth analysing because the records that could show the drift early already exist, and the staff who
could act on them each see only part of it. The course lecturers, the lecturer-advisors (lecturers who advise student
classes, cố vấn học tập) and the student support office decide whom to contact at each review point (D1); the program
manager decides where to place their time each semester (D2); and university leadership, with legal staff and the data
office, decides whether to extend the approach (D3); students are the subject of every data use (Table A.1, Appendix I).
Their needs, from simulated interviews in which generative artificial intelligence (AI) took both sides, motivate the design but are
never evidence: they ask for a short list at weeks 3 and 5 with a factual reason for each name, within about 30–40 contacts a week
for the support office and three to five for each lecturer-advisor, for support only and with no automated step. The
Open University, which published our dataset, runs a tool, OU Analyse, that informs teachers about students at risk so
that the teachers act [3].
