## Appendix XII – AQ1 Results in Detail

This appendix supports Section 11.2. It gives the definitions behind the AQ1 results, the outcome shares under both denominators in each year, the rates by module and start month with the start-month gap within each module, the effect of the denominator by run, the rates by group of students, the week of the first missed task by outcome and by module, and the comparison between 2013 and 2014.

The first question is descriptive and asks what share of enrolments end in pass, fail or withdrawal, by module, run, start month and year, and in which week non-completers first miss an assessed task (AQ1, Section 3). Its answer sets the size of the problem that the early-sign rule, the model and the contact lists of the later questions address. We computed it on all 22 module runs, following the Milestone 4 plan in every step: on the 9 runs of 2013 (6 modules, 13,529 enrolments) and the 13 runs of 2014 (all 7 modules, 19,064 enrolments) apart, and on the 22 runs pooled (32,593 enrolments). Module CCC ran only in 2014, so it enters the 2014 and pooled figures and no 2013 figure. Using the 2014 runs for description leaves the AQ3 design as Milestone 4 fixed it (Change Log C15, Table A.8).

Three definitions carry every number below. Non-completion is a final result of Fail or Withdrawn, and completion is Pass or Distinction. The primary denominator is the KPI population, the enrolments still registered at day 14 (registered by day 14, or with an unknown registration day, and not unregistered by it), which mirrors the add-drop rule of the primary KPI (Section 2.1); the second denominator is all enrolments. Week n covers days 7(n−1) to 7n−1 of the run, so the end of week 4 is day 27. Every rate carries a 95% Wilson score interval, and every difference between two rates a 95% Newcombe hybrid score interval.

### What AQ1 means for the project

AQ1 answers its descriptive question for both years with high confidence in the sense of Section 11: the results are computationally verified for OULAD 2013 and 2014, since the populations are fixed, the two aggregation paths agree to the enrolment in each year, the 2014 counts equal those Milestone 3 published and every rate carries its interval, while their validity for the university's courses remains open. Nearly one in two enrolments still registered at day 14 did not complete in each year, the rate varies by more than 30 points between runs, and the denominator alone can move it by up to 13.6 points. For the program manager's decision on where to place support time (D2), this gives a hypothesis rather than a placement: the modules and start months with the highest rates in OULAD show where support time would go first if the same pattern appears in the university's own records. Among the six shared modules, AAA has the lowest and DDD the highest rate in both years, while the February and October start months have no direct counterpart in the university's two-semester calendar, so the start-month contrast must be re-examined there. A high rate also shows where non-completion concentrates, not where support would help most. The timing result adds that a list built on missed tasks alone would come too late for most non-completers in both years, which is the reason the early-sign rule also counts inactivity. The answer describes OULAD's UK distance-learning modules of 2013–2014 only; it does not explain why the rates differ, and the university's own rates remain open, as Section 12 sets out. Section 11.3 turns from how many students did not complete to which early signs go with non-completion.
