## 11.4. AQ3 and AQ4: Expected Outputs

Sections 11.2 and 11.3 answered the descriptive and diagnostic questions on real data from 2013. The predictive question (AQ3) and the prescriptive ranking question (AQ4) are different in kind: their answers depend on a model trained on the 2013 runs and tested once on the 2014 runs, and that prototype has not been run (Section 10). Because the project completed Level 1 for these two questions, their answers are the outputs they are expected to produce, the success criteria that Milestone 4 fixed before any result existed, and the way a result would be read. No model metric is given, because no model has been trained and the 2014 outcomes stay unopened.

### 11.4.1. AQ3: estimating non-completion at the end of weeks 2 and 4

AQ3 asks how well non-completion can be estimated from the data up to the end of week 2 and of week 4, compared with the early-sign rule, when a model built on the 2013 runs is applied to the 2014 runs (Section 3). The expected output is a risk score for each enrolment at each cut-off, day 13 and day 27, used as a rank within its run and shown as a probability only if calibration holds. The proposed primary model is a penalised logistic regression on the 19 usable primary predictors, one model per cut-off, chosen because staff must see each student's reasons and because the 2013 runs give about 230 non-completers per model parameter (Section 8.1). It would be compared with three baselines: the early-sign rule (C10) ranked by its number of signs, a logistic model on background variables only, and a random list. Gradient-boosted trees or the extended predictor set would replace the primary model only with a gain of at least 0.03 in pooled normalised recall and gains in at least 7 of the 9 development folds, a margin fixed before any fold result.

The question would be answered by a single gate, S1, judged once on all 13 runs of 2014 (15,724 enrolments registered at day 27). At day 27 and a capacity of 5 contacts per 20 students, the model's pooled normalised recall would have to exceed that of the rule-ranked list of the same length, with the 95% interval of the difference from a paired two-level bootstrap (runs, then enrolments within runs) lying above 0 and a one-sided sign-flip permutation test over the 13 per-run differences giving a p-value below 0.05. Normalised recall divides the recall of a list at capacity by the most a list of that length could reach, so that 1 means the best list possible; this matters because a list of 5 per 20 at day 27 can hold between 46% and 100% of a run's non-completers in the training runs. The gate output would report the pooled normalised recall of the model and of the rule, their difference with its bootstrap interval, the sign-flip p-value, the wins, ties and losses over the 13 runs, and the verdict. Gate S1 sets no minimum size of gain, so a pass would be reported with the number of extra non-completers the model's lists reach, which shows whether the gain is worth giving up the transparency of the rule (Section 8.1). Precision at capacity (S2) would be reported beside it without being a gate. Probabilities would be shown to staff only if the calibration check S3 holds, that is, calibration-in-the-large between −0.2 and 0.2 and a calibration slope between 0.8 and 1.2; with a training rate of 44.4%, these bounds allow an average predicted risk between about 40% and 49%. A failed calibration check would limit the output to ranks and reasons and would never lead to recalibration on the 2014 runs.

Several conditions frame how an AQ3 result would be read. The 9 runs of 2014 that pair with a 2013 run (10,801 enrolments at day 27) and the 4 unpaired runs (4,923: CCC in February and October, EEE and GGG in February) would be reported apart, so that transfer to a module or start month the model has not seen is visible (Change Log C16, test on all 13 runs of 2014). The 1,586 test enrolments at day 27 of students already seen in 2013 would stay in the test and be removed in a sensitivity analysis. Every other population, cut-off and capacity is a sensitivity analysis that cannot change the verdict. Fold results from the leave-one-run-out development on 2013 would serve only the choice between candidates and would never be reported as performance. If the model does not beat the rule, that result would be reported as it is, and the rule's order would stay on the D1 lists, with its own list performance, measured as baseline B2 of the same test, beside it.

### 11.4.2. AQ4: a ranked list under assumed capacity

AQ4 asks which enrolments should rank first on a list of an assumed capacity, with what gain over a random list and what recall, overall and by deprivation band and disability (Section 3). The expected output is a ranked list per run of the length capacity allows, 3 or 5 per 20 students, with the signs behind each name, so that a member of staff decides whom to contact. The list would be built from the frozen AQ3 scores and compared with a random list of the same length, whose expected recall is the list length divided by the run size, and with a list ranked by the early-sign rule. Its primary metric would be normalised recall at capacity, with lift, overlap with the rule list, the split between Fail and Withdrawn among the listed students, and recall at 3 and at 5 per 20 reported beside it.

AQ4 would be answered when four conditions hold together: the list meets gate S1; the fairness rule FR1 passes or its flags have been reviewed; the assumption that the order of risk matches the order of need is stated as untested; and no statement is made about the effect of contact. FR1 flags a confirmatory group for human review when its recall at capacity is more than 10 points below that of the other students and the one-sided 95% bootstrap interval of the gap, Holm-adjusted over the three groups, lies below 0. The confirmatory groups are those with at least 400 test enrolments: students with a declared disability (1,488 at day 27), the most deprived band, 0–10% (1,486), and the missing deprivation band (610). Students aged 55 or over (118) are too few for the confirmatory check, so their gap would be reported as insufficient data, not as cleared. The fairness output would give, for each group, its size, its non-completers, its recall and that of the other students, the difference, the upper bound of the Holm-adjusted interval and the flag. A flag calls for human judgement on why the gap arises; it does not reject the model. The absence of a flag would mean no evidence of a gap at the precision the group's size allows, not a finding that the list is fair. Sensitivity outputs would cover the paired and unpaired runs, CCC apart, the test without students seen in 2013, without Withdrawn enrolments lacking a withdrawal day, a labels-available variant for the February runs, the day-13 cut-off and a capacity of 3 per 20.

### 11.4.3. What the expected outputs can and cannot support

Table 11.12 sums up, for both questions, the expected output, the result that would answer the question and the limits fixed in advance.

**Table 11.12. Expected outputs of AQ3 and AQ4 and the criteria that would decide them**

| Question | Expected output | Result that would answer the question | If the criterion is not met | Limits fixed in advance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| AQ3 (predictive) | Risk score per enrolment at days 13 and 27, a rank within the run; a probability only if S3 holds | Gate S1 on all 13 test runs at day 27, 5 per 20: pooled normalised recall above the rule's, bootstrap interval above 0, sign-flip p < 0.05 | The rule list stays the answer for D1; the result is reported as it is | One earlier run per module and start month; module CCC unseen in development; 9 training runs from 6 modules |
| AQ4 (prescriptive ranking) | Ranked list per run of 3 or 5 per 20, with each student's signs; a member of staff decides | S1 met by the list; FR1 passes or flags reviewed; risk-need assumption stated as untested; no claim about contact | No list replaces the rule's order; flagged gaps go to human review | Capacity assumed; runs stand in for the groups of each lecturer-advisor; no contact data, so nothing on whom contact helps |

These outputs would let the course lecturers, the lecturer-advisors and the support office know whom to list first at the week-3 and week-5 review points (D1), but they would not say who should make the contact, with which offer, or whether contact raises completion. A predictive score ranks students by risk; it does not explain why a student struggles, and it does not show that the students at highest risk are those who would benefit most from contact. Those questions need contact records and a comparison design that OULAD cannot provide, as Section 11.5 and Section 12 set out. Section 11.5 brings the four answers together and relates them to the decisions they support.
