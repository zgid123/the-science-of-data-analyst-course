## 11.4. AQ3 and AQ4: Early Warning and Ranked Lists

AQ3 is answered by the single test of Section 10: a penalised logistic model built on the 2013 runs ranks the
2014 students better than the early-sign rule, by a small margin, and gate S1 is met. The answer holds at both
cut-offs: lists of 5 per 20 hold 38.6% of later non-completers at day 13 and 41.5% at day 27, so waiting two more weeks
adds little. The model and the rule share most names; each list holds 903 students the other does not, and the
model's own picks more often had a previous attempt at the module (30.7% against 6.4%), and 59.7% of them do not
complete, against 50.6% of the rule's own picks. The answer is a rank with reasons, not a probability, because calibration fails in 8 of the 13
runs (Table A.37, Appendix XIV).

AQ4 is answered as a ranking. On the 2014 runs the first contact per tutor group of 20 finds a non-completer 92.4% of
the time, the fifth 57.6% and the sixth 55.2%; from the seventh on, 47.3% or less, close to the base rate of 44.0%.
For 80.7% of listed students the main reason is behavioural (low activity, a missed task or a late submission); for
14.2% it is prior education, a background fact a tutor cannot act on. Rule FR1 flags none of the three confirmatory
groups: students with a declared disability are reached slightly better (+5.5 points), the most deprived band about
equally (+1.3) and the missing band less well (−7.4 points, interval −16.8 to +2.3), within the 10-point tolerance;
students aged 55 or over (118) are too few to clear. Of the 2,868 listed non-completers, 111 withdraw in the week the
list arrives and 57.4% stay to the end and fail. The risk order is not shown to match the need for help, and neither
answer says who should make contact or whether contact helps.
