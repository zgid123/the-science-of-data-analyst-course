# 11. Findings, Discussion and Decision Support

Section 10 states that no predictive prototype has been run, so the evaluation design of Section 9 stays a proposal for the two predictive and prescriptive questions. In contrast, the descriptive question (AQ1) and the diagnostic question (AQ2) needed no model training and no access to the 2014 test runs, so we ran them on the 9 module runs of 2013 exactly as Milestone 4 had fixed them before any result existed (Sections 8.1 and 9.1). The findings return to the four final Analytical Questions of Section 3 and answer each one at the level of evidence the project actually reached: AQ1 and AQ2 with real results on their stated populations, AQ3 and AQ4 with the outputs they are expected to produce and the success criteria that would decide them. Building on these answers, Section 11.5 relates them to the decisions the results can support for the program manager, the lecturer-advisors and the support office (D1–D3, Section 2.1).

Each answer is read according to its analytics level, because the same number means different things at different levels. A descriptive rate states what happened to a defined population; it does not explain why. A regression fitted to capture how a sign and an outcome move together is itself descriptive of an association, not a test of a cause, and Shmueli (2010) separates this use explicitly from causal explanation and from prediction [S900]. As a result, the AQ2 estimates are associations within module runs, never effects of the signs. A predictive claim, in turn, needs its own evaluation on data the model has not seen, because explanatory power does not imply predictive power [S900]; for this reason AQ3 carries no performance figure until the single test on the 2014 runs has been run. Finally, a recommendation drawn from observational data is a decision option to test, since questions of effectiveness are ideally answered by a randomised experiment [S904]. Statistical significance plays a supporting role only: a p-value does not measure the size or importance of an effect [S903], so every finding below is stated as an effect size with its 95% interval and its population.

**Confidence labels.** The confidence given to each answer uses three grades. High means that the counts are verified by two independent computations on the stated population, so the answer is computationally verified for that population; it says nothing about validity for the university's courses. Moderate means that the estimates keep their direction under every check run, while they rest on one year, on a model whose fit has a known weakness or on associations that unrecorded reasons could explain. Not applicable means that no result exists yet.
