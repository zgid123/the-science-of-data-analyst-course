## 12.1. Future Work and Data Needed

Five steps run on OULAD as it stands: the Level 2 prototype of AQ3, which also measures the rule's list (baseline B2),
followed by the AQ4 list and its fairness audit, the AQ2 replication, the several-modules sign and the 2014 detail of
AQ1 (Table A.42, Appendix XV). At the university, the baseline is recounted under the add-drop rule and the assumed
values are replaced by its records. Vietnam's Law on Artificial Intelligence requires human control over every
decision of an AI system [17], and ranking learners is listed as a high-risk use in education [18], so the list
needs a risk classification first. A randomised pilot among the flagged students of the pilot courses, with time zero at the eligibility cut-off, then
tests whether contact helps, because only random assignment separates the effect of contact from the reasons students
are flagged [14]; its contacts are logged with their outcomes before any model is retrained, since a model retrained
on outcomes shaped by contact would learn its own influence. Adoption also depends on people: at the Open University,
the spread of predictive learning analytics depended on the faculty's engagement and on teachers acting as champions
[19], so the lecturer-advisors and the support office take part in the pilot as its users from the start.
