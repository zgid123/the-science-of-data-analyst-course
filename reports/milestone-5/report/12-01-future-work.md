## 12.1. Future Work and Data Needed

The limitation log of Section 12 separates what the data showed no evidence for, what OULAD holds no data to test and what stays untested by design. Future work follows the same split: the untested parts need the predictive test on data that already exist, while the parts without data need records that only the university and its pilot can create. Table 12.2 lists each item of future work with the question it answers, the data it needs and the step it depends on.

The first five steps can run on OULAD as it stands. The Level 2 prototype of AQ3 follows the protocol Milestone 4 froze: baselines first, the penalised logistic regression and its challengers on leave-one-run-out folds of 2013, a second freeze with the test access recorded, and a single scoring of the 13 runs of 2014, with unfavourable results reported as they come. Only after that test may the 2014 outcomes inform the other questions, so the AQ4 list and its fairness audit, the AQ2 replication with module CCC, the several-modules sign and the 2014 detail of AQ1 all follow it. Their outputs are already specified, for instance the AQ2 replication as 2013 and 2014 estimates with intervals per sign and module, so no choice remains to be made once the 2014 data open.

The remaining steps need data that no open dataset holds. None of the seven candidate datasets screened in Milestone 2 records contacts, attendance or year of study for the same students, so three parts of the proposal can only be answered with the pilot's own records: which contact works best and what its effect is (AQ4's deferred part), comparisons by year of study, delivery mode and attendance (AQ1 and AQ2), and the follow-up rate of secondary KPI 2 together with the contact part of secondary KPI 1. We do not impute or simulate any of these variables, because invented values would turn assumptions into apparent evidence. Several design values also rest on assumptions or on simulated stakeholder views and must be replaced by the university's records: the baseline completion rate, the capacity figures, the one-week data delay, the list-length scenarios of AQ4 and the ten-point fairness gap of rule FR1. The acceptance of activity-based contact, in turn, was voiced only by simulated students in Milestone 1, so it needs confirmation from real students in Vietnam before the pilot.

Use at the university also brings legal duties. Vietnam's Law on Artificial Intelligence, No. 134/2025/QH15, applies from 1 March 2026 and requires that human control and the ability to intervene be kept over every decision of an AI system [S914], and Decision 33/2026/QĐ-TTg lists among the high-risk uses in education AI systems that automatically assess or rank learners and those that monitor or analyse learner behaviour [S915], so a contact list built from a model is likely to be classed as high-risk. A pilot therefore needs a risk classification first, and staff keep the final say on every contact. The pilot itself is proposed as a randomised comparison with time zero at the eligibility cut-off, because only random assignment separates the effect of contact from the reasons students are flagged [S904]. Randomisation would take place among the flagged students within the pilot courses and answers whether contact helps, while the course-level comparison of Section 2 measures the SMART Goal; the pilot protocol fixes, before contact begins, the share of flagged students assigned to contact, which also bounds how much of the five-point target the contacts can carry. Steps 6 and 7 must be complete before contact begins in Semester 2 of 2026–2027, which is why the start date of Section 2 is conditional on them. Its contacts are logged with their outcomes before any model is retrained, since once the list is used, outcomes partly reflect the contacts made and a model retrained on them would learn its own influence.

Finally, a working model is not enough for adoption. At the Open University, the spread of predictive learning analytics depended on the faculty's engagement, on teachers acting as champions, on evidence generation and dissemination, on digital literacy and on conceptions about teaching online [S113]. We therefore propose that the lecturer-advisors and the support office take part in the pilot as its users from the start, and that its evidence, including a negative result, be reported back to them. Section 13 turns from the data to the way generative AI supported this work and how its output was verified.
