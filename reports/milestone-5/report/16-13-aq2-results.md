## Appendix XIII – AQ2 Results in Detail

This appendix supports Section 11.3. It gives the population, signs and model of AQ2, the association of each sign in 2013 and in 2014 with its prevalence and odds ratio, non-completion by the number of signs present, the estimates by module, the sensitivity runs and model checks with a second implementation of the same design, the estimates by group of students, the comparison of the two years, the several-modules sign and what the results mean for the project.

Section 11.2 showed that nearly half of the enrolments still registered at day 14 did not complete, and that most non-completers had not yet missed a task by the end of week 4. The second question is diagnostic and asks which signs recorded by the end of week 4 (missed and late tasks, inactivity, activity against the run's median) go with later non-completion, compared within each module (AQ2, Section 3). Its answer supplies the reasons shown beside each name on a contact list and tests whether the parts of the early-sign rule (C10) carry information. As a diagnostic question on observational data, it is answered as an association: a regression used to capture how a sign and an outcome move together describes that association and does not test a cause [10]. No estimate below is an effect of a sign on completion.

### Population, signs and model

We estimated AQ2 on the 9 runs of 2013, as Milestone 4 fixed, on the 11,811 enrolments still registered at day 27, of whom 5,245 (44.4%) did not complete, and replicated it with the same specification on the 13 runs of 2014, including module CCC, on 15,724 enrolments, of whom 6,911 (44.0%) did not complete, as the Milestone 4 plan provides (C14; Change Log C15, Table A.8). Five signs were measured at day 27, each from events dated on or before that day. The two task signs are a TMA or CMA due by day 27 with no submission by day 27 (missed task) and a TMA due by day 27 submitted late (late TMA); they are defined only in the runs with a task due by day 27, 7 runs with 9,960 enrolments in 2013 and 9 runs with 12,585 in 2014, because the EEE and GGG runs had no task due by then. The three click signs are defined in every run: no click on days 21–27 (inactive in the last seven days), clicks on days 0–27 below the median of the same run (low relative activity), and no click before day 0 (no activity before the start). The first three signs are the parts of the early-sign rule (C10); the fourth is the "activity against the run's median" that AQ2 names, cut at the run median because the plan fixes no other cut; the fifth comes from the inputs of the Milestone 4 AQ2 Modeling Brief.

The model follows the Milestone 4 plan. It is a logistic regression of non-completion on the five signs, with a fixed effect for each run, previous attempts and credits studied as background terms, and sign × module terms coded as deviations from the average module, so that each sign is compared within module run. Standard errors are heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1) without clustering; the model has 37 parameters, converged on 11,811 enrolments and reaches a McFadden pseudo-R² of 0.145 in 2013, and 45 parameters with a pseudo-R² of 0.135 on 15,724 enrolments in 2014. A module where a sign never occurs gets no sign × module term and takes the average-module estimate: no CCC enrolment of 2014 had submitted a TMA late by day 27. Each sign's effect is reported as an average risk difference in percentage points: the mean, over the enrolments where the sign is defined, of the predicted risk with the sign present minus the predicted risk with it absent, with a 95% interval by the delta method. Odds ratios at the average module are reported beside it, and the unadjusted difference serves as the baseline. The Milestone 4 success criterion calls a sign a consistent association when its mutually adjusted risk difference is at least 5 points, its interval excludes 0, and its direction is the same in at least two-thirds of the modules where it is defined. Because a p-value does not measure the size or importance of an effect, and decisions should not rest on whether it passes a threshold [12], p-values take no part in this verdict.

The pattern of the residuals shows that yes/no activity signs miss part of the activity gradient. Enrolments with no click at all in the first four weeks did not complete more often than the model predicts, and those just below the median less often, because the below-median sign treats every enrolment under the median alike, although non-completion keeps falling as activity rises. In 2014 the residuals follow the same pattern, from +5.1 points for no click to −5.3 points for twice the median or more. The association between early activity and non-completion is therefore graded rather than a step at one threshold.

Because the misfit concerns the click signs whose adjusted values serve as reasons on the D1 list, we added one sensitivity run after seeing it, which the Milestone 4 plan did not contain: the same model on the same 11,811 enrolments of 2013 with the logarithm of one plus the clicks on days 0–27 added as a continuous adjustment. In 2013 the refit removes the misfit, with the residuals of all five activity levels inside their ±2 standard-error bands, and its McFadden pseudo-R² rises from 0.145 to 0.150. With this adjustment the three click signs shrink but each stays above 5 points with an interval excluding 0: no click on days 21–27 falls to 9.3 points (5.8–12.7), activity below the run median to 5.8 (3.4–8.3) and no activity before the start to 8.9 (6.1–11.6). The task signs barely move: a missed task falls to 37.3 points (33.8–40.9) and a late TMA to 19.5 (15.2–23.9). Every sign keeps the consistent-association verdict in 2013, although the below-median sign now has the same direction in 5 of 6 modules rather than 6 of 6 and an interval whose lower end lies below 5 points. The same adjustment on 2014 gives a missed task 35.6 points (32.4–38.8), a late TMA 19.5 (14.9–24.1), inactivity 10.7 (8.0–13.4) and no activity before the start 6.5 (4.3–8.8), all still consistent associations, while activity below the run median falls to 5.9 points (3.7–8.1) with the same direction in only 4 of 7 modules and no longer meets the criterion; the pseudo-R² rises from 0.135 to 0.141. In 2014 the refit reduces the misfit without removing it: three of the five activity levels stay outside their bands, the largest for enrolments with no click, −3.0 points against a band of 2.6. Much of what the binary relative-activity sign carried is therefore the activity gradient itself, which supports the design choice of Milestone 4 to give the AQ3 model continuous click measures, the click counts before the start and up to the cut-off (Section 7.1, Appendix VIII), rather than the binary signs alone. Because this run was chosen after the residuals were seen, it qualifies the verdict rather than replacing the planned estimates, which remain the primary result.

A second, separately written implementation of the same Milestone 4 design reaches the same five verdicts in both years. It differs from ours in two specification choices: it cuts low activity at half the run median rather than at the median, and it averages the late-TMA difference only over the modules where a late TMA can occur, which leaves out the 3,521 CCC enrolments of 2014; it also enters previous attempts and credits on a log scale and gives a module its own sign term only where at least 20 enrolments have the sign and 20 do not. Under its own specification a late TMA goes with 21.7 points in 2013 and 22.5 in 2014, and low activity below half the run median with 14.7 and 14.8, the values of our sensitivity run at that cut to within 0.1 point. With low activity cut at the run median, as here, its estimates lie within 0.11 points of ours for every sign and year except the late TMA of 2014, 21.8 points against 20.3, the difference that leaving out CCC makes. With clicks added as a continuous adjustment, it also finds activity below the run median meeting the criterion in 2013 and failing it in 2014, at 5.9 points in both years.

### Groups of students

The Milestone 4 plan asks that the associations be described by gender, disability and deprivation band rather than adjusted away. Refitted within each of the 15 groups, the missed-task sign keeps a positive mutually adjusted difference with an interval excluding 0 in every group, from 18.8 points (0.05–37.6) among the 418 enrolments with a missing deprivation band to 49.9 points (43.2–56.5) in the 30–40% band. It goes with 44.9 points (41.0–48.8) for women and 37.1 points (33.0–41.3) for men, and with 40.6 points (37.4–43.8) for students without and 38.7 points (32.2–45.2) for students with a declared disability. The click signs are smaller and less certain in the small deprivation-band groups: their intervals include 0 for inactivity in three of the eleven bands and for no activity before the start in five, and the late-TMA interval includes 0 in the least deprived band. In 2014 the missed-task sign again keeps a positive mutually adjusted difference with an interval excluding 0 in each of the 14 groups where the model could be fitted, from 32.7 points (25.8–39.6) among students with a declared disability to 44.8 points (37.2–52.4) in the 30–40% band; the group with a missing deprivation band (610 enrolments) is described without adjustment, because its model did not converge. The intervals include 0 for no activity before the start in four bands and for a late TMA in two. These are descriptions of where the association is weaker or less certain, not grounds for treating any group differently.

### Comparison of the two years and the several-modules sign

The replication answers whether the 2013 associations hold in a later year, in a module not seen in 2013 and in 13 runs instead of 9. They do: each sign keeps its verdict, and the mutually adjusted difference changes by at most two points between the years, by −1.6 points (−5.8 to 2.6) for a missed task, +0.1 (−6.3 to 6.4) for a late TMA, +1.4 (−2.7 to 5.4) for inactivity, +1.2 (−1.2 to 3.7) for activity below the run median and −1.9 (−5.4 to 1.6) for no activity before the start, each interval including 0. Pooled over all 22 runs, on 27,535 enrolments registered at day 27, the five signs go with 39.8, 20.6, 14.7, 14.0 and 9.4 points and meet the criterion in every module. The several-modules sign (tasks missed in more than one module at once) can be described only on 2014, because of the 1,382 enrolments that a student took at the same time as another, only 12 fall in 2013 (C12, several-modules sign described for 2014 only). In 2014 it marks 28 of the 15,724 enrolments registered at day 27, of whom 27 did not complete (96.4%, interval 82.3–99.4), against 43.9% of all others, a difference of 52.6 points (38.4 to 55.6). The sign is strong but too rare to change a list: 28 enrolments are 0.2% of the 2014 day-27 population, and every one of them already shows a missed task. Attendance at in-class sessions has no counterpart in OULAD at all (Section 5.1).

### What AQ2 means for the project

AQ2 answers its diagnostic question for both years with moderate confidence: each of the five signs goes with higher later non-completion within module runs in 2013 and again in 2014, in the same direction across modules apart from one wide AAA estimate, and under every planned sensitivity run, and a missed task goes with the largest difference. The confidence is moderate rather than high because the binary click signs fit the activity gradient poorly in both years, and the relative-activity sign falls below the criterion in 2014 once activity enters as a continuous measure, so it is the weakest of the five reasons. In addition, part of what the missed-task sign marks may be students who are already leaving: the share of non-completers who are still reachable, those not withdrawn within seven days after the cut-off, belongs to the planned error analysis of the AQ3 list (Section 9.2) and is not estimated here, so the 40.8 and 39.3 points describe an association with later non-completion, not the share of students who can still be helped. For the lists of D1, the signs give each name a factual reason that staff can check against the student's record, and they confirm that each part of the early-sign rule (C10) carries information on its own. For D2, they suggest the hypothesis that support time early in the run is best placed in modules where the signs are common, to be checked on the university's records, since a common sign marks concentrated risk, not proven benefit. The estimates remain associations in two years of UK distance-learning modules: one possibility the data cannot rule out is that a student who stops clicking has already decided to leave for reasons they do not record, so the signs are not shown to cause non-completion, and contact triggered by them is an option to test rather than a proven remedy (Section 11.5). Section 11.4 turns to the two questions that build on these signs, AQ3 and AQ4, whose results wait for the predictive test.
