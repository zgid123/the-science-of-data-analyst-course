# 1. Introduction and Educational Context

Students who stop taking part in an online course rarely leave at once: they miss a task, open the course less often
and fall silent weeks before the course records a fail or a withdrawal. The project is set in a Vietnamese university
that is expanding online and blended learning on its learning management system (LMS), in one undergraduate program of
about 1,200 students, a scenario assumption like every figure about the university; the university holds LMS, enrolment
and result records but has not decided which questions they should answer. Withdrawal is also a regulated measure of
quality: Circular 01/2024 caps the annual withdrawal rate at 10% and the first-year rate at 15% [1], and Circular
83/2026 keeps these thresholds from 15 November 2026 [2]. The problem is that students who stop taking part are
noticed late: in the simulated stakeholder interviews of Milestone 1, lecturers become sure around week 5 or 6, the
program manager sees counts only after the semester, and the support office meets most of these students on the
academic warning list at its end, while the window for effective support of a student at risk is between two and four
weeks [3]. Following the course brief [4], the project traces one analytics chain from
the stakeholders' needs to the data, their preparation, the choice of methods, the evaluation design and the educational
decisions they support. Appendix I gives the setting, the published studies and the stakeholder detail.
