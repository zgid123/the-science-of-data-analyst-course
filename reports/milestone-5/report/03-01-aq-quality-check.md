## 3.1. Quality Check of the Final Questions

The final questions of Table 3.1 carry every decision of the project, so each must be precise enough to be settled by
data. The course brief requires every analytical question to be clear, specific, scoped, data-oriented and
answerable, and we apply these criteria to the final wording with the following meanings. A question is clear when it
asks one thing that a reader of the question can restate; specific when it names its measure or target and the groups
it compares; scoped when it fixes its population, courses and period; data-oriented when it names the data it needs;
and answerable when those data can settle it.

**The check.** Table 3.2 applies the five criteria to each final question. The scope and data cells use OULAD terms:
a module run is one offering of a module, the day-14 population is the set of enrolments still registered on day 14
(the KPI proxy of Section 2.1), and a tutor-marked assignment (TMA) and a computer-marked assessment (CMA) are the two
kinds of assessed task that fall due during a run.

**Table 3.2. Quality check of the final questions**

| Criterion | AQ1 | AQ2 | AQ3 | AQ4 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Clear | Yes: how enrolments ended, and when non-completers first missed a task | Yes: which early signs go with later non-completion | Yes: how well non-completion can be estimated early, against a simple rule | Yes: which enrolments to rank first on a list of fixed length |
| Specific | Yes: shares of pass, fail and withdrawal with 95% intervals; week of the first missed TMA or CMA; groups by module, run, start month and year | Yes: named signs (missed and late tasks, inactivity, activity against the run's median); adjusted difference in later non-completion, in percentage points; comparison within module | Yes: one target (non-completion); normalised recall at capacity against the early-sign rule; two cut-offs | Yes: lift and normalised recall at list length; groups by deprivation band and disability |
| Scoped | Yes: OULAD module runs; day-14 population; computed on the 9 runs of 2013, year comparison from published 2014 counts | Yes: enrolments registered at day 27; signs dated up to day 27; 9 runs of 2013, then the 13 runs of 2014 after the AQ3 test | Yes: cut-offs at day 13 and day 27; development on the 9 runs of 2013; one test on the 13 runs of 2014 | Yes: per run and cut-off; lists of 3 or 5 per 20 students; 2014 test runs only |
| Data-oriented | Yes: final result, registration and unregistration days, task due and submission days | Yes: daily clicks, task due and submission days, previous attempts and credits | Yes: day-13 and day-27 feature views built only from events dated up to the cut-off | Yes: AQ3 scores, list lengths per run, deprivation band and disability (for the audit only) |

**What the check shows.** All four questions are clear, specific, scoped and data-oriented in their final wording.
Answerability differs by question and is reported honestly in the final status of Table 3.1: AQ1 and AQ2 are
answerable and answered for the 2013 runs, AQ3 is answerable by a design that has not yet been run, and AQ4 is
answerable only in its ranking part. The one criterion that no data in OULAD can meet is the contact part of AQ4, which
is why that part is deferred rather than answered with assumptions.

**How refinement made the questions checkable.** The questions became checkable through two rounds of refinement. In
Milestone 1 the validation round and an AI reviewer's critiques, numbered R1 to R12, sharpened the first drafts; two
examples show the effect. The first draft of AQ2, "What causes students to stop taking part in online courses?",
claimed an effect that records of observed behaviour cannot show and named no factor to compare; the question was
narrowed to named signs and a difference in later non-completion. The first draft of AQ3, "Can we predict which
students will drop out?", had no target, no unit, no horizon and no data cut-off, and mixed leaving the program with
not completing one course; the question gained all four and a simple rule as the standard to beat. In Milestones 2 and
3 the real data then narrowed how each question is measured, which Section 3.2 records question by question in the AQ
Revision Log.
