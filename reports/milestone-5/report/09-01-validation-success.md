## 9.1. Validation, Metrics and Success Criteria

The Evaluation Plan of Section 9 names one primary metric and one success criterion per question; the validation behind each and the reasons for the thresholds follow, question by question.

**AQ1.** The evaluation of the descriptive question checks that every rate is counted correctly on the right population. The primary metric is the non-completion rate and the outcome shares per module, run, start month and year, each with a 95% interval for proportions, and the secondary metric is the cumulative share of enrolments with a first missed task by week, compared by outcome. Validation compares two independent aggregation paths, one from the prepared view and one from the raw tables with the day-14 rule recomputed, which must agree exactly, and checks each denominator against its known total. The baselines are the pooled rate, the rate over all enrolments and the rate without the enrolments flagged for an unknown withdrawal or registration day. On the 9 runs of 2013 the denominators are 13,529 enrolments in all and 12,076 in the day-14 population, and the flagged enrolments number 21, of which 8 are in the day-14 population. The error analysis looks for runs whose rate moves by more than its interval between the two denominators, read as the all-enrolment rate lying outside the 95% interval of the day-14 rate, and for runs with no task due by day 27. Rates by gender, disability, deprivation band and age band are reported as description only. The success criterion is that both aggregation paths agree to the enrolment, every rate carries its denominator and interval, and no rate is compared across runs without its interval; Section 11.2 reports the check with the results.

**AQ2.** The evaluation of the diagnostic question judges the size and consistency of each association. The primary metric is the average risk difference of each sign within module run, in percentage points with a 95% interval, with the odds ratio beside it; the secondary metrics are the unadjusted rate difference and the module-specific risk differences from the sign × module terms. The estimation uses the 9 runs of 2013, and the task-based signs only the runs with a task due by day 27. The error analysis looks at binned residuals by run and activity level and at the correlation among the click-based signs, and four planned sensitivity runs test the estimates against de-duplicated clicks, the rule variant that also counts late CMAs, the flagged enrolments and dropping the runs with no task due. A sign counts as a consistent association when its adjusted risk difference is at least 5 percentage points with an interval excluding 0 and has the same direction in at least two-thirds of the modules where it is defined; otherwise it is weak or inconsistent. Five points is the smallest gap we treat as worth the attention of the staff who make contact. It has the same size as the target gain of the SMART Goal, but the two lie on different scales, a gap between students with and without a sign against a gain in a course's completion rate, so the equality is a convention fixed in Milestone 4, not a derivation. The criterion is also lenient: at a base rate near 44% a behavioural sign of disengagement can clear five points easily, so the verdict screens out weak signs, while the size of each estimate, not the verdict, distinguishes the signs (Section 11.3). P-values are reported but take no part in the verdict, because a p-value does not measure the size of an effect or the importance of a result [S903], and conclusions should not rest only on whether a p-value passes a threshold [S903]. No association is stated as a cause. The replication on the 13 runs of 2014, including module CCC, waits until the AQ3 model is frozen and scored (Change Log C14), because looking at 2014 outcomes while the AQ3 predictors are chosen would leak test information.

**AQ3: metrics.** The primary metric of the proposed model would be normalised recall at capacity, computed per run with the run's own list and pooled over runs: the non-completers listed in all runs divided by the most the lists could hold, at day 27 with 5 contacts per 20. The secondary metrics would be recall, precision and lift at capacity, which re-express the same listed non-completers; the unweighted mean of the per-run differences between model and rule; the areas under the ROC and precision-recall curves, computed within run and averaged; calibration-in-the-large and the calibration slope; the Brier score, the mean squared difference between predicted risk and outcome; and net benefit as context only, since it describes a threshold policy rather than a fixed capacity. The same measures would be reported at day 13 and at 3 per 20. Calibration is included because good discrimination does not guarantee reliable risk estimates [S902]; a well-calibrated model has a calibration slope of 1 and an intercept of 0 [S902].

**AQ3 and AQ4: success criteria.** The criteria are fixed before any result exists, and Table 9.2 gives them with their role.

**Table 9.2. Success criteria of the proposed model and ranking**

| Criterion | Definition | Role |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| S1, single gate | On all 13 test runs, all eligible enrolments, day 27, k = 5 per 20: the primary model's pooled normalised recall would have to exceed the rule-ranked list's, with the 95% paired two-level bootstrap interval of the difference above 0 and a one-sided sign-flip permutation test on the 13 per-run differences with p below 0.05; wins, ties and losses counted apart | Would decide whether the model is better than the rule; it sets no minimum size of gain, so a pass would be reported with the number of extra non-completers listed beside it |
| S2, precision at capacity | Share of the list who do not complete | Reported, not a gate |
| S3, calibration | Calibration-in-the-large between −0.2 and 0.2 and slope between 0.8 and 1.2 on the pooled estimates | Condition for showing probabilities; otherwise staff see ranks and reasons only |
| AQ4 list | Gate S1 would have to be met by the list, and the fairness rule FR1 pass or its flags be reviewed before use (Section 9.2) | Condition for using the list |
| Sensitivity | All other populations, cut-offs and capacities | Reported; cannot change the verdict |

Calibration cannot be a success criterion on its own, and a failed calibration check limits the output to ranks and reasons; it never leads to recalibration on the 2014 runs, which would use the test outcomes. For AQ4, the secondary metrics would be lift over the run's base rate, the overlap of the model list with the rule list, the listed non-completers split into Fail and Withdrawn, and recall at 3 and at 5 per 20; the assumption that the order of risk matches the order of need is stated as untested, and no statement is made about the effect of contact.

**AQ3 and AQ4: validation design.** Evaluation data should be distinct from the data used to train the model, tune it or select it [S901], and the design meets this with time-ordered runs. The 13 test runs of 2014 share no run with development. The 1,586 test enrolments at day 27 of students already seen in 2013 stay in the main test, as they would in use on a later run, and are removed in a sensitivity analysis that meets the guideline's stricter rule of no overlap in participants [S901]. Development uses leave-one-run-out folds on the 9 runs of 2013: each run forms its own fold, students who also appear in the held-out run are removed from that fold's training data, and every step that learns from data, including filling, scaling and tuning, is repeated inside each fold.

A challenger, the gradient-boosted trees or the extended predictor set, replaces the primary model only with a gain of at least 0.03 in pooled normalised recall, about 89 more non-completers listed over the 9 runs, together with a gain in at least 7 of the 9 folds. The margin is fixed before any fold result exists, so that a small chance improvement cannot change the choice. The protocol has two freezes and one test: the question, splits, metrics and baselines are fixed before training, and the final model is fixed, with access to the test data recorded, before testing. The chosen model would then be refitted on all 2013 runs and scored once on the 13 runs of 2014.

The test covers all 13 runs of 2014 (Change Log C16): the 9 paired runs, 10,801 enrolments at day 27, test prediction for a later run of the same module and start month, and the 4 unpaired runs, 4,923 enrolments, test transfer to a module or start month not seen in training; both groups are also reported apart as sensitivity analyses. Two features of the run structure need their own handling. Module CCC has no run in 2013, so module terms are coded as deviations from the average module and an unseen module receives the average. In addition, a university would not yet know the outcomes of the October 2013 runs when the February 2014 runs reach their cut-offs, so a labels-available variant scores the 2014 February runs with a model trained only on the 2013 February runs. Uncertainty about the gate comes from a paired two-level bootstrap, which resamples runs and then enrolments within each sampled run, together with a sign-flip permutation test on the 13 per-run differences. Section 9.2 adds the checks that protect this design against leakage and unfair errors.
