## Appendix IX – Evaluation Design in Detail

This appendix supports Section 9. It gives the validation of each question, the full list of metrics, the success criteria of the proposed model and ranking, the validation design with its handling of the run structure, the fairness audit groups and rule, and the planned error analysis and interpretability checks.

**One design, two kinds of question.** The evaluation design was fixed in Milestone 4 before any code ran, which rules out choosing metrics or thresholds after the results are seen. The purpose of each question decides what its evaluation measures. AQ1 describes completed runs, so its evaluation checks counts and denominators rather than accuracy. AQ2 measures association, which is judged by the size and consistency of each estimate and never read as cause. AQ3 and AQ4 are predictions, and a predictive claim needs its own out-of-sample evaluation, because a model that explains the data it was fitted on may still predict new cases poorly [10]. For the predictive questions we follow the TRIPOD+AI reporting guideline for prediction models, which frames evaluation as discrimination, calibration and usefulness for the decision [11]; each measure below is stated with its reason.

**Capacity and the main operating point.** The decision D1 acts on a list whose length is set by staff capacity, not by a probability threshold. The capacity k of a run is 3 or 5 contacts per group of 20 students, a scenario value (Section 2.1), that is 15% or 25% of the enrolments registered at the cut-off. The main operating point is 5 per 20 at day 27, because it serves the list delivered in week 5 and is close to the share of students the early-sign rule flags at day 27: 28.8% of the 27,535 enrolments registered then across all 22 runs, a share computed without any outcome. At a fixed list length, the cells of the confusion matrix of listed and unlisted students against their outcome give recall, precision and lift, which replace the F1 score, because the list length is set by capacity rather than chosen to balance the two kinds of error.

**Metric definitions.** For a run with n enrolments, its set of non-completers and a list of the k highest scores, recall at capacity is the share of the run's non-completers who are on the list. Attainable recall is the most a list of length k can reach, which is below 1 when the run has more non-completers than places on the list. Normalised recall divides recall by that ceiling, so that 1 means the best list possible. Precision is the share of the list who do not complete, and lift divides precision by the run's base rate of non-completion, so that 1 means no better than a random list. Normalised recall is needed because the ceiling varies widely: in the training runs, a list of 5 per 20 at day 27 can hold between 46% and 100% of a run's non-completers, so raw recall would compare runs on different scales.

**AQ1.** The evaluation of the descriptive question checks that every rate is counted correctly on the right population. The primary metric is the non-completion rate and the outcome shares per module, run, start month and year, each with a 95% interval for proportions, and the secondary metric is the cumulative share of enrolments with a first missed task by week, compared by outcome. Validation compares two independent aggregation paths, one from the prepared view and one from the raw tables with the day-14 rule recomputed, which must agree exactly, and checks each denominator against its known total. The baselines are the pooled rate, the rate over all enrolments and the rate without the enrolments flagged for an unknown withdrawal or registration day. On the 9 runs of 2013 the denominators are 13,529 enrolments in all and 12,076 in the day-14 population, and the flagged enrolments number 21, of which 8 are in the day-14 population. The error analysis looks for runs whose rate moves by more than its interval between the two denominators, read as the all-enrolment rate lying outside the 95% interval of the day-14 rate, and for runs with no task due by day 27. Rates by gender, disability, deprivation band and age band are reported as description only. The success criterion is that both aggregation paths agree to the enrolment, every rate carries its denominator and interval, and no rate is compared across runs without its interval; Section 11.2 reports the check with the results.

**AQ2.** The evaluation of the diagnostic question judges the size and consistency of each association. The primary metric is the average risk difference of each sign within module run, in percentage points with a 95% interval, with the odds ratio beside it; the secondary metrics are the unadjusted rate difference and the module-specific risk differences from the sign × module terms. The estimation uses the 9 runs of 2013, and the task-based signs only the runs with a task due by day 27. The error analysis looks at binned residuals by run and activity level and at the correlation among the click-based signs, and four planned sensitivity runs test the estimates against de-duplicated clicks, the rule variant that also counts late CMAs, the flagged enrolments and dropping the runs with no task due. A sign counts as a consistent association when its adjusted risk difference is at least 5 percentage points with an interval excluding 0 and has the same direction in at least two-thirds of the modules where it is defined; otherwise it is weak or inconsistent. Five points is the smallest gap we treat as worth the attention of the staff who make contact. It has the same size as the target gain of the SMART Goal, but the two lie on different scales, a gap between students with and without a sign against a gain in a course's completion rate, so the equality is a convention fixed in Milestone 4, not a derivation. The criterion is also lenient: at a base rate near 44% a behavioural sign of disengagement can clear five points easily, so the verdict screens out weak signs, while the size of each estimate, not the verdict, distinguishes the signs (Section 11.3). P-values are reported but take no part in the verdict, because a p-value does not measure the size of an effect or the importance of a result [12], and conclusions should not rest only on whether a p-value passes a threshold [12]. No association is stated as a cause. The replication on the 13 runs of 2014, including module CCC, waits until the AQ3 model is frozen and scored (Change Log C14), because looking at 2014 outcomes while the AQ3 predictors are chosen would leak test information.

**AQ3: metrics.** The primary metric of the proposed model would be normalised recall at capacity, computed per run with the run's own list and pooled over runs: the non-completers listed in all runs divided by the most the lists could hold, at day 27 with 5 contacts per 20. The secondary metrics would be recall, precision and lift at capacity, which re-express the same listed non-completers; the unweighted mean of the per-run differences between model and rule; the areas under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and precision-recall curves, computed within run and averaged; calibration-in-the-large and the calibration slope; the Brier score, the mean squared difference between predicted risk and outcome; and net benefit as context only, since it describes a threshold policy rather than a fixed capacity. The same measures would be reported at day 13 and at 3 per 20. Calibration is included because good discrimination does not guarantee reliable risk estimates [13]; a well-calibrated model has a calibration slope of 1 and an intercept of 0 [13].

**AQ3 and AQ4: success criteria.** The criteria are fixed before any result exists, and Table A.23 gives them with their role.

**Table A.23. Success criteria of the proposed model and ranking**

| Criterion | Definition | Role |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| S1, single gate | On all 13 test runs, all eligible enrolments, day 27, k = 5 per 20: the primary model's pooled normalised recall would have to exceed the rule-ranked list's, with the 95% paired two-level bootstrap interval of the difference above 0 and a one-sided sign-flip permutation test on the 13 per-run differences with p below 0.05; wins, ties and losses counted apart | Would decide whether the model is better than the rule; it sets no minimum size of gain, so a pass would be reported with the number of extra non-completers listed beside it |
| S2, precision at capacity | Share of the list who do not complete | Reported, not a gate |
| S3, calibration | Calibration-in-the-large between −0.2 and 0.2 and slope between 0.8 and 1.2 on the pooled estimates | Condition for showing probabilities; otherwise staff see ranks and reasons only |
| AQ4 list | Gate S1 would have to be met by the list, and the fairness rule FR1 pass or its flags be reviewed before use (Section 9.2) | Condition for using the list |
| Sensitivity | All other populations, cut-offs and capacities | Reported; cannot change the verdict |

Calibration cannot be a success criterion on its own, and a failed calibration check limits the output to ranks and reasons; it never leads to recalibration on the 2014 runs, which would use the test outcomes. For AQ4, the secondary metrics would be lift over the run's base rate, the overlap of the model list with the rule list, the listed non-completers split into Fail and Withdrawn, and recall at 3 and at 5 per 20; the assumption that the order of risk matches the order of need is stated as untested, and no statement is made about the effect of contact.

**AQ3 and AQ4: validation design.** Evaluation data should be distinct from the data used to train the model, tune it or select it [11], and the design meets this with time-ordered runs. The 13 test runs of 2014 share no run with development. The 1,586 test enrolments at day 27 of students already seen in 2013 stay in the main test, as they would in use on a later run, and are removed in a sensitivity analysis that meets the guideline's stricter rule of no overlap in participants [11]. Development uses leave-one-run-out folds on the 9 runs of 2013: each run forms its own fold, students who also appear in the held-out run are removed from that fold's training data, and every step that learns from data, including filling, scaling and tuning, is repeated inside each fold.

A challenger, the gradient-boosted trees or the extended predictor set, replaces the primary model only with a gain of at least 0.03 in pooled normalised recall, about 89 more non-completers listed over the 9 runs, together with a gain in at least 7 of the 9 folds. The margin is fixed before any fold result exists, so that a small chance improvement cannot change the choice. The protocol has two freezes and one test: the question, splits, metrics and baselines are fixed before training, and the final model is fixed, with access to the test data recorded, before testing. The chosen model would then be refitted on all 2013 runs and scored once on the 13 runs of 2014.

The test covers all 13 runs of 2014 (Change Log C16): the 9 paired runs, 10,801 enrolments at day 27, test prediction for a later run of the same module and start month, and the 4 unpaired runs, 4,923 enrolments, test transfer to a module or start month not seen in training; both groups are also reported apart as sensitivity analyses. Two features of the run structure need their own handling. Module CCC has no run in 2013, so module terms are coded as deviations from the average module and an unseen module receives the average. In addition, a university would not yet know the outcomes of the October 2013 runs when the February 2014 runs reach their cut-offs, so a labels-available variant scores the 2014 February runs with a model trained only on the 2013 February runs. Uncertainty about the gate comes from a paired two-level bootstrap, which resamples runs and then enrolments within each sampled run, together with a sign-flip permutation test on the 13 per-run differences. Section 9.2 adds the checks that protect this design against leakage and unfair errors.

**Leakage in the evaluation.** The data-side controls of Section 7.1 keep every feature dated up to its cut-off and every learned parameter on the training runs; the evaluation adds three controls of its own. The 2014 outcomes stay unopened until the single logged test, and the analyses that could otherwise look at them wait: the AQ2 replication on 2014 follows the AQ3 test (C14), and AQ1 is computed on 2013 with only the published 2014 outcome counts (C15). Every learned step, including filling, scaling and tuning, is refitted inside each development fold, so that no held-out run informs its own prediction. And fold results serve only the choice between candidates and are never reported as performance, because a score that guided a choice is optimistic about the chosen model. Each predictor must also be available at the moment of use, which the timing rule of Section 7.1 guarantees for every predictor of the primary and extended sets.

**Fairness: what is audited and why.** Gender, disability, deprivation band and age band stay out of the predictors and serve only the audit, because leaving them out does not make a model blind to them: activity, previous attempts or credits can carry the same information. An audit is possible only because group membership is kept in the data [6]. TRIPOD+AI asks for model performance with confidence intervals for key subgroups, including sociodemographic ones [11], and the need is concrete on OULAD, where a fairness study of at-risk prediction reproduced a disparity of five to six percentage points in favour of male students [7]. The frozen model would therefore be evaluated separately for each audit group, because aggregate metrics can hide differences between groups.

**One primary criterion.** Fairness criteria cannot all hold at once when non-completion rates differ between groups, so the audit names one primary criterion, separation, measured as equal recall at capacity across groups. Separation matches the decision: the main harm of a contact list is missing a student who struggles, and equal recall means that a struggling student has the same chance of being listed whatever the group.

**The audit groups.** The confirmatory check covers the three groups that AQ4 names and that hold at least 400 test enrolments at day 27. Table A.24 gives the groups and how each is handled.

**Table A.24. Fairness audit groups of the proposed ranking**

| Group | Test enrolments at day 27 | Handling |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Declared disability | 1,488 | Confirmatory check, rule FR1 |
| Most deprived band, 0–10% | 1,486 | Confirmatory check, rule FR1 |
| Missing deprivation band | 610 | Confirmatory check, rule FR1; reported as its own group |
| Aged 55 or over | 118 | Point gap only, marked "insufficient data, not cleared" |
| Gender, other age and deprivation bands | – | Descriptive checks |

The fairness rule FR1 flags a confirmatory group for review when its recall at capacity is more than 10 percentage points below that of the other students and the one-sided 95% bootstrap interval of the gap, Holm-adjusted over the three groups, lies below 0. The ten-point gap carries forward the fairness limit set in Milestone 1, which kept first-year recall within ten points of later-year recall; since OULAD records no year of study, the limit moved to the groups the data do record, deprivation band and disability (Section 3.2). A flag calls for human review of why the gap arises rather than rejecting the model, because the right response depends on the cause, which the metric alone cannot show. Students aged 55 or over, with 118 test enrolments, fall below the size at which the gap can be estimated reliably, so their gap is reported but not treated as cleared. For the same reason, a confirmatory group without a flag would show no evidence of a gap at the precision its size allows, not that the list is fair to it: recall is computed among the group's non-completers only, and the Holm adjustment raises the bar further.

The descriptive checks complete the audit: selection rate, precision and calibration by group; a proxy check that compares scores and predictors across groups, to show which predictors carry group information; and the absolute between-ROC area (ABROCA), the area between a group's ROC curve and that of the other students, which stays descriptive because it measures differences across all thresholds rather than at the capacity where the decision is made.

**Error analysis.** The errors of the proposed model would be examined by slice: missed non-completers and listed completers by run, module, activity level, previous attempts, late registration and kind of non-completion, Fail or Withdrawn. Recall would also be computed among active students only, and among reachable non-completers, those not withdrawn within seven days after the cut-off, because a list that catches only students who have already left helps no one. The stability of list membership near rank k would be measured under the bootstrap, and the CCC runs, from the module with no 2013 run, would be reported apart. For AQ4, the error analysis separates runs where the list fills its capacity with completers, and students with no activity at all, whom every list catches, from active students at risk.

**Interpretability.** Interpretability is built into the choice of model rather than added afterwards. Each student's reasons on the list come from the same logistic model that ranks the student, so the reasons shown are the reasons used. No coefficient is read as a cause: a sign that raises a student's rank is a reason to look, not an explanation of why the student struggles. For AQ2, interpretation is limited in the same way: its risk differences describe how much more often students with a sign fail to complete within the same run, which supports where staff look but not what would change the outcome. The final model would be documented in a model card and its data in a datasheet [9], so that its intended use, its population and its limits travel with it. Section 10 states what of this design has been implemented.
