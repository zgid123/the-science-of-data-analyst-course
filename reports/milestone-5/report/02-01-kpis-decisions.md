## 2.1. Key Performance Indicators and Decisions

Table 2.1 gives the goal with one primary key performance indicator (KPI), which measures it, and two secondary KPIs,
which show during the semester whether contact works. On OULAD the primary KPI has a proxy: an enrolment counts when it
is still registered at day 14, because the registration window closes two weeks after a run starts (Change Log entries C03 and C09, Table A.8).
Secondary KPI 1 rests on the early-sign rule (C10), the one rule of the project on OULAD and in the pilot (C17): by the
data cut-off, a tutor-marked assignment (TMA) or computer-marked assessment (CMA) due but not submitted, a TMA submitted
late, or no platform activity in the previous seven days. Both secondary KPIs need a contact record, so their contact
parts are measured only in the pilot (Appendix II).

**Table 2.1. Final SMART Goal and KPIs**

| Element | Final definition | When and from where | Status on OULAD |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| SMART Goal | Completion after add-drop in the pilot courses up five points from the recounted baseline (assumed 70% to 75%) by the end of 2027–2028 | Pilot courses, 2027–2028 | Measured only in the pilot |
| Primary KPI: completion rate | Passes over enrolments after add-drop; leave and transfer leave the denominator | End of each semester; academic records | Proxy at day 14: 28,061 enrolments (22 runs), 12,076 (2013) |
| Secondary KPI 1: early-sign contact rate | Students meeting the early-sign rule at the end of week 4 contacted by the end of week 5, over students meeting it | Week 6; LMS, academic and contact records | Sign part only: rule flags 28.8% of 27,535 (22 runs) at day 27 |
| Secondary KPI 2: follow-up rate | Listed students contacted within seven days, over students listed | A week after each review point; contact record | Measured only in the pilot |

The goal serves three decisions (Table A.3, Appendix II). D1, whom to contact at each review point, is operational and
reversible, so it can start with the early-sign rule checked by staff; D2, where the program manager places support
time, needs a pattern that repeats over semesters; and D3, whether to extend the approach and link data routinely, is
close to irreversible and needs pilot results and legal approval.
