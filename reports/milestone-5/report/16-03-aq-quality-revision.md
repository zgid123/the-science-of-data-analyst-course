## Appendix III – Analytical Questions: Quality Check, Revision Log and Change Log

This appendix supports Section 3. It gives how the four questions connect, the quality check of the final wording with the reason in each cell, the AQ Revision Log with every cell written out, the mapping of the milestones' status scales onto the final scales, and the Controlled Change Log C01–C17.

**How the questions connect.** AQ1 shows which courses and runs lose most students and in which week non-completers
first fall behind, which tells AQ2 where to look. AQ2 compares the early signs named in its wording and asks which go
with later non-completion rather than which cause it, because records of what students did cannot show why they did
it. AQ3 tests whether those signs and the other early records identify students in time, against the early-sign rule
as the simple standard to beat. AQ4 turns the AQ3 ordering into a list of the length that staff capacity allows. All
four questions take the same unit, one student's enrolment in one module run, so that a student who struggles in one
course and not in another is seen correctly.

**The levels and how each answer may be read.** A descriptive question states what happened; a diagnostic question
states an association or a pattern, and a cause only when the design allows causal inference; a predictive question
states how well an outcome can be foreseen on a proper validation, without implying a cause; and a prescriptive
question, when only observational data exist, yields a decision option to be tested rather than a proven
intervention. AQ2 is therefore labelled "Diagnostic (association only)". AQ4 is labelled "Prescriptive (ranking under
capacity)": it still recommends whom to list first, but the choice of contact, and whether contact helps, has no data
in OULAD or in any dataset screened, and waits for the pilot.

**Two definitions used by every question.** The early-sign rule (C10) flags a student who, by the cut-off, has a task
due but not submitted, a TMA submitted late, or no activity on the learning platform in the previous seven days; it is
both the basis of secondary KPI 1 and the comparison that AQ3 must beat. A list is judged by its recall, the share of
later non-completers it reaches, against the most a list of its length can reach (normalised recall), and by its gain
over the run's base rate (lift), the share of non-completers on the list divided by the share in the whole run (C13).
These two measures replace the precision limit of 50% set in Milestone 1, because non-completion is 45.3% at day 13 and
44.2% at day 27 across the 22 runs, and 24.9–58.3% by run in the day-14 population, so a random list already reaches about 45% precision.

**What the final status means.** A question is Answered when real results answer all of it on its stated population;
Partial when real results answer its core while a named part waits for data or for a later step; Deferred when the
design is complete but no result exists yet; and Dropped when it is abandoned. AQ1 is Partial because it is answered
for the 2013 runs, while the 2014 rates on the day-14 population and the 2014 week of the first missed task, including those of module CCC, which ran only in 2014, wait until the AQ3 test has opened the 2014 outcomes. AQ2 is Partial because all five signs tested, the four named in its wording and the activity before the start that the AQ2 Modeling Brief of Milestone 4 adds as an input (Section 8.1), meet the criterion of a
consistent association on the 2013 runs, while the replication on 2014 waits for the same reason. AQ3 is Deferred
because no model has been trained and the 2014 test runs stay unopened, and AQ4 is Deferred because its ranking needs
the AQ3 scores and its contact part has no data. Deferring AQ3 and AQ4 is our choice of scope: the brief makes the Level 2 experiment optional, and we keep the single-use 2014 test for one complete run of the frozen protocol, so that no 2014 result is seen before the model is frozen (Section 10). No question is dropped. Section 3.1 checks the final wording against
the five quality criteria of the brief, Section 3.2 traces each question from its first wording, and Section 11
reports the answers.

**What the check shows.** All four questions are clear, specific, scoped and data-oriented in their final wording.
Answerability differs by question, as the final status of Table 3.1 records: AQ1 and AQ2 are
answerable and answered for the 2013 runs, AQ3 is answerable by a design that has not yet been run, and AQ4 is
answerable only in its ranking part. The one part that no data in OULAD can answer is the contact part of AQ4, which
is why that part is deferred rather than answered with assumptions.

**How refinement made the questions checkable.** The questions became checkable through two rounds of refinement. In
Milestone 1 the validation round and an AI reviewer's critiques, numbered R1 to R12, sharpened the first drafts; two
examples show the effect. The first draft of AQ2, "What causes students to stop taking part in online courses?",
claimed an effect that records of observed behaviour cannot show and named no factor to compare; the question was
narrowed to named signs and a difference in later non-completion. The first draft of AQ3, "Can we predict which
students will drop out?", had no target, no unit, no horizon and no data cut-off, and mixed leaving the program with
not completing one course; the question gained all four and a simple rule as the standard to beat. In Milestones 2 and
3 the real data then narrowed how each question is measured, which Section 3.2 records question by question in the AQ
Revision Log.

**The changes that touch every question.** Four entries of Milestone 2 (C01–C04) apply to all questions. Because no open dataset
of Vietnamese university courses exists, OULAD stands in for the university's courses, while the university's records
remain the target of the pilot (C01). A semester becomes a module run counted in days, with the cut-offs of the week-3 and week-5 lists on day 13 and day 27 (C02). The add-drop period maps onto the first 14 days of a run, when the Open University's registration window closes, so the KPI population counts the enrolments still registered on day 14 (C03). Comparisons by delivery mode and year of study, which OULAD does not
record, are replaced by comparisons by module and run, and the fairness limit of AQ4 moves to deprivation band and
disability (C04). Milestone 3 then fixed one population rule for every question: an enrolment counts on a given day
when the student registered by that day, or the registration day is unknown, and had not unregistered by it (C09); this refines C03 by also removing the 58 late registrants.

The fit, readiness and entry ratings and the revision action describe a question and its data before analysis, while the final status describes the results available now. A question rated Ready can therefore be Deferred when its design is complete and its result waits for a named step, as AQ3 and AQ4 do.

**Three entries recorded in the final integration.** Integrating the four milestones brought three changes to the
Change Log, each with its reason. First, AQ1 is computed on the 9 runs of 2013 (13,529 enrolments, of which 12,076 were
registered at day 14), and its year comparison uses the 2014 outcome counts per run that Milestone 3 published (19,064
enrolments, all-enrolment denominator), because relating 2014 outcomes to task and activity measures before the AQ3
model is frozen would open the test outcomes, the same leak that C14 prevents for AQ2 (C15). Second, the AQ3 test set
moved in Milestones 3 and 4 from the 2014 runs of the 9 paired modules and start months to all 13 runs of 2014 (15,724
enrolments at day 27), so that the test also covers a module and start months the model has not seen; the 9 paired
runs (10,801 enrolments) and the 4 unpaired runs (4,923) are reported apart, and the entry records a change that had
been made without one (C16). Third, one early-sign rule serves the project on OULAD and in the pilot, so that secondary
KPI 1 counts the same students as the rule the AQ3 model must beat (C17).

**What did not change.** No question was dropped: every question keeps a core that the data answer, and only the
contact part of AQ4 is deferred, to a pilot in which contacts and their outcomes are logged. The fairness limit of
Milestone 1, first-year recall within ten points of later-year recall, was not lost when year of study proved absent:
Milestone 4 restored the ten-point gap as review rule FR1 on the groups OULAD records, added the condition that the
one-sided 95% interval of the gap, adjusted for several groups, lies below zero, and turned the limit from a
constraint on the list into a flag for human review.
