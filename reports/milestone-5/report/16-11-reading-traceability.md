## Appendix XI – Reading the Findings and Detailed Traceability

This appendix supports Sections 11 and 11.1. It gives how each answer is read by its analytics level, the rule behind each final status, the Final Project Traceability Matrix with every cell written out, and the justification of each final status.

Each answer is read according to its analytics level, because the same number means different things at different levels. A descriptive rate states what happened to a defined population; it does not explain why. A regression fitted to capture how a sign and an outcome move together is itself descriptive of an association, not a test of a cause, and Shmueli (2010) separates this use explicitly from causal explanation and from prediction [10]. As a result, the AQ2 estimates are associations within module runs, never effects of the signs. A predictive claim, in turn, needs its own evaluation on data the model has not seen, because explanatory power does not imply predictive power [10]; for this reason AQ3 carries no performance figure until the single test on the 2014 runs has been run. Finally, a recommendation drawn from observational data is a decision option to test, since questions of effectiveness are ideally answered by a randomised experiment [14]. Statistical significance plays a supporting role only: a p-value does not measure the size or importance of an effect [12], so every finding below is stated as an effect size with its 95% interval and its population.

The four final questions can only support decisions if each one can be followed back to the goal it serves and forward to the evidence that answers it. The Final Project Traceability Matrix gives this chain in one row per final question: the Business Goal and SMART Goal with the KPI the question feeds (Section 2), the final wording and analytics level (Section 3), the data the question requires and the OULAD tables and variables that meet it (Sections 4 and 5), the preparation and feature logic (Section 7), the candidate method with its rationale (Section 8), the evaluation design or result (Sections 9, 11.2 and 11.3), the decision supported (D1–D3, Section 2.1) and the final status. All four rows share one Business Goal: improve successful course completion in the program's online and blended courses by identifying students who stop taking part early enough to support them within the semester. They also share one SMART Goal: raise the completion rate of students enrolled after the add-drop period in the pilot courses from the recounted baseline (assumed 70%) by five percentage points (assumed 75%) by the end of 2027–2028. Where a link has no evidence, the cell reads GAP or N.A. with its reason instead of a filled-in value.

The status of each row follows one rule. A question is Answered when real results answer all of it on its stated population; Partial when real results answer its core while a named part waits for data or for a later step; Deferred when no result exists yet but the design is complete and the answer waits for a named step; and Dropped when the question is abandoned. No question is Dropped, because every change since Milestone 1 narrowed how a question is measured or deferred a part of it, as the AQ Revision Log records (Section 3.2).

Reading the rows across shows where the chain is complete and where it is broken. For AQ1 and AQ2 every link holds evidence, from the KPI to a measured result, and both questions meet the success criteria Milestone 4 fixed for them (Sections 11.2 and 11.3). For AQ3 and AQ4 the chain is complete up to the evaluation design, while the result cell stays N.A. because the predictive prototype has not been run (Section 10). The GAP cells mark data that OULAD does not hold: delivery mode and year of study for AQ1 and AQ2, attendance for AQ2, and contacts, contact types and outcomes after contact for AQ4. In addition, secondary KPI 2 and the contact part of secondary KPI 1 need a contact record that only the pilot can create, so their link to AQ4 is a GAP on OULAD rather than a measured value.
