# 3. Analytical Questions and Analytics Levels

The three decisions of Section 2.1 need evidence of four kinds: what happened, which signs go with it, whether it can
be foreseen, and whom to list first. Four analytical questions (AQ1–AQ4) supply that evidence, one at each analytics
level, and they form one path from understanding the situation to supporting action, each question handing its result
to the next.

**How the questions connect.** AQ1 shows which courses and runs lose most students and in which week non-completers
first fall behind, which tells AQ2 where to look. AQ2 compares the early signs named in its wording and asks which go
with later non-completion rather than which cause it, because records of what students did cannot show why they did
it. AQ3 tests whether those signs and the other early records identify students in time, against the early-sign rule
as the simple standard to beat. AQ4 turns the AQ3 ordering into a list of the length that staff capacity allows. All
four questions take the same unit, one student's enrolment in one module run, so that a student who struggles in one
course and not in another is seen correctly.

**The levels and how each answer may be read.** A descriptive question states what happened; a diagnostic question
states an association or a pattern, and a cause only when the design allows causal inference; a predictive question
states how well an outcome can be foreseen on a proper validation, without implying a cause; and a prescriptive
question, when only observational data exist, yields a decision option to be tested rather than a proven
intervention. AQ2 is therefore labelled "Diagnostic (association only)". AQ4 is labelled "Prescriptive (ranking under
capacity)": it still recommends whom to list first, but the choice of contact, and whether contact helps, has no data
in OULAD or in any dataset screened, and waits for the pilot.

**The final questions.** Table 3.1 gives the four questions in their final wording, with their level, scope, the
decision each supports and their final status. The scope column uses the time frame of OULAD: a semester becomes a
module run, week n spans days 7(n−1) to 7n−1 from the start of the run, and the data cut-offs of the week-3 and week-5
lists fall on day 13 and day 27. The status column uses the scale of the course brief, explained after the table.

**Two definitions used by every question.** The early-sign rule (C10) flags a student who, by the cut-off, has a task
due but not submitted, a TMA submitted late, or no activity on the learning platform in the previous seven days; it is
both the basis of secondary KPI 1 and the comparison that AQ3 must beat. A list is judged by its recall, the share of
later non-completers it reaches, against the most a list of its length can reach (normalised recall), and by its gain
over the run's base rate (lift), the share of non-completers on the list divided by the share in the whole run (C13).
These two measures replace the precision limit of 50% set in Milestone 1, because non-completion is 45.3% at day 13 and
44.2% at day 27 across the 22 runs, and 24.9–58.3% by run in the day-14 population, so a random list already reaches about 45% precision.

**What the final status means.** A question is Answered when real results answer all of it on its stated population;
Partial when real results answer its core while a named part waits for data or for a later step; Deferred when the
design is complete but no result exists yet; and Dropped when it is abandoned. AQ1 is Partial because it is answered
for the 2013 runs, while the 2014 rates on the day-14 population and the 2014 week of the first missed task, including those of module CCC, which ran only in 2014, wait until the AQ3 test has opened the 2014 outcomes. AQ2 is Partial because all five signs tested, the four named in its wording and the activity before the start that the AQ2 Modeling Brief of Milestone 4 adds as an input (Section 8.1), meet the criterion of a
consistent association on the 2013 runs, while the replication on 2014 waits for the same reason. AQ3 is Deferred
because no model has been trained and the 2014 test runs stay unopened, and AQ4 is Deferred because its ranking needs
the AQ3 scores and its contact part has no data. Deferring AQ3 and AQ4 is our choice of scope: the brief makes the Level 2 experiment optional, and we keep the single-use 2014 test for one complete run of the frozen protocol, so that no 2014 result is seen before the model is frozen (Section 10).
