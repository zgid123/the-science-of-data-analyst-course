# 14. Conclusion

The project set out to improve successful course completion in the program's online and blended courses by identifying
students who stop taking part early enough to support them within the semester, with a SMART Goal of five more
percentage points of completion in the pilot courses by the end of 2027–2028 (Section 2). From this goal came four
final questions, from description (AQ1) and association (AQ2) to prediction (AQ3) and a ranking under capacity (AQ4),
each tied to the decisions D1–D3 (Sections 3 and 11.1).

The data could support part of this ambition. OULAD stands in for the university's records, with a dated final result,
dated tasks and daily platform activity for every enrolment, but no delivery mode, year of study, attendance, staff
capacity or contact record (Sections 4 and 5); its prepared version dates every sign on or before its cut-off and keeps
the 2014 runs apart as a test set (Section 7). Grouped rates answer AQ1: on the 9 runs of 2013, 45.7% of the enrolments
still registered at day 14 did not complete, rates varied strongly between runs, and most non-completers first missed a
task after week 4 (Section 11.2). An association model answers AQ2: all five early signs go with later non-completion,
a missed task by 40.8 points, in the same direction across modules (Section 11.3). For AQ3 and AQ4, a penalised logistic
model judged once against the early-sign rule and a capacity-limited list audited for fairness are the appropriate
methods, and their outputs remain expected rather than measured (Section 11.4).

What can be concluded is therefore bounded by the level of each answer. On OULAD, an early list cannot rest on missed tasks alone,
and the signs can serve as reasons that staff check beside each name. What cannot be concluded is that these signs cause
non-completion, that a model ranks students better than the rule, or that contacting a listed student raises
completion: a regression capturing associations is descriptive rather than causal [10], and questions of
effectiveness need a randomised experiment [14]. The next step is the single AQ3 test under the frozen protocol, then
a recount on the university's own records, a risk classification of the list and a randomised pilot with contacts and
outcomes logged, which answers what no open dataset can: whether, and for whom, early contact improves completion
(Section 12.1).
