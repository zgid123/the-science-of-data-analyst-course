# Abstract

Students who stop taking part in an online course are noticed late. We address this for a Vietnamese university, where
lecturers, lecturer-advisors and the support office decide whom to contact at each review point (D1), the program manager where to place support
time (D2) and leadership whether to extend the approach (D3). No open dataset of Vietnamese courses exists, so we use
the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) as a stand-in, with every early sign dated by its cut-off. Four
final questions span the analytics levels: outcome shares and timing (AQ1, descriptive), early signs by week 4 (AQ2,
diagnostic), estimation against the early-sign rule (AQ3, predictive) and a ranked list under staff capacity (AQ4,
prescriptive). We answered AQ1 and AQ2 on all 22 module runs of 2013 and 2014; AQ3 and AQ4 remain a Level 1 design,
not yet run. Of the 28,061 enrolments registered at day 14, 45.3% (44.7–45.8) did not complete, 45.7% in 2013 and
44.9% in 2014, runs ranged from 24.9% to 58.3%, and under one in five non-completers had missed a task by week 4. All
five early signs go with later non-completion within module in both years, a missed task by 40.8 (37.7–43.9) and 39.3
percentage points (36.4–42.1). These results give D1 a factual reason beside each name, D2 a hypothesis on where
support time goes first and D3 a measurement method, a gate and a fairness audit. They are associations, not causes,
describe two years of UK distance-learning modules and must be re-checked on the university's records; the model test
and the effect of contact await future work.
