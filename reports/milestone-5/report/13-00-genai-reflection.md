# 13. Generative AI Use and Reflection

We used generative AI as a simulated stakeholder, a research and code assistant, a critic and a writing partner, and in
every role under one rule: AI was never a source of data, statistics, licence information or results, and every fact it
offered was checked against a saved source, the data files or code that we ran.

## 13.1. AI Decision Log, Milestones 1–5

Table A.43 (Appendix XVI) gives the AI Decision Log in the columns of the brief, one row for each earlier milestone and
four rows for the final integration; for Milestones 1 to 4 it records how AI served as simulated stakeholders, research
assistant, critics and code assistant, how we checked each output and which critiques we accepted or rejected. In
Milestone 5, AI agents extracted 369 statements, each checked automatically against a quotation from its source; wrote
the AQ1 and AQ2 code, whose results we accepted only after the automated consistency checks passed, a byte-identical rerun and an
independent recomputation; and reviewed the full draft, raising 66 critiques that we checked against the data: 42 accepted, 14
in part, 10 handled in the finishing steps and none rejected. No AI produced a metric value, threshold or result.

## 13.2. Reflection on AI as a reasoning and writing partner

The roles of AI changed across the milestones while the rule stayed the same: its output was a proposal for us to
verify, never a finding. This follows the guidance of the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO)
on generative AI in education and research, which holds that
generative AI cannot be trusted to be accurate [20] and that learners and researchers should never accept its
information at face value [20]. Verification was matched to what could go wrong: quotes against transcripts,
literature against saved copies with verbatim quotations, code against fixtures and independent recounts, and critics
confined to the brief and the data. The reviews helped most where the critic had to argue from the data, as when the
Milestone 3 auditor found tasks due before registration counted as missed and a leakage check that could not fail. The
decisions stayed ours: to keep the 2014 runs closed, to keep one early-sign rule, to report AQ3 and AQ4 as designs and
to state every association as an association. AI therefore supported our reasoning by challenging it and by speeding
up routine work, while the evidence came from the data, the sources and the code we ran.
