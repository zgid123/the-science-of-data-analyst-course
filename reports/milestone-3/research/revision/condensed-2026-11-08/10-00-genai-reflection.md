# 10. Generative AI Usage and Reflection

Generative artificial intelligence (AI) supported this milestone in five of the roles the brief allows, and every
finding about the data was confirmed by code, statistics, documentation or a direct check on the dataset, as the brief
requires [1]; AI was never treated as a source of data [1]. AI agents worked on both sides of each review, and
team members verified the decisions; Appendix V gives the AI Decision Log, the prompts and every decision.

As data dictionary interpreters, research agents read the descriptor, the OU's assessment policy and the IMD
documentation, and each fact was kept only with a verbatim quotation; the 2025 rule that a CMA cannot be late was
tested on the data and not applied. As code assistant, AI drafted every script of the package, verified by 13 figures
equal to an independent recount, nine unit tests, six features recomputed for every row without a difference, and
one row recounted by hand. As preparation critic, an isolated agent raised 12 critiques, 9 accepted and 3 in part,
and as leakage auditor a second one raised 8 findings, all accepted, without finding any use of data after a cut-off.
As documentation and synthetic-data assistant, AI drafted the dictionary, lineage and package guide and the small
invented test records, which never enter a view.

The reviews shaped the rules the pipeline applies: the AQ4 list is judged by its gain over chance (C13), CMA lateness,
seen in 47.2% of CMA submissions, stays out of the early-sign rule (C10), a task counts as missed only if it falls due
on or after the registration day, and the leakage check can fail. One request was declined in part: the test
enrolments of students seen in 2013 are counted, but reporting their outcome rate before modelling would expose test outcomes during preparation, which the brief rules out [1].
