# S523 – Sclater, N. (2016). Developing a code of practice for learning analytics. Journal of Learn

## Summary in my own words

Sclater describes how Jisc built its Code of Practice for Learning Analytics from a taxonomy of ethical and legal issues and expert workshops. The code covers responsibility, transparency and consent, privacy, validity, access, enabling positive interventions, minimising adverse impacts and stewardship. On adverse impacts, institutions must ensure that labelling of students does not bias staff perceptions, recognise that analytics never give a complete picture, and regularly review systems. The Open University noted that its scale makes human intermediation unfeasible for many uses.

## Key facts

- Lifecycle of issues – "ensuring validity in the data and the analytics, enabling student access to the data, carrying out"
- Labelling must not bias perceptions – "Institutions will take steps to ensure that trends, norms, categorization or any labelling of students"
- Analytics are incomplete – "Institutions recognize that analytics can never give a complete picture of an individual’s learning"
- Regular review – "Analytics systems and interventions will be carefully designed and regularly reviewed to ensure"
- Human intermediation and OU scale – "a representative from The Open University said that the sheer scale of that institution makes it unfeasible"
- Campbell J. P. et al. (EDUCAUSE Review 2007; not registered) and Greller W. & Drachsler H. (2012; not registered) on not prejudicing students by categorising – "institutions should be careful not to"
