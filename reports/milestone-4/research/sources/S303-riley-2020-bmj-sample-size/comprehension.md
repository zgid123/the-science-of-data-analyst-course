# S303 – Calculating the Sample Size Required for Developing a Clinical Prediction Model (Riley et al., BMJ 2020)

## Summary in my own words

This BMJ "Research Methods and Reporting" article turns the Riley et al. (2019) criteria into a step-by-step guide for calculating the minimum sample size needed to develop a prediction model. It warns that many models are built on datasets too small for the number of participants or outcome events, which leads to inaccurate predictions. Only the abstract and bibliographic record were readable (BMJ and repository PDF refused automated access); the criteria themselves are taken from S304.

## Key facts

- Problem – "many are developed using a dataset that is too small for the total number of participants or outcome events."
- Consequence – "This leads to inaccurate predictions and consequently incorrect healthcare decisions for some individuals."
- Purpose – "the authors provide guidance on how to calculate the sample size required to develop a clinical prediction model."
- Riley R. D. et al. (2019, Statistics in Medicine; S304) holds the derivation of the criteria.
