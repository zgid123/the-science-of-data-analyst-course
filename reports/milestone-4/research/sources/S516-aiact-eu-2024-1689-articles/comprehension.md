# S516 – AI Act text (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended): Articles 6, 14, 26, 86, 113 and Annex III

## Summary in my own words

The AI Act Explorer reproduces the articles of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 with the amendments of the Digital Omnibus (S518) marked. Annex III point 3 lists four education uses as high-risk: admission, evaluating learning outcomes including steering the learning process, assessing the level of education a person will access, and monitoring prohibited behaviour in tests. An Annex III system escapes high-risk status only if it poses no significant risk and does not materially influence decisions, but a system that profiles natural persons is always high-risk. Article 14 requires that high-risk systems can be effectively overseen by people, who must understand the system's limits, stay aware of automation bias, interpret outputs correctly and be able to disregard or override them. Article 26 makes the deployer assign competent overseers and monitor the system in use; Article 86 gives affected persons a right to an explanation. After the amendment, the Annex III obligations apply from 2 December 2027.

## Key facts

- Annex III 3(a) – "AI systems intended to be used to determine access or admission or to assign natural persons to educational and vocational training institutions at all levels;"
- Annex III 3(b) – "AI systems intended to be used to evaluate learning outcomes, including when those outcomes are used to steer the learning process of natural persons in educational and vocational training institutions at all levels;"
- Annex III 3(c) – "AI systems intended to be used for the purpose of assessing the appropriate level of education that an individual will receive or will be able to access"
- Annex III 3(d) – "AI systems intended to be used for monitoring and detecting prohibited behaviour of students during tests"
- Article 6(3) derogation – "an AI system referred to in Annex III shall not be considered to be high-risk where it does not pose a significant risk of harm to the health, safety or fundamental rights of natural persons, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision making."
- Profiling is always high-risk – "an AI system referred to in Annex III shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs profiling of natural persons."
- Article 14(1) – "High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use."
- Article 14(4)(b) automation bias – "to remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output produced by a high-risk AI system (automation bias)"
- Article 14(4)(d) override – "to decide, in any particular situation, not to use the high-risk AI system or to otherwise disregard, override or reverse the output of the high-risk AI system;"
- Article 26 deployer oversight – "Deployers shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support."
- Article 26 monitoring – "Deployers shall monitor the operation of the high-risk AI system on the basis of the instructions for use"
- Article 86 explanation – "shall have the right to obtain from the deployer clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system in the decision-making procedure and the main elements of the decision taken."
- Amended application date – "2 December 2027 as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article 6(2) and Annex III; and"

## Against reference.md

The outline line for Part 5 reads "fairness and high-stakes use with human oversight; data constraints and transfer to the university" (reference.md, Part 5). Confirms the human-oversight requirement and refines the outline's premise: Annex III point 3 does not name dropout or early-warning prediction; such a model falls under point 3(b) only if its output is used to evaluate learning outcomes or steer learning, but a student risk score is profiling, and profiling keeps an Annex III system high-risk. The outline's 'high-stakes use' label holds as a design assumption, and the legal classification is for the university's counsel. The Annex III date is 2 December 2027, not 2 August 2026 (amendment S518).
