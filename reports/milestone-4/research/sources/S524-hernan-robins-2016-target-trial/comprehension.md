# S524 – Using Big Data to Emulate a Target Trial When a Randomized Trial Is Not Available - PMC

## Summary in my own words

Hernán and Robins argue that a causal question answered from observational data should be framed as the emulation of a target trial: a randomised experiment that would answer it, specified by eligibility, treatment strategies, assignment, follow-up, outcome, causal contrast and analysis plan. Emulating randomisation requires adjusting for all baseline confounders; when the data lack them, emulation fails, and confounding is most serious when comparing an active treatment with no treatment. Time zero must be where eligibility is met and treatment is assigned.

## Key facts

- Randomised experiment is the ideal; observational data emulate it – "Ideally, questions about comparative effectiveness or safety would be answered using an appropriately designed and conducted randomized experiment."
- Seven protocol components – "the eligibility criteria, treatment strategies being compared (including their start and end times), assignment procedures, follow-up period, outcome of interest, causal contrast(s) of interest, and analysis p"
- Emulation fails without baseline confounders – "If the observational database does not contain sufficient information on baseline confounders or if we fail to identify them, successful emulation of the target trial's random assignment is not possible."
- Active versus no treatment is the hardest case – "Confounding bias may be especially serious when emulating target trials that, like ours, compare an active treatment with no treatment (or usual care)"
- Time zero rule – "Eligibility criteria need to be met at that point but not later; study outcomes begin to be counted after that point but not earlier."
