# S311 – The ASA Statement on p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose (Wasserstein & Lazar, The American Statistician 2016)

## Summary in my own words

The American Statistical Association issued six principles on the use of p-values: among them, that decisions should not rest only on whether a p-value crosses a threshold, and that a p-value does not measure the size or importance of an effect. Good practice combines design, multiple summaries, context and complete reporting. The statement text was not readable from the journal; the six principles were read from the ASA's own press release.

## Key facts

- Six principles – "The American Statistical Association (ASA) has released a "Statement on Statistical Significance and P-Values" with six principles underlying the proper use and interpretation of the p-value."
- Threshold decisions – "Scientific conclusions and business or policy decisions should not be based only on whether a p-value passes a specific threshold."
- Effect size – "A p-value, or statistical significance, does not measure the size of an effect or the importance of a result."
- Good practice – "emphasizes principles of good study design and conduct, a variety of numerical and graphical summaries of data, understanding of the phenomenon under study, interpretation of results in context, complete reporting"
- Executive director's comment – "The p-value was never intended to be a substitute for scientific reasoning,"
