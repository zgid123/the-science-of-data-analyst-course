## 5.2. Causal Limits, Fairness and High-Stakes Use

Beyond the validity risks of Section 5.1, the list will shape decisions about real students. Its first limit is
causal: the effect of an intervention is best answered by a randomised experiment, which observational data can at
most emulate [43], and estimating for whom contact helps needs randomised or unconfounded treatment data [9] that
OULAD lacks; risk and benefit can also diverge, as field experiments in customer retention showed [20]. The contact
decision therefore stays deferred, and the next step is a randomised pilot whose time zero is the cut-off at which
students become eligible [43], with contacts and outcomes logged together. Fairness has a second limit: a score cannot
have equal predictive value and equal error rates across groups with different non-completion rates [35], so
separation leads (Section 4.4). A flag leads to support and expires with the run, because flagged students should be
able to prove the prediction wrong [44] and labels must not bias staff perceptions [45].

The use is high-stakes and regulated. The European Union (EU) AI Act, a reference standard here, lists as high-risk the systems that evaluate learning outcomes or steer learning and requires that people can oversee and override them [46]; its main obligations for these systems apply from 2 December 2027 after Regulation
(EU) 2026/1744 [47]. Vietnam's Law on Artificial Intelligence 134/2025/QH15 applies from 1 March 2026 and requires
human control over AI decisions [48], Decision 33/2026/QĐ-TTg lists as high-risk in education the systems that rank
learners or analyse learner behaviour [49], and processing personal data with AI must be classified by risk level
[50]. Such a list at a Vietnamese university is therefore likely to be high-risk: a pilot needs risk classification first, and tutors keep the final say, can override any entry and see the reasons. Once tutors act on lists, outcomes partly reflect the contacts, a performative effect [51], so contacts are
logged with outcomes before any retraining.
