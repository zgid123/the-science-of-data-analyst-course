## 3.4. Ranking under Capacity

AQ4 uses the AQ3 scores of Section 3.3 to form one list per module run whose length k follows the assumed capacity: 3
or 5 contacts per tutor group of about 20 students [2], that is 15% or 25% of the enrolments registered at the
cut-off, and it is judged by the top of the list, where the ranking matters [6], [7]. The baselines are a random list, whose expected recall equals k/n, and the rule-ranked list of the same length.
Risk tiers, a first tier of the top 3 per 20 and a second of the next 2 per 20, are the candidate form for tutors who
want a lighter second contact, and both cut points come from capacity, not from the test data.

The list gives tutors ranks and each student's main contributing signs, and shows probabilities only when calibration
holds (Section 4.3). The tutor decides whom to contact, using knowledge the data do not hold [11]. Ranking by expected
benefit of contact is rejected, because estimating how the effect of an intervention varies needs randomised or
unconfounded treatment data [9], and in field experiments the customers at highest predicted risk were not the
best targets of an intervention [20]. The assumption that risk order matches need therefore stays untested until a
randomised pilot (Section 5.2).
