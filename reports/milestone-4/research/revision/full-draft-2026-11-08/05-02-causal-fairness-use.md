## 5.2. Causal Limits, Fairness and High-Stakes Use

**Causal limits.** Beyond the validity risks of Section 5.1, the list will shape decisions about real students. Its
first limit is causal. The effect of an intervention is best answered by a randomised experiment, which observational
data can at most emulate [43], and estimating for whom a contact helps needs randomised or unconfounded data on
treatment [9], which OULAD lacks. Risk and benefit can also diverge, as field experiments in customer retention showed
[20]. The contact decision therefore stays deferred, and the next step is a randomised pilot whose time zero is the
cut-off at which students become eligible for the list [43], with contacts and outcomes logged together.

**Fairness.** Fairness has a second limit: a score cannot have equal predictive value and equal error rates across
groups whose non-completion rates differ [35], so separation leads (Section 4.4). A flag leads to support and expires
with the run, because flagged students should be able to prove the prediction wrong [44], and labels must not bias how
staff see a student [45].

**High-stakes and regulated use.** The use is high-stakes, and it is regulated. The European Union (EU) AI Act, a
reference standard here rather than a law that binds a Vietnamese university, lists as high-risk the systems that
evaluate learning outcomes or steer learning, and requires that people can oversee and override them [46]; its main
obligations for these systems apply from 2 December 2027 under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 [47]. Vietnam's Law on
Artificial Intelligence 134/2025/QH15 applies from 1 March 2026 and requires human control over decisions made with AI
[48]; Decision 33/2026/QĐ-TTg lists as high-risk in education the systems that rank learners or analyse learner
behaviour [49]; and processing personal data with AI must be classified by level of risk [50]. Such a list at a
Vietnamese university is therefore likely to be high-risk. A pilot needs a risk classification first, and tutors keep
the final say, can override any entry and see the reasons behind it.

**Feedback after use.** Once tutors act on the lists, the outcomes partly reflect the contacts, a performative effect
in which a prediction changes what it predicts [51], so contacts are logged with outcomes before any model is
retrained.
