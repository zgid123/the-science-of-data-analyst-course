## 5.2. Causal Limits, Fairness and High-Stakes Use

**Use on real students.** Beyond the validity risks of Section 5.1, the list will shape decisions about real students.
It supports the choice of whom to contact first and claims nothing about the effect of contact, because such an effect
needs a randomised experiment or unconfounded data on treatment [44], [9], and the students at highest risk do not
always benefit most [20]; the next step is therefore a randomised pilot with contacts and outcomes logged together.
Fairness leads with separation, because equal predictive value and equal error rates cannot hold together when rates
differ between groups [36]; a flag leads to support, expires with the run and must not bias how staff see a student
[45], [46]. The use is high-stakes and regulated. The European Union (EU) AI Act, a reference standard here rather
than a law that binds a Vietnamese university, lists such systems as high-risk and requires human oversight [47], with
its main obligations for them applying from 2 December 2027 [48]; Vietnam's Law on AI, No. 134/2025/QH15, requires human
control over decisions made with AI [49], Decision 33/2026/QĐ-TTg lists the ranking of learners and the analysis of
learner behaviour as high-risk in education [50], and processing personal data with AI must be classified by level of
risk [51]. A pilot therefore needs a risk classification, confirmed by the university's legal staff, before it starts;
tutors keep the final say and can override any entry; and contacts are logged with outcomes before any retraining,
because a prediction can change what it predicts [52]. Appendix V, item 3, treats these limits in full.
