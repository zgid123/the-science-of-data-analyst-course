# Reader re-test of the Vietnamese report (Milestone 1)

Reader: a Vietnamese lecturer of data analytics marking a student proposal, reading only the `.vi.md` files (01-00 to 09-00, plus the abbreviation and glossary pages 00-1 and 00-2), in file-name order, without the source-language text. Evidence codes and citations in square brackets were ignored. Date: 2026-11-05.

Overall judgement: yes, a reader of the field could explain this document afterwards. The argument holds together from start to finish: late detection of students who fall behind in online and blended courses, five simulated stakeholders, a course-completion goal (assumed baseline 70%, target 75% by 2027–2028), two in-semester KPIs, four analytical questions from descriptive to prescriptive, contact lists at weeks 3 and 5, and three decisions classified with Anthony/Simon/Bezos. Most sentences are short and grammatical, and nothing is impossible to decode. The friction comes from four sources. First, a few long noun phrases translated from English recur on almost every page, above all "số sinh viên (mà) cán bộ liên hệ được mỗi tuần" (17 times) and "sinh viên giảm tham gia học tập" (13 times); they make sentences heavy and sometimes drop the relative marker "mà", so the head noun has to be re-found. Second, some sentences pack an implicit inference that the reader has to reconstruct: the "one in six" arithmetic, the data-lag logic behind weeks 2/4 versus 3/5, why a simple rule is the right baseline, and the "contacted students who would pass even if nobody contacted them" paradox. Third, stakeholder codes (CL5, PM1, AD1) appear in Section 1.2 before Section 2.3 explains them, and Section 4.1 brings in a "phòng công tác sinh viên" that is never distinguished from "phòng hỗ trợ sinh viên". Fourth, course-brief English stays untranslated (Business Goal, SMART Goal, Milestone, persona, prompt, Before → After); a lecturer of this course accepts these, but they still sound translated. No passage scored 1.
