## 01-00-project-context.md

**Claims:** C1-001, C1-002, C1-003, C1-004, C1-005, C1-006

**Outline:**
- Setting: a university expanding online and blended learning (brief section 3); in Vietnam this sits inside a national rule that allows up to 30% of a regular programme online, with online quality to be evidenced and courses graded through several component scores (C1-001, C1-002).
- Institutional pressure: withdrawal is a regulated quality criterion – at most 10% a year and 15% in the first year, tied to enrolment quotas (C1-003, C1-004); from 15 November 2026 Circular 83/2026 keeps the thresholds and defines withdrawal more precisely (C1-005).
- Consequence for the scenario university: it needs student-level data on who is active, on leave, transferred or withdrawn (C1-006) – the bridge to why analytics is needed.
- Label every figure as real-world background, never as a fact about the scenario university (outline Scope and rules).

**Conflicts with the outline:** None. Note for the writer: the Vietnamese regulation measures programme-level withdrawal per year, while the outline's problem is course completion and mid-semester disengagement; present the regulation as institutional pressure, not as the project KPI.

**Gaps:** Official texts of Circulars 08/2021, 01/2024 and 83/2026 not saved (vbpl.vn 404, thuvienphapluat 403); claims rest on secondary news reports (S106, S107, S113). Whether Circular 08/2021 has been amended is unverified.

## 01-01-situation-background.md

**Claims:** C1-007, C1-008, C1-009, C1-010, C1-011, C1-012, C1-013, C1-016, C1-014, C1-015, C1-017, C1-018, C1-019, C1-020, C1-021, C1-022, C1-023, C1-024, C1-025

**Outline:**
- Size internationally: online retention is lower than face-to-face (C1-007), by 8–14 or 10–20 points in US studies of 2009–2011 (C1-008); blended courses complete better than other online modes (C1-009); distance institutions show a large graduation deficit (C1-010); programme-level benchmark from NCES, fall 2019→2020 and 2014 cohort (C1-011); MOOC median completion 12.6% (C1-012), labelled as non-credit courses.
- Size in Vietnam: about 20% overall and 20–30% for Vietnamese institutions, concentrated in the first year (C1-013, C1-016); a Vietnamese instance of mid-semester non-participation – 2,252 students at one HCMC university in semester I of 2019–2020 (C1-014); few Vietnamese data by year of study (C1-015).
- Timing and early signs: half of MOOC enrolees never show up and the first two weeks are critical (C1-017); in a credit-bearing blended course weeks 5–6 are the last useful warning point, and midterm grades come too late (C1-018, C1-019).
- Reasons: factor groups from two systematic reviews (C1-020, C1-022), interacting student, course and environment factors (C1-021), Vietnamese personal, institutional and family factors (C1-023, C1-016 for wrong-major choice).
- Response in the literature: early intervention and proactive contact (C1-024, C1-025), which leads into the problem statement.

**Conflicts with the outline:**
1. Timing: the outline frames the problem as students stopping "in the middle of the semester"; Jordan (2015; S110) finds the steepest disengagement in the first two weeks of online courses, and Howard et al. (2018; S112) place the useful warning point at weeks 5–6, before midterms. Suggested wording: mid-semester non-participation is the visible end of disengagement that starts in the first weeks. For the human to decide.
2. Level of measurement: the Vietnamese and NCES figures (S103, S105, S106) are programme-level dropout, retention or forced withdrawal, not course completion in online or blended courses, which the outline names; the report must keep the levels distinct.

**Gaps:** No national Vietnamese dropout statistic (Ministry figures not found); no Vietnamese data on completion of online or blended courses. Lee and Choi (2011; S101), Simpson (2013; S108) and Reich and Ruipérez-Valiente (2019; S109) were read only as metadata or abstract; the "69 factors" count of Lee and Choi is unverified and should not be stated. International percentage gaps are from 2009–2011 US studies.

## 01-02-problem-statement-scope.md

**Claims:** C1-014, C1-034, C1-019, C1-026, C1-024, C1-027, C1-033, C1-028, C1-029, C1-030, C1-031, C1-032, C1-005, C1-006, C1-035, C1-015

**Outline:**
- Problem: students stop taking part during the semester, sometimes silently – not attending (C1-014) or not registering and not returning after leave (C1-034) – and grade-based warnings arrive too late (C1-019).
- Scope of the measure: retention and withdrawal are defined inconsistently, so the project must define course completion and disengagement before measuring (C1-026); the Vietnamese rule itself excludes approved leave and transfers (C1-005).
- Why analytics: early intervention needs early-semester data (C1-024); student-level data across the course of study is now required (C1-006); several units and advisors must act together from the start of the course (C1-027, C1-033).
- Data a university typically holds (concept level only): student records, continuous assessment, LMS usage (C1-028, C1-029); engagement measured as resource access or assignment submission (C1-030); an institutional example (C1-031) and an open dataset named only as a possible source (C1-032).
- Limits: analytics never give a complete picture of a student (C1-035); Vietnamese evidence by year of study is thin (C1-015).

**Conflicts with the outline:** None beyond the timing nuance recorded under 01-01 (the problem statement should say disengagement starts in the first weeks and becomes visible mid-semester). Claims C1-028 to C1-032 describe data and an institutional system, not a model to build: Milestone 1 "analyses no dataset, trains no model" (outline Scope and rules).

**Gaps:** Jisc's 2016 review of learning analytics practice (S111) was blocked (403/429), so no survey of which data UK or US universities hold beyond S006, S008 and S112; no EDUCAUSE source saved. No Vietnamese source on LMS data use for early warning.
