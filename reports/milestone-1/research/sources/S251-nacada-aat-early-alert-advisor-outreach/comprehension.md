# S251 – Reddick et al. (2014), Maximizing the use of an early alert system through advisor outreach

## Summary in my own words

Advisors at East Carolina University describe how they run early-alert outreach. Faculty send notifications, mostly around registration, and advisors follow up by email, phone and meetings; email is by far the most common channel. Advisors carry about 350 students and note that the alert traffic becomes overwhelming as faculty use grows. At-risk students tend not to resolve problems on their own, so phone calls are recommended. The authors recommend a yearly assessment with data from faculty, students and advisors.

## Key facts

- Caseload – "typically carry an average caseload of 350 students"
- Channel – "96% of advisors use email to follow up with students, which often prompts a face-to-face meeting"
- Volume – "the notification traffic can become overwhelming to an advisor"
- Student reluctance – "students, especially those at risk, may be reluctant to resolve academic concerns independently"
- Lynch-Holmes K., Troy A. B. & Ramos I. (connect.edu white paper, 2012) on early alert – "One popular way to identify students in academic difficulty is the use of an academic early alert system"
