# S011 – Gorry & Scott Morton (1971), A framework for management information systems (MIT Sloan Working Paper 510-71)

## Summary in my own words

Gorry and Scott Morton build a framework for management information systems from two earlier classifications of managerial activity. From Robert Anthony (1965) they take three categories defined by the purpose of the activity: strategic planning, management control and operational control, and they quote his definition of each. From Herbert Simon (1960) they take the distinction between programmed and nonprogrammed decisions, quoting his definitions, and rename them structured and unstructured. Crossing the two gives a grid with a middle class of semi-structured decisions; structured decisions can be largely automated, while unstructured ones require human judgment. The paper is therefore a citable secondary route to both originals, and a primary source for the combined grid.

## Key facts

- Anthony R. N. (Planning and Control Systems, Harvard 1965; S104), strategic planning – "Strategic planning is the process of deciding on objectives of the organization, on changes in these objectives, on the resources used to attain these objectives, and on the policies that are to govern the acquisition, use and disposition of these"
- Strategic planning is done by few senior people, non-repetitively – "the strategic planning process typically involves a fairly small number of high level people"
- Anthony R. N. (1965; S104), management control – "Management control is the process by which managers assure that resources are being used effectively and efficiently in the accomplishment of the organization's objectives."
- Anthony R. N. (1965; S104), operational control – "the process of assuring specific tasks are carried out"
- Tasks versus people – "operational control is concerned with tasks (such as manufacturing a specific part) whereas management control is most often concerned with people"
- Less judgment in operational control – "there is much less judgment to be exercised in the operational control area"
- Category boundaries – "the boundaries between these three categories are often not clear"
- Anthony's book as cited – "Robert Anthony, Planning and Control Systems: A Framework for Analysis, Graduate School of Business Administration, Harvard University Boston, 1965."
- Simon H. A. (The New Science of Management Decision, Harper 1960, p. 6; S105), continuum – "He regards them as being at the opposite ends of a continuum"
- Simon (1960; S105), programmed – "Decisions are programmed to the extent that they are repetitive and routine, to the extent that a definite procedure has been worked out for handling them"
- Simon (1960; S105), nonprogrammed – "Decisions are nonprogrammed to the extent that they are novel, unstructured and consequential."
- Simon's book as cited – "H. A. Simon, The New Science of Management Decision, Harper and Rowe, 1960, p. 6."
- Renaming to structured and unstructured, chosen – "because they imply less dependence on the computer"
- Human judgment in unstructured problems – "the human decision maker must provide judgment and evaluation as well as insights into problem definition in the unstructured case."
- Automation of structured problems – "much if not all of the decision-making process can be automated."
- Basis of each scheme – "Anthony's categorization is really based on the purpose of the management activity, whereas Simon's classification is based on the way in which"
- Semi-structured class – "These decisions are those with one or two of the intelligence, design and choice phases unstructured."
