# S112 – Howard, Meehan & Parnell (2018), Contrasting prediction methods for early warning systems at undergraduate level

## Summary in my own words

Howard, Meehan and Parnell (University College Dublin) build an early warning system for a large first-year statistics course whose materials are mostly online (blended, LMS Blackboard) and which has weekly continuous assessment. They use three kinds of data: background information from student records, continuous-assessment results, and fine-grained LMS usage. They find weeks 5–6, halfway through the semester, the best time to act: late enough for reasonable accuracy, early enough for students to change. Continuous-assessment results are the strongest predictors; LMS data add a little accuracy and show where students struggle. They note that early warning systems that rely on midterm grades come too late, because students may already have dropped out.

## Key facts

- Setting – "We present findings from a statistics university course which has a large proportion of resources on the LMS Blackboard and weekly continuous assessment."
- Timing of intervention – "We identify weeks 5-6 of our course (half way through the semester) as an optimal time to implement an early warning system, as it allows time for the students to make changes to their study patterns whilst retaining reasonable prediction accuracy."
- Pistilli M. D. & Arnold K. E. (2010; About Campus, 15(3)) – midterm-based warnings come late – "By the time midterms have been corrected it is often far into the semester, and students may have already dropped out."
- Early operation needed – "It is crucial that early warning systems operate in the early stages of the semester."
- Data categories – "Data were recorded for students in regards to three categories: students’ background information; continuous assessment; and LMS usage on a fine-grained scale."
- Typical variables in the literature (You J. W., 2016; Pistilli & Arnold, 2012 = S004) – "Variables based on students’ demographic/historic data, continuous assessment results and LMS usage have been collected for early warning systems"
- LMS data examples – "LMS data can include length of time on a LMS system, number of visits to a module page, contributions to a module discussion thread et cetera."
- Strongest predictor – "Continuous assessment proved unsurprisingly the most important category."
- Many early warning systems use LMS data – "Many of these early warning systems rely on data from students’ engagement with Learning Management Systems (LMSs)."
- Course size – "In the first semester of 2015/16 there were a total of 144 students registered for Practical Statistics."
