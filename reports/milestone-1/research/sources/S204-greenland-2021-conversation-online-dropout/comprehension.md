# S204 – Greenland (2022), The Conversation: biggest causes of online students' dropout

## Summary in my own words
The lead author of S203 summarises the 200-interview study for a general audience. Personal circumstances (employment, family and relationships, health, personal situation, location) made up over 65% of the main dropout reasons; learner context (enrolment approach, motivation, time management, learning resources) almost a quarter; and administration and module design only 10%. Students asked for flexible deadlines, simpler extension requests, flexible semesters and ways to pause and resume without penalty, plus study-skill and resilience preparation before starting.

## Key facts
- Personal circumstances dominate – "Personal circumstances were the major drivers. Over 65% of the main dropout reasons fell into this category."
- Learner context share – "Learner context aspects accounted for almost one-quarter of the main dropout reasons."
- Course design is a minor cause – "Only 10% of main dropout reasons related to administrative issues and module design and delivery."
- Support wanted – "Students with sudden temporary increases in personal demands need simpler processes for requesting extensions and adjusting hand-in dates."
- Pause without penalty – "Students wanted such interventions to be made available without financial or academic penalty."
- Policies do not fit lives – "The students often mentioned that university policies and processes did not allow for their personal challenges."
- Online vs campus – "online students are 2.5 times more likely than on-campus students to withdraw without a qualification."
