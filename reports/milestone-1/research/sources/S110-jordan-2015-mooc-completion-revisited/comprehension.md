# S110 – Jordan (2015), Massive open online course completion rates revisited: Assessment, length and attrition

## Summary in my own words

Katy Jordan (The Open University, UK) compiles enrolment and completion data for 221 MOOCs from 14 providers and 78 institutions. Completion, defined as the share of enrolled students who complete, ranges from 0.7% to 52.1% with a median of 12.6%; longer courses complete less, more recent courses and auto-graded courses complete more. For 59 courses with week-by-week data, about half of enrolled students never show up, and the first two weeks are critical: after them, the share of active students and of students submitting assessments levels out. The study is about open, free MOOCs rather than credit-bearing university courses, so it shows the timing pattern of disengagement more than the level of a degree programme.

## Key facts

- Data – "This analysis is based upon enrolment and completion data collected for a total of 221 Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs)."
- Completion range and median – "Completion rates (defined as the percentage of enrolled students who completed the course) vary from 0.7% to 52.1%, with a median value of 12.6%."
- Course length – "longer courses having lower completion rates"
- No-shows – "Around half of a MOOCs’ enrolled students will not show up, and the first two weeks of a course appear to be critical in gaining student engagement."
- Levelling after week 2 – "the first and second weeks appear to be critical in achieving student engagement, after which the proportion of active students and those submitting assessments levels out, with less than 3% difference between them"
- Perna L. W., Ruby A., Boruch R. F. et al. (2014; Educational Researcher, 43(9)) on 16 Coursera MOOCs from the University of Pennsylvania corroborate the early-week pattern – "This study corroborates the findings here in that the initial weeks of courses are key for students engagement."
- Engagement measures used – "The two most common definitions of engagement across the duration of courses used by the sources were the number of students accessing resources, or completing assignments."

## Against reference.md

Confirms and refines the outline's problem statement ("students stop taking part in the middle of the semester", Scope and rules): in open online courses the steepest disengagement is in the first two weeks, before mid-course, so the report should frame mid-semester disengagement as the visible end of a process that starts early. This is a nuance to the outline's "middle of the semester" wording rather than a contradiction; recorded as a conflict for the human to decide.
