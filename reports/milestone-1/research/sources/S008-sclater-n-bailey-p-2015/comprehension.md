# S008 – Code of practice for learning analytics (Sclater & Bailey, Jisc 2015, updated 2026)

## Summary in my own words

Jisc's code of practice sets out UK institutions' responsibilities for using learning analytics responsibly, appropriately and effectively, in eight areas: responsibility, transparency and consent, privacy, validity, access, enabling positive interventions, minimising adverse impacts, and stewardship of data. It says analytics should benefit students and must not be used for assessing, grading or profiling; that data sources, purposes, metrics, access and interpretation must be explained; that predictive algorithms must be validated by qualified staff; that students can see the analytics and labels attached to them; that labels must not bias staff or reinforce discrimination; and that data be kept to the minimum and for defined periods. The live page, first published 4 June 2015, was updated on 9 September 2026 and now includes an appendix on artificial intelligence (explainability, purpose limitation, human judgement, contestability, bias). It was read through the Wayback Machine because jisc.ac.uk refused the script (403).

## Key facts

- Purpose – "Setting out the responsibilities of educational institutions to ensure that learning analytics is carried out responsibly, appropriately and effectively."
- Dates – "04 June 2015" and "09 September 2026"
- Student benefit and limits – "Learning analytics should be used for the benefit of students." and "It must not be used for assessing, grading, or profiling."
- Transparency – "Complete transparency and clear institutional policies are therefore essential regarding the purposes of learning analytics, the data collected, the processes involved, and how they will be used to enhance the educational experience."
- Responsibility for stewardship – "The retention and stewardship of data used for and generated by learning analytics"
- What must be explained – "The data sources, the purposes of the analytics, the metrics used, who has access to the analytics, the boundaries around usage and how to interpret the data must be explained clearly to staff and students."
- Consent for special category data, such as ethnicity – "such as ethnicity, as defined by UK GDPR"
- Consent for interventions – "Students will also normally be asked for their consent for personal interventions to be taken based on the learning analytics."
- Restricted access – "Access to student data and analytics should be restricted to those identified by the institution as having a legitimate need to view them."
- Re-identification risk – "Re-identification of individuals by aggregating multiple data sources"
- Validity – "Spurious correlations are avoided"
- Validation by staff – "All algorithms and metrics used for predictive analytics or interventions should be understood, validated, reviewed and improved as appropriate by qualified staff."
- AI outputs as support – "AI-generated analysis should be treated as supporting material, with judgement remaining with skilled staff."
- Student access to labels – "They should also be able to view the metrics and labels attached to them."
- Support, not penalties – "AI-enabled predictions should prompt offers of support, not restrictions, penalties or high-stakes decisions, unless such uses have been explicitly justified and governed."
- Human intermediation and audit – "Some may require human rather than automated intermediation. Predictions and interventions will be recorded, auditable, and their appropriateness and effectiveness reviewed."
- Incomplete picture – "Institutions must recognise that analytics can never give a complete picture of an individual’s learning and may sometimes ignore personal circumstances."
- Labelling – "Institutions should take steps to ensure that trends, norms, categorisation or any labelling of students do not bias staff, student or institutional perceptions and behaviours towards them, reinforce discriminatory attitudes or increase social power differentials."
- Student autonomy – "Students maintain appropriate levels of autonomy in decision making relating to their learning"
- Fairness of AI analytics – "AI-enabled analytics do not introduce or amplify bias, including through indirect proxies for protected characteristics, and produce fair outcomes across different groups of students"
- Data minimisation and retention – "Kept to the minimum necessary to deliver the purposes of the analytics reliably" and "Retained only for appropriate and clearly defined periods"
- Interpretation by humans – "AI should assist qualified staff rather than replace them, with responsibility for interpreting outputs and making decisions remaining with a suitably qualified person."
- Licence and citation form – "Published under the CC BY 4.0 license. Please cite as Jisc (2015): Code of practice for learning analytics."
