# S010 – Bezos, 2015 Letter to Shareholders (Amazon.com, Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K, filed April 2016)

## Summary in my own words

Jeff Bezos's annual letter to Amazon shareholders, filed with the SEC as Exhibit 99.1 to Amazon's 8-K in April 2016 and usually titled the "2015 Letter to Shareholders", warns large organisations against one-size-fits-all decision making. He splits decisions into Type 1 decisions, which are consequential and irreversible or nearly so (one-way doors) and must be made slowly, carefully and with consultation, and Type 2 decisions, which are changeable and reversible (two-way doors) and should be made quickly by high-judgment individuals or small groups. He argues that applying the heavy Type 1 process to Type 2 decisions causes slowness, risk aversion and too little experimentation, while the opposite error tends to kill companies before they grow. For the proposal this gives a citable rule: the reversibility of a decision sets how much process and evidence it needs.

## Key facts

- Pitfall named (one-size-fits-all decision making) – "One common pitfall for large organizations"
- Type 1 definition – "Some decisions are consequential and irreversible or nearly irreversible – one-way doors – and these decisions must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation."
- Type 1 label – "We can call these Type 1 decisions."
- Type 2 definition – "But most decisions aren’t like that – they are changeable, reversible – they’re two-way doors."
- Type 2 consequences – "If you’ve made a suboptimal Type 2 decision, you don’t have to live with the consequences for that long."
- Type 2 process – "Type 2 decisions can and should be made quickly by high judgment individuals or small groups."
- Over-applying the heavy process – "there seems to be a tendency to use the heavy-weight Type 1 decision-making process on most decisions, including many Type 2 decisions."
- Its cost – "The end result of this is slowness, unthoughtful risk aversion, failure to experiment sufficiently, and consequently diminished invention."
- The opposite error (footnote 1) – "Any companies that habitually use the light-weight Type 2 decision-making process to make Type 1 decisions go extinct before they get large."
- Signatory – "Jeffrey P. Bezos"
- Filing date of the 8-K (fetch 2, EDGAR index) – "Filing Date"
