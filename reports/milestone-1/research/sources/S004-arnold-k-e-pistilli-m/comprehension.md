# S004 – Course Signals at Purdue (Arnold & Pistilli, LAK 2012)

## Summary in my own words

Only the bibliographic record and the abstract could be read (OpenAlex metadata; the ACM full text returned 403 and an older Purdue copy is gone). The abstract describes Course Signals as an early intervention system for instructors at Purdue University that predicts student performance from grades, demographic characteristics, past academic history and effort measured as interaction with the Blackboard Vista learning management system. The result reaches students as a personalised email from the faculty member and a traffic-light colour. The paper reports retention and performance outcomes and faculty and student perceptions; the figures themselves are not in the abstract. The retention claim was later disputed (S101).

## Key facts

- Purpose and user – "an early intervention solution for collegiate faculty called Course Signals is discussed"
- Real-time feedback by instructors – "Course Signals was developed to allow instructors the opportunity to employ the power of learner analytics to provide real-time feedback to a student."
- Predictors – "Course Signals relies not only on grades to predict students' performance, but also demographic characteristics, past academic history, and students' effort as measured by interaction with Blackboard Vista"
- Output – "a specific color on a stoplight -- traffic signal -- to indicate how each student is doing"
- Delivery – "The outcome is delivered to the students via a personalized email from the faculty member to each student"
- Outcomes reported – "along with retention and performance outcomes realized since its implementation"
- Venue and pages – "pages: 267–270"
- Citation traction – "cited_by_count (OpenAlex): 986" (as of 2026-11-02)

## Against reference.md

Confirms the outline's reference [4] as prior work on early warning (section 5). The outline does not mention that the retention claims were disputed; Caulfield M. (Hapgood blog 2013; S101) argues the comparison was confounded by the number of courses taken, so the proposal should cite Course Signals as a design precedent, not as proof that early warning raises retention.
