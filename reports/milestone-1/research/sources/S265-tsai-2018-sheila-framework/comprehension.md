# S265 – Tsai et al. (2018), The SHEILA framework (Journal of Learning Analytics)

## Summary in my own words

The SHEILA project built a learning analytics policy framework from 64 interviews with 78 senior managers at 51 European institutions, coded with the ROMA model. Four cases show common challenges: data infrastructure that cannot process LMS data or individual opt-outs, useful data inaccessible for privacy reasons, skill gaps, staff resistance and doubts about indicators. An Estonian university's dropout-prediction project failed its ethics application after eight months with the legal team. The paper cites EDUCAUSE surveys on data quality, integration and low investment.

## Key facts

- Interviews – "based on 64 interviews with 78 senior managers from 51 European HEIs"
- Ethics – "this plan did not pass an ethics application after eight months of consultations with the university legal team"
- Infrastructure – "The existing infrastructure is not mature enough to process data from the LMS or to cope"
- Indicators – "It remains questionable whether student dropout rate is the best success indicator"
- Proxy validity – "The captured data of time spent online may not truly reflect learning."
- Buy-in – "The benefit of using LA to support decision-making was clear to senior managers but not to teaching staff."
- Arroway P., Morgan G., O'Keefe M. & Yanosky R. (EDUCAUSE ECAR report, 2016; S268, blocked) – "data-quality concerns and system-integration difficulties were part of the major challenges"
- Yanosky R. & Arroway P. (EDUCAUSE ECAR report, 2015) – "4 in 10 institutions reported little or no investment in learning analytics"
- Macfadyen L. P. et al. (Research & Practice in Assessment, 2014) on workload resistance – "because of the perceived increase in workload for staff"
