**Relevant excerpts**

*Relationships with students*
- "advisors form 1:1 relationships with their advisees." Where advising is well resourced, this begins at new student orientation.
- "students are more likely to respond to emails, calls, and text messages from an individual on campus." The authors argue this is more likely when the student has a relationship with that person.
- "than they would be to respond to outreach in the form of cold calls from a central retention office." This contrasts advisor outreach with central-office outreach.

*Caseloads and follow-up*
- "Often, a major challenge to early alert is follow-up." The authors note follow-up is needed to gauge student need and assess whether an intervention worked.
- "can follow up with individual referring faculty in a way that a central office cannot." This is said of advisors with finite, defined caseloads.
- "When advisor caseloads are appropriately sized, 300:1 according to national best practice." The authors cite this ratio as the benchmark.

*Data on who conducts early intervention*
- "at about 90% of responding institutions." The article reports that faculty and advisors are consistently engaged in early intervention at about this share of institutions (Barefoot et al., 2012; Estrada & Latino, 2019).

*Definition and cautions*
- "formal, proactive, feedback system through which students and student-support agents are alerted to early red flags." This is Cuseo's definition of early intervention programs (n.d., p. 1).
- "sending up a red flag in and of itself is not a student retention panacea." Karp (2014) makes this point. The authors argue that outreach by a helping agent is what may improve outcomes.

*Advisors as the appropriate responders*
- "academic advisors are the most apt campus representatives to perform outreach and intervention" The authors conclude this follows from the relational nature of advising, curricular expertise, and manageable caseloads.
