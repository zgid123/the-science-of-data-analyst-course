# S260 – Dial & McKeown (2020), Why academic advisors are best suited to intervene with at-risk students

## Summary in my own words

Two University of South Carolina authors argue that advisors, not a central retention office, should run early-alert outreach. Students answer emails, calls and texts more readily from someone they know. Follow-up is named as the main weakness of early alert. They cite Karp (2014) that a flag alone does not improve retention, and 300:1 as a commonly cited caseload benchmark.

## Key facts

- Relationship – "students are more likely to respond to emails, calls, and text messages from an individual on campus"
- Follow-up – "Often, a major challenge to early alert is follow-up."
- Karp M. (Inside Higher Ed, 2014) – "sending up a red flag in and of itself is not a student retention panacea"
- Barefoot B. O. et al. (John N. Gardner Institute national survey, 2012) and Estrada S. & Latino J. (2017 national survey on the first-year experience, 2019) – "at about 90% of responding institutions."
- Benchmark – "When advisor caseloads are appropriately sized, 300:1 according to national best practice."
