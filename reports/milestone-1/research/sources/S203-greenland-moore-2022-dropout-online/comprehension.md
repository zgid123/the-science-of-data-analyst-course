# S203 – Greenland & Moore (2022), Large qualitative sample on dropout in open online education

## Summary in my own words
Charles Darwin University and University of Liverpool researchers interviewed 226 students who had dropped out of open online education (Open Universities Australia context) and coded 41 dropout subthemes in 10 broad reasons, plus 19 subthemes in 5 intervention themes that students said could have prevented their dropout. The crawl holds the abstract and practitioner notes only; the full text was blocked (HTTP 403), so the list of subthemes and verbatim student quotes are not on disk. The authors' own summary for a general audience is S204.

## Key facts
- Sample – "A probability‐based qualitative sample of 226 participants captured dropout subthemes down to a 5% level of incidence"
- Structure of reasons – "Thematic analysis revealed 41 subthemes within 10 broad dropout reasons."
- Students proposed preventions – "This study also captured students' suggestions for what might have prevented their dropout."
- Personal-context causes are often treated as out of reach – "Many intervention subthemes address personal and learner context dropout factors that have often been considered uncontrollable and unavoidable."
