# S200 – Roberts et al. (2016), Student attitudes toward learning analytics: "the Fitbit version of the learning world"

## Summary in my own words
Four focus groups (41 undergraduate and postgraduate students at Curtin University, Australia) discussed dashboards and early alerts. Students feel anonymous in large courses and some would welcome a personal message from a tutor when they struggle, yet most preferred automated emails over staff emails for fairness. Others feared that a "bad signal" would demotivate, overwhelm or push a struggling student to drop the unit, and many rejected being "babied" or micromanaged. Students also doubted that LMS login data reflects real learning. The source gives contrasting student voices on being flagged as at risk.

## Key facts
- Students feel anonymous in big courses – "they’re already like a faceless number to some extent."
- A personal tutor message is welcomed by some – "I think any measure to personalize a learning experience especially in the big university like Curtin, it can only be a good thing"
- Most preferred automated emails, for equity – "the majority of students reported a preference for automated emails over emails from teaching staff"
- Alerts should name support services – "we’re here to help you. These are the options available to help you, feel free to come and see us."
- Doubt about LMS data – "There—there’s like a risk of the data not being accurate,"
- A negative alert may push a student to quit – "Probably like dejected...might give up and drop the whole course or unit"
- Repeated messages feel like surveillance – "The lecturer’s continuously watching you is pressuring."
- Rejection of hand-holding – "We’re not here to be babied, we’re all like you’ve got to be selfmotivated."
- Off-campus students feel isolated – "it can feel pretty isolated at times where you don’t know what’s going on or how everybody else is traveling."
- Atif A., Bilgin A. & Richards D. (2015, Americas Conference on Information Systems; not registered) surveyed 85 mostly first-year students – "the majority (90%) wanted to be contacted immediately when their performance in a unit become unsatisfactory"
- Reimers G. & Neovesky A. (2015, 7th International Conference on Computer Supported Education; not registered) – "reported students supported the use of automated alerts"
