# S202 – Tri-C (2026), Course withdrawal as an early warning signal

## Summary in my own words
Cuyahoga Community College's institutional research office analyses its Student Course Withdrawal Survey (about 300 responses on the new instrument, over 10,000 on the old one) with students' open-ended comments. Mental and physical health was the most-selected reason; just over 30% said content or teaching did not fit their learning needs; about a quarter cited work schedule conflicts; about one in five found expectations unclear or the format a poor fit. Comments describe disorganised course shells, unclear due dates, slow grading and "teaching themselves". The report argues withdrawal is a timing problem: early signs (missed low-stakes quiz, students who stop opening materials) appear weeks before alerts, which come around weeks 4–7, and many students never ask for help. It stresses faculty are closest to the signals but need support without unrealistic workload.

## Key facts
- Sample – "The newer instrument has roughly 300 responses so far and should be treated as an early but useful signal"
- Health top reason – "Mental and physical health concerns were the most frequently selected reason in the new survey."
- Learning fit – "Just over 30 percent of respondents reported that the course content or instructional approach did not fit their learning needs."
- Teaching themselves – "the feeling that they were largely teaching themselves without enough timely guidance."
- Work – "Roughly one quarter of respondents cited job or work schedule conflicts."
- Withdrawal protects other obligations – "withdrawing from a course can become the only available way to preserve employment, family obligations, or progress in another class."
- Unclear course shells – "students described disorganized course shells, outdated syllabi, shifting or unclear due dates"
- Early signals – "a student who stops opening course materials, a pattern of late submissions, or a confused message asking what is due."
- Alert timing – "many alerts are activated several weeks into the term, often around the fourth to seventh week in 14- or 16-week courses."
- Students do not ask – "Many students who are falling behind do not announce that they are falling behind."
- Reasons for not asking – "Students may be embarrassed, overwhelmed, unsure whether their problem is serious enough, uncertain whom to contact"
- Faculty are closest – "Faculty are often the people closest to the first evidence of student difficulty."
- Faculty workload – "without creating unrealistic workload?"
- Trellis Strategies "Some College, No Credential" survey (cited, not registered) – "Trellis found that many former students left without speaking with faculty or staff before leaving."
- Jaggars S. S. (2014, American Journal of Distance Education; not registered) is cited on community college student voices about online versus face-to-face courses – "Choosing between online and face-to-face courses: Community college student voices."
