# S007 – Learning analytics: ethical issues and dilemmas (Slade & Prinsloo, American Behavioral Scientist 2013)

## Summary in my own words

Slade and Prinsloo argue from a sociocritical perspective that learning analytics raises ethical questions of power, surveillance, consent, de-identification and the classification of students, and they propose six principles: learning analytics as moral practice, students as agents, student identity and performance as temporal constructs, student success as complex and multidimensional, transparency, and the claim that higher education cannot afford not to use data. They then list practical considerations: who benefits; conditions for consent, de-identification and opting out; vulnerability and harm, including bias, labelling and stereotyping in predictive risk profiles; and data collection, analysis, access and storage. Their recommendations include informing students of the uses of their data and the models, giving students the chance to prove predictions wrong, setting an expiry date for data, and using robust, representative datasets. The text read is the authors' manuscript (running head, page numbers of the submission), hosted in the Commonwealth of Learning's open book "Learning Analytics: A Primer".

## Key facts

- Ethical challenges – "including location and interpretation of data; informed consent, privacy, and deidentification of data; and classification and management of data"
- Six principles – "We propose six principles as a framework for considerations to guide higher education institutions to address ethical issues in learning analytics"
- Principle 1 – "Principle 1: Learning analytics as moral practice"
- Education is not causal – "Education is primarily a moral practice, not a causal one."
- Principle 2 – "learning analytics should engage students as collaborators and not as mere recipients of interventions and services"
- Consent and collaboration – "Not only should students provide informed consent regarding the collection, use and storage of data"
- Principle 3 and data lifespan – "Data collected through learning analytics should therefore have an agreed-upon lifespan and expiry date, as well as mechanisms for students to request data deletion under agreed-upon criteria."
- Principle 4 and data quality – "our analyses vulnerable to misinterpretation and bias"
- Principle 5 – "higher education institutions should be transparent regarding the purposes for which data will be used, under which conditions, who will have access to data and the measures through which individuals’ identity will be protected"
- Principle 6 – "higher eduction institutions cannot afford to not use learning analytics" (sic, "eduction" in the source)
- Informing students of uses and models – "there are few, if any, reasons not to provide students with information regarding the uses to which their data might be put, as well as the models used"
- Opt-out – "Students may, however, choose to opt out of institutional initiatives to personalize learning – on condition that students are informed and aware of the consequences of their decision."
- Bias in risk profiles – "We suggest that the potential for bias and stereotyping in predictive analysis should be foregrounded in institutional attempts to categorize students’ risk profiles."
- Chance to disprove predictions – "Institutions should provide additional opportunities for these students either to prove the initial predictive analyses wrong or incomplete"
- Representative data – "institutions should aim to ensure that analyses are conducted on robust and suitably representative datasets"
- Labelling as harm – "labeling (where students may be branded according to some combination of characteristics"
- Context transfer – "Data harvested in one context may not be directly transferable to another."
