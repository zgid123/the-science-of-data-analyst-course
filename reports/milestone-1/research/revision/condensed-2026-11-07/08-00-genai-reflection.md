# 8. Generative AI Usage and Team Reflection

Generative AI was used in the three roles the brief allows, simulated stakeholder, sparring partner and reviewer, and
as a research assistant, never as a source of facts about the university or its students [1]. Table 8.1 sets out
each use and its control.

**Table 8.1. Roles of generative AI and their controls**

| Role | How AI was used | Control |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Simulated stakeholder | Five isolated personas answered prepared questions asked by an AI interviewer (Section 2.2) | Persona traits cite published accounts; statements labelled and checked by team members |
| Sparring partner | Personas challenged the team's understanding, e.g. doubting a five-point rise in one semester (VPM5) | Each challenge got a recorded decision (Table 2.4) |
| Reviewer | An isolated AI reviewer checked Sections 1–7 with the prompt in Appendix IV | Each critique got a decision and a reason (Table A.13) |
| Research assistant | AI agents found and summarised published sources | Every fact is tied to a saved source and a quoted passage |

The team accepted nine critiques, partly accepted two and rejected one. It fixed the AQ3 data cut-off, which
contradicted the risk table (Table 6.2), because the cut-off decides what is known at prediction time. It defined the SMART Goal's
population and pooling rule, which the reviewer found unclear, because a target that depends on which courses are
counted could be met by changing the courses (R4). It kept the Business Goal's mention of early
identification, which the reviewer questioned, because the brief's own example of a suitable goal names it and the
stakeholders asked for that timing (PM1, AD1).

Several points still need real evidence. The baseline, capacity figures, data delay and the limits of AQ4 are
assumptions or stakeholder estimates that the university's records must replace in Milestone 2. The early timing of
disengagement is supported by studies elsewhere [7], [8], not yet by the university's data. Acceptance of contact
based on study activity rests on one simulated student and on studies abroad [12] and needs confirmation from real
students in Vietnam. Because the personas speak from published accounts, the interviews show plausible needs rather
than verified ones.
