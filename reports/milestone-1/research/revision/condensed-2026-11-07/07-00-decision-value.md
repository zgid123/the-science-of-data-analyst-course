# 7. Decision Supported and Expected Value

The analysis of Sections 4–6 serves three decisions, made by different people at different levels, and Table 7.1
classifies each one. Three classifications are used. Gorry and Scott Morton [24] combine two older ones. Anthony's levels
separate strategic planning, which decides on objectives and the resources to reach them, from management control,
which ensures that resources are used effectively, and from operational control, which ensures that specific tasks
are carried out. Simon calls a decision programmed to the extent that it is repetitive and routine with a worked-out
procedure. Bezos
separates one-way doors, which are nearly irreversible and need careful deliberation, from two-way doors, which can
be reversed and decided quickly [25].

**Table 7.1. Decisions supported and their classification**

| Decision | Who decides, using what | Level | Programmed? | Door | Evidence needed |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| D1. Whom to contact at each review point, by whom, with which offer | Lecturer-advisors and support office; AQ4 list | Operational control | Programmed | Two-way | A list with higher recall than the simple rule (AQ3); staff check each name |
| D2. Where to place advisors' and tutors' time each semester | Program Manager; AQ1–AQ2 and KPIs | Management control | Semi-programmed | Two-way | A pattern repeated over semesters, correct records, staff agreement (PM7) |
| D3. Whether to extend to all courses and link data routinely | Leadership, legal, data office | Strategic planning | Non-programmed | Close to one-way | Pilot results, legal approval, AQ1 and KPIs for pilot and comparison courses (VAD4, VPM9) |

The level of each decision sets how much the project must prove. D1 is frequent and reversible, so a transparent
rule checked by staff is enough to start. D2 moves scarce staff time, so it needs patterns repeated over semesters.
D3 commits the university's data practice and students' trust, so it needs AQ1 and the KPIs for pilot and comparison
courses before and after the pilot; whether contact itself caused any change needs the design that the evaluation
plan of Milestone 4 sets out.

The expected value follows. Students who fall behind are contacted while catching up is still possible, by someone
they know and with a practical offer (ST5, ST6). Staff spend their limited time on the students who most need it
(VCL4). The program manager sees which courses and groups lose students during the semester (VPM3). The university
gains the student-level records that Circular 83/2026 calls for [4]. Even a negative pilot result shows, at small
scale and before any one-way commitment, which part of the path does not work.
