## 2.3. Synthesised Themes and Priorities

The brief asks the team not to turn the AI's answers directly into a Business Goal, but to synthesise them in five
steps: organise, identify themes, prioritise, refine and validate [1]. This section covers the first four steps, and
Section 2.4 covers validation.

### 2.3.2. Identify Themes

Six themes recur across the stakeholders. Table 2.3 states each theme, the statements that support it and the
stakeholders who raised it.

**Table 2.3. Themes across the stakeholders**

| Theme | What the stakeholders said | Raised by |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| T1. Late detection | Non-completion becomes visible to the program only after the semester, to the support office only on the warning list, and to the lecturer only around week 5 or 6 (PM1, PM5, AD1, CL5, ST4, DO5) | All five |
| T2. Early start of disengagement | Students begin to drift in the first weeks, and the first sign is usually a missed quiz or assignment (PM3, CL1, CL2, ST3, ST10) | Program manager, lecturer, student |
| T3. Small, fixed capacity | Staff can follow up only a few dozen students a week, so they need a short list with a reason for each name (PM8, CL10, CL11, AD3, AD4, AD10) | Program manager, lecturer, advisor |
| T4. Who contacts, and how | Contact helps when it comes from someone the student knows and offers something practical; generic emails are ignored (AD5, AD8, ST5, ST6, ST9) | Advisor, student |
| T5. Trust in the data | Records must be linked, student status must be correct, meanings must be agreed, and the purpose must be lawful (DO1–DO9, PM9, CL3) | Data officer, program manager, lecturer |
| T6. Use for support only | Results must never label students, affect grades or records, or evaluate lecturers (PM10, CL9, ST8, AD9, DO10) | All five |

Theme T3 rests on two capacity figures that recur in the rest of the proposal, both of them the advisor's own
estimates. First, each of the three officers of the support office can follow up ten to fifteen students in a normal
week, about 30–45 for the office, a figure the advisor gave as 30–40 in the validation round (AD3, VAD5). Second, each
lecturer-advisor, a lecturer who also acts as academic advisor (cố vấn học tập) for one or two classes of students,
can contact three to five students a week (AD3, VAD5). The lecturer's own account fits the second figure: the
lecturer can give one to one and a half hours a week to this work and would act on a list of five names with a reason
for each (CL10, CL11).

### 2.3.3. Prioritise

The brief asks the team to rank the themes by their link to the organisation's goal, their effect on decisions and
their feasibility [1]. Table 2.4 applies these three criteria to each theme.

**Table 2.4. Priority of the themes**

| Theme | Link to the organisational goal | Effect on decisions | Feasibility | Priority |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| T1. Late detection | Direct: completion is the outcome the program reports | Makes decisions within the semester possible | Data already held (DO1) | Core |
| T2. Early start | Direct | Sets when staff can act | Quiz and assignment records are the most reliable LMS data (CL2, DO2) | Core |
| T3. Capacity | Indirect | Sets the length and order of a list | A known limit, with no new budget (PM8) | Core |
| T4. Contact | Indirect | Sets the action for each student | Depends on staff, not on data | Defines the actions of AQ4 |
| T5. Data trust | Enabling condition | A gate before any use | Needs governance decisions (DO7) | Constraint for Section 6 |
| T6. Support only | Enabling condition | Limits how results may be used | A matter of policy | Constraint for Sections 6 and 7 |

Themes T1–T3 form the core of the project: completion is the outcome the program reports, the early signs set when
staff can still act, and capacity sets how long a list of students can be. Theme T4 defines the actions among which
staff choose, which the prescriptive question of Section 4 (AQ4) uses as its alternatives. Themes T5 and T6 do not
define what the project measures but limit how it may work, so they become the data and privacy constraints of
Section 6 and the decision limits of Section 7.

### 2.3.4. Refine

Refining the core themes gives the problem statement of Section 1.2: students who stop taking part are noticed late
and only some of them are noticed, although their disengagement usually begins in the first weeks, and staff who can
follow up only a few dozen students a week have no shared, timely and trusted information on whom to contact first
and why. Refining also removed requests that lie outside the project. Changing assessment during the semester is
excluded, because the lecturer can change support but not assessment (CL7). Students' circumstances outside the
course, such as work and family, cannot be changed by the program (PM6), so the project addresses the university's
response to them, not the circumstances themselves. Admissions, financial aid, the evaluation of lecturers (CL9) and
automated decisions about students (AD9) are excluded for the reasons given in Section 1.2.
