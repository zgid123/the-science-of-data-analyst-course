## 3.2. SMART Goal

The SMART Goal fixes what is measured, for whom and by when. Its figures need one assumption, stated first. The
scenario gives no real completion rate for the university, and the brief asks that any baseline not taken from real
data be marked as an assumption rather than presented as a fact [1]. The proposal therefore assumes a baseline
completion rate of 70%. Before any target is committed, this assumed figure is replaced by the real rate, recounted
from the university's records for the same courses, as the program manager asked (VPM8).

> **SMART Goal.** Raise the course completion rate of students enrolled after the add-drop period in the pilot
> courses from their recounted baseline (assumed 70%) by five percentage points (assumed target 75%) by the end of
> the 2027–2028 academic year.

The pilot courses are a small number of the program's online and blended courses, chosen before the pilot begins in
Semester 2 of 2026–2027. The data office can support only a few courses with a consistent setup at first (VDO4), and
fixing the courses in advance prevents the target from being met by changing which courses are counted. Their
completion rate is compared with that of similar courses outside the pilot, so that a good result cannot come simply
from choosing easy courses (VPM9).

The timeline has three stages. The baseline is the completion rate of the same courses in the two academic years
2024–2025 and 2025–2026, recounted under the definitions of Circular 83/2026, so that approved leave and transfers do
not count as non-completion (VPM8). The pilot runs in Semester 2 of 2026–2027. The target is assessed at the end of the
2027–2028 academic year. The program manager doubted that five points could be gained in one semester (VPM5) and
accepted the goal in this longer form (VPM7).

Table 3.1 checks the goal against the five SMART criteria.

**Table 3.1. The SMART Goal against the five criteria**

| Criterion | How the goal meets it |
| :---- | :---- |
| Specific | It concerns one outcome, passing the course, for one population: students enrolled after the add-drop period in the pilot courses of one program of about 1,200 students, in a faculty of about 4,000 students (both figures are scenario assumptions). Completion means passing the course (VPM6). |
| Measurable | It is measured by the primary KPI, the completion rate pooled over the pilot courses (Section 3.3), against a baseline recounted for 2024–2025 and 2025–2026 under Circular 83/2026 (VPM8). The assumed values are 70% before and 75% after. |
| Achievable | At a baseline of 70%, 30 of every 100 enrolments end without a pass; five more passes per 100 enrolments means that one in six of those students finishes. The pilot is small enough for the weekly follow-up capacity of the support office and the lecturer-advisors (AD3, VAD5). The program manager accepts the target over a full academic year rather than one semester (VPM5, VPM7). Published gains from early-warning programs are contested [19], so the target remains an assumption to be reviewed after the pilot. |
| Relevant | It measures the Business Goal directly, and it answers the needs of the program manager and the support office for real completion and earlier contact (PM2, AD1). |
| Time-bound | The pilot runs in Semester 2 of 2026–2027, and the target is assessed at the end of the 2027–2028 academic year, against the same courses' rate in 2024–2025 and 2025–2026. |

The SMART Goal measures progress, not the effect of contact. Students who are contacted may have passed anyway, as the
advisor warned (VAD4), so a rise in completion shows that the situation has improved but not why. Whether contact
itself raised completion needs a comparison design, which Section 7 discusses.
