**Interviewer (opening prompt, brief 4.2):** see scenario.md, role = DO – Learning Technology and Data Officer.

**Interviewer (Q1):** From where you sit, what would you like the university to be able to do better with the information its systems already collect about teaching and learning?

**DO:** Honestly, my first wish is a basic one: to be able to put the data we already hold side by side. Today the LMS logs, the academic affairs records and the quality assurance surveys share a student ID, but nothing links them automatically, so every request means I export by hand and match things up in spreadsheets. Second, I'd like us to agree on what a number means before we report it. Every lecturer sets up their course page differently, so "activity" in one course isn't the same as in another, and in any case clicks are not learning. Third, the student status records need to be exact enough to tell approved leave or a transfer apart from a student who has really dropped out; otherwise any dropout figure is shaky. And I'd want whatever comes out to reach the people who actually act, the advisors and lecturers, not just sit in a report for managers. One caveat: I think legal has to sign off on the purpose before we link LMS logs to student records. That's my own view from running these systems, not something the university has decided.

**Interviewer (Q1 follow-up):** You said results should reach the people who act. Who receives data or reports from you today, how often, and in what form?

**DO:** Mostly the academic affairs office. They're my main customer: I prepare extracts from the LMS for them, usually as spreadsheet files, and in my experience the requests bunch up around the end of a semester, when grades and student status are being finalised. Beyond that it's ad hoc. A faculty or a manager asks for something like course activity for a programme, I pull an export and send it by email. Some lecturers look at the built-in reports on their own Moodle course pages, but I think only a minority do, and that's my impression, not something I've counted. What strikes me is who isn't on that list. Advisors don't get anything from me on a regular basis, and as far as I know they can't easily see even basic information about the students they look after. The quality assurance office runs its surveys separately, so I don't receive or pass on those results. So today it's on request, files rather than anything live, and it mostly goes to administrators rather than to the people who talk to students.

**Interviewer (Q2):** What information about students' courses and study activity exists today, who owns each part, and how easy is it to bring together?

**DO:** There are three main pieces, and each has a different owner. The LMS is mine to run: activity logs, quiz and assignment records, submissions. But the course pages belong to the lecturers, and each one sets theirs up differently, so the same kind of record can mean different things from one course to the next. Enrolment, final grades and student status sit in the academic management system, which the academic affairs office runs. The surveys belong to the quality assurance office. All three use the same student ID, so in principle you can match them, but nothing links them automatically. Today I do it by hand with exports and spreadsheets, which works for one request but not on a regular basis. I'd also add a few cautions. The status records have to separate approved leave and transfers from real dropout, and I'm not confident they always do. I assume some data we'd want sits with outside parties or can't be used for privacy reasons. And the LMS only shows part of how students learn; whatever happens offline or on other tools isn't there.

**Interviewer (Q2 follow-up):** You said manual matching works for one request but not regularly. During a semester, how fresh could information from the LMS and the academic management system realistically be when it reaches staff, and what would limit that?

**DO:** Realistically, and this is my own estimate rather than anything we've tested, LMS activity could reach staff with a lag of about a week if I do the exports by hand. That's because it competes with everything else I do. On the academic management side, the information is only as fresh as the academic affairs office keeps it. Grades and status changes often get entered in batches, and in my experience that can be well into or after the semester, so a leave of absence or a transfer may not show up for weeks. The main limits, as I see them, are these. First, people: matching is manual and I don't have a dedicated analytics budget or staff. Second, the systems: I'm not sure our infrastructure can handle the full logs at scale, or remove individual students who opt out. Third, rules: until legal agrees the purpose for linking the two systems, I shouldn't do it routinely at all. And fourth, agreement between offices on whose status record is the correct one, otherwise fresher data just spreads errors faster.

**Interviewer (Q4):** What would make you confident that information prepared for staff during a semester is correct and on time?

**DO:** A few things would make me confident. First, a shared process between the academic affairs office, student affairs and my unit, so we all check against the same student status record and agree who corrects what. Right now, if a number looks odd, I can't easily trace it back and say which office's record is right. Second, agreed meanings. If "active in the course" means something different in each lecturer's setup, I'd want that written down before anyone acts on it, otherwise a correct export can still give a wrong picture. Third, I'd want to be able to check a sample by hand: pick a few students and confirm against the source systems. If I can't do that, I wouldn't trust it myself. On timing, I'd feel confident only with an agreed schedule each office can actually keep, not a promise that depends on me finding a free afternoon. And honestly, I'd want legal sign-off settled first; a report that might be pulled halfway through the semester isn't really on time.

**Interviewer (Q5):** What would need to be decided, and by whom, before staff could use student activity information in their work?

**DO:** From where I sit, the first decision is the purpose, and that has to come from leadership with legal and whoever handles data protection here. Under the new Personal Data Protection Law, as I understand it, consent is tied to a purpose and silence doesn't count as consent, so someone senior has to say exactly what the information is for and whether students agree to it. Second, who may see what. I'd want the owners of each system, academic affairs, quality assurance and my unit, to agree which staff get access, at what level of detail, and that nobody uses shared accounts. Third, what happens when a student is flagged. Once staff can see a student is struggling, I believe the university is obliged to do something, so the faculties and student affairs need to decide who follows up, and that a person, not a number, makes the judgement. I'd also want lecturers and advisors involved in deciding this, otherwise it's just extra work to them. I've heard of a university whose project stalled for months in ethics review; I'd rather settle these points first.

**Interviewer (Q6):** What rules or concerns about privacy, consent and access would apply, and how are they handled today?

**DO:** The main one is the Personal Data Protection Law, which has applied since the start of this year. As I understand it, consent has to be given for each purpose, silence isn't consent, and consents collected under the old Decree 13 still count. Then there are the Ministry's rules for the education database: no shared accounts, strong passwords, and a breach must be reported within 72 hours. In 2024 the Ministry also told universities to classify the personal data they hold and review their internal rules. And from 15 November, Circular 83 makes the accuracy of student status records a compliance matter, not just good practice. How it's handled today is honestly patchy, and this is my impression, not an audit. Each office controls access to its own system. I give out LMS extracts when there's an official request, and I strip what I can. But I'm not aware of consent that covers linking activity logs to grades or status for this kind of purpose, and I don't think our classification work is finished. Like many organisations, we've been slow on this.

**Interviewer (Q7):** What could go wrong technically or ethically if such information were used to act on individual students?

**DO:** Quite a lot, in my view. Technically, the biggest risk is acting on a wrong record. If the status data doesn't separate approved leave or a transfer from real dropout, someone could contact a student who has done nothing wrong, or miss one who has quietly gone. Then there's the meaning problem: low clicks might just mean a student studies from downloaded files or in a group, or that the lecturer barely uses the course page. Clicks are not learning, and the LMS only shows part of what students do. Honestly, I don't think our data is clean enough to feed anything automated yet. Ethically, I worry about labels sticking: once a student is marked "at risk", staff may treat them differently. If we use the data for a purpose students didn't agree to, that's a legal problem as well as a trust problem. And if we flag students but nobody has time to follow up, I think that's worse than not knowing, because the university knew and didn't act. Finally, a single extract sent to the wrong person is a breach we'd have to report.
