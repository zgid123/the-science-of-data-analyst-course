**Interviewer (validation):** [validation-brief.md, points 1–5] Would you accept this kind of contact if you were told about it at the start of the course? What should the first message say?

**ST:** Mostly you've got it right, especially that it starts early, that contact should come from someone who knows me, and that it should offer something practical, never "at risk". One correction on point 1: for me it didn't show first in missed quizzes. First I skipped a session and rushed a quiz on my phone, and I kept logging in and clicking through, so on the system I probably looked fine for a while. So week 5 might already be a bit late for someone like me. On point 4, please add that my family or employer never get told. What's missing, I think, is the reason behind it. For me it was shifts and family illness, not the course itself, so a message only helps if the extension or pause it offers really exists and is easy to get. Also, I should be able to reply simply, or say "I'm fine, thanks", and not get chased every week. On point 5, I can't judge the numbers, I don't know where 70% comes from. To your question: yes, I'd accept it if I'm told at the start and it's really for support. The first message should say something like: "Hi, I noticed you've missed a couple of things recently. That happens, and there are options, like a new deadline or a short chat at a time that suits you. Just reply if you'd like either." That's my own view; some classmates would rather get a general email.
