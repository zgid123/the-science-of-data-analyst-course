Same conversation as transcripts/program-manager.md, continued after the interview (T11, 2026-11-05). The team presents its understanding (validation-brief.md) and asks the persona to confirm, correct and add; answers are written here verbatim by the persona agent.

**Interviewer (validation):** [validation-brief.md, points 1–5] Is a five-point rise in completion within one semester realistic in your view, and is completion the right main measure for the program?

**PM:** Points two, three and four are right, and I'm glad you kept "support only" and "a person decides". On point one, please correct one thing: the four or five weeks was my own judgement, not something we have measured, so don't write it as a fact about our students. The missed quizzes and assignments came from your other colleagues, not from me; I can't confirm it. On point four, add that the new circular takes effect on 15 November and changes who counts as a dropout, so the student-status records have to follow the new definitions, not only be "correct". What is missing: I need a program-level view, which courses and which groups keep producing these cases, not only names for advisors. First-year students deserve explicit attention. And you should say we will check afterwards whether the contact actually helped. On your question: honestly, I doubt five points in one semester. The 70% baseline is your assumption; I don't know our real figure yet, and with one semester you can't tell a real change from an ordinary up-and-down year, or from a change in how we count. I'd rather see the same courses compared over a year or two. Completion is acceptable as the main measure only if it means passing the course, not just staying enrolled, and only if nobody lowers the standard to reach it. My question back: who in your plan will confirm the real baseline before anyone commits to a target?

**Interviewer (validation follow-up):** Thank you. On your question: the baseline will be taken from the academic management system records of the selected courses for the last two academic years, checked with the academic affairs office and counted under the Circular 83 definitions, before any target is committed; until then 70% stays a labelled assumption. We would revise the goal like this: completion means passing the course; the target is the baseline plus five percentage points by the end of the 2027–2028 academic year, after a one-semester pilot in a few courses, comparing the same courses with their previous two years; and we report completion as progress, not as proof that contact caused it. Does this version work for you?

**PM:** Yes, this version I can take to the dean. Taking the baseline from two years of records, checked with the academic affairs office and counted under Circular 83, answers my main worry, and I appreciate that you won't claim the contact caused the change. A pilot in a few courses first is sensible too. Two small requests. Keep in mind that the two earlier years were counted under the old circular, so please recount them under the new definitions rather than mixing the two; academic affairs will know which cases changed. And choose pilot courses with lecturers who are willing, but tell me how they differ from the others, so a good result isn't simply because we picked the easy courses. Whether five points is reachable even over a year is still my opinion only; we'll see once the real baseline is known.
