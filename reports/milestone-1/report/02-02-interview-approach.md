## 2.2. Interview Approach

The interviews followed the brief's procedure: prepared questions, asked one at a time in a separate conversation for
each stakeholder, with the answers labelled afterwards [1]. Generative artificial intelligence (AI) took both sides:
an isolated AI persona answered as the stakeholder, and an AI interviewer acting for the Data Analytics Team asked the
questions and follow-ups. Team members verified every transcript.

**Personas grounded in real accounts.** Each persona trait cites a published source: students who say a "you are at
risk" message could make them give up [12], withdrawal surveys led by work, family and health [13], and Vietnamese
students who keep silent online for fear of losing face [14]; tutors who lose students between the second and fourth
assignment [15]; advisor caseloads of about 300 in the United States [16] and students who answer a known advisor
more readily [17]; the regulated withdrawal limits [3]; and Vietnam's data protection law, under which consent is
given per purpose [18]. Identity details and the capacity figures used later (AD2, AD3) are scenario assumptions.

**Questions.** Each list has seven or eight open questions covering the brief's five information groups: business
goals, success vision, challenges, decision making and risk anticipation [1]. No question names a cause, a data
source or a tool, and the words "at risk" and "dashboard" appear only if the stakeholder uses them first (Appendix I).

**Conduct and labels.** Each conversation began with the brief's role prompt, which tells the AI to answer as the
stakeholder, not to propose questions or solutions, and to mark anything outside the scenario as an assumption or
opinion [1]. The interviewer asked one or two follow-ups per interview where an answer was general or opened a new
point. Key statements were then labelled as opinion, assumption or scenario information [1] and checked by team
members against the transcript (Appendix II).
