## 6.2. Feasibility, Quality Risks and Privacy

**Feasibility.** The data needs of Table 6.1 are feasible for a small pilot. The data office can support a few courses
with a consistent setup once legal approval for linking the records has been given, while routine linking across the
whole program and any automated step must wait (VDO2, VDO4, VDO5). Exports done by hand take about a week, so a list
built from the data of week 4 can reach staff in week 5 (VDO3).

**Quality risks.** Five risks could make the data mislead the analysis, and the project handles each one: student
status that mixes approved leave and transfers with withdrawal, handled by recounting the baseline under Circular
83/2026 [4]; LMS measures that mean different things in courses built differently, handled by comparing activity within
each course; activity that is not learning [22], handled by using missed and late tasks as the main signs, with a
member of staff checking each name; records matched by hand with about one week of delay, absorbed by the data
cut-offs; and small groups, whose results are reported only above an agreed minimum size. Appendix IX (Table A.20)
sets out each risk with its source and handling.

**Privacy and ethics.** Privacy and ethics set firm limits on the project. Vietnam's Personal Data Protection Law ties
consent to a stated purpose and does not treat silence as consent [18], and the Ministry of Education and Training
requires universities to secure their education databases and to report data breaches within 72 hours [23]. The
project adopts the codes of practice for learning analytics: purpose, data, access and use explained to staff and
students; consultation, and consent before any personal intervention; access only for staff with a legitimate need; an
agreed data lifespan; and no use for grading, profiling students or judging lecturers [11], [22]. A list never exceeds
the capacity to follow it up (DO10), the student's family and employer are never told about a contact (VST2), and no
real student data are entered into public AI tools [1]. Appendix IX gives each rule with its source.
