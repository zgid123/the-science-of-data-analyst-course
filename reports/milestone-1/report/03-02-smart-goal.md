## 3.2. SMART Goal

The SMART Goal fixes what is measured, for whom and by when. The scenario gives no real completion figure, so the
proposal assumes a baseline of 70% [1]. Before any target is committed, this assumed figure is replaced by the real
rate, recounted from the university's records (VPM8). Table 3.1 checks the goal against the five criteria.

> **SMART Goal.** Raise the course completion rate of students enrolled after the add-drop period in the pilot
> courses (the program's online and blended courses chosen for the pilot in Semester 2 of 2026–2027) from their
> recounted baseline (assumed 70%) by five percentage points (assumed target 75%) by the end of the 2027–2028
> academic year.

**Table 3.1. The SMART Goal against the five criteria**

| Criterion | How the goal meets it |
| :---- | :---- |
| Specific | Students enrolled after add-drop in the pilot courses of one program (about 1,200 students, in a faculty with about 4,000 students; assumptions); completion means passing (VPM6) |
| Measurable | Completion rate pooled over pilot courses (Section 3.3); baseline recounted for 2024–2026 under Circular 83/2026 (VPM8); assumed 70% → 75% |
| Achievable | Five points means one in six non-completers finishing; pilot size fits weekly capacity (AD3, VAD5); the program manager accepts the target over a full academic year rather than one semester (VPM5, VPM7); published gains are contested [19], so the target stays an assumption |
| Relevant | Measures the Business Goal and the needs of the program manager and support office (PM2, AD1) |
| Time-bound | Pilot in Semester 2 of 2026–2027; target assessed at the end of the 2027–2028 academic year, against the same courses' rate in 2024–2025 and 2025–2026 |

Because contacted students may have passed anyway (VAD4), a rise in completion shows progress, not the effect of
contact (Section 7).
