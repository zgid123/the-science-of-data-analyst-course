## 1.1. Situation and Background

A university in Vietnam is expanding online and blended learning. In several courses, students take part less as the
semester goes on, and some do not complete the course. The university holds data from its learning management system (LMS),
 its academic management system, assignments, quizzes, grades and surveys, but has not decided which questions
to analyse or which decisions analytics should support [1]. This university is a scenario: every figure about it is
an assumption, while the figures below come from published sources about other institutions.

Online teaching is now part of regular programs in Vietnam: since 2021, a full-time program may teach up to 30% of
its content online [2]. Withdrawal is also a regulated quality measure. Circular 01/2024 caps annual withdrawal at 10%
and first-year withdrawal at 15%, and a university above 15% in the first year may not raise its enrolment quota [3].
Circular 83/2026, in force from 15 November 2026, keeps both limits and no longer counts approved leave or transfers
as withdrawal [4], so universities need exact student records.

Published studies show that non-completion is common and starts early. Retention in online learning is significantly
lower than in face-to-face settings, and blended courses complete more often than fully online ones [5]. A 2024
Vietnamese review puts university dropout at about 20–30%, highest in the first year [6]; these figures count students
who leave a program, and published Vietnamese sources give no figure on course completion in online or blended
courses. In massive open online courses, the sharpest drop in participation comes in the first two weeks [7]; in a
blended first-year course, weeks 5–6 were the best point for an early warning, and warnings based on midterm grades
came too late [8]. Students report health, finances, technology, isolation and workload as reasons [9], and Vietnamese
studies add family factors and weak coordination of student management [6]. The common response is early, proactive
contact based on early-semester data [5], [10].
