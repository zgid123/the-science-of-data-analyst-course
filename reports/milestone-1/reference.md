# Milestone 1 proposal outline: course completion and mid-semester disengagement (team N04)

Derived on 2026-11-05 from the instructor brief `final-project/milestones/01/milestone-1.md` [1], which fixes the task
list (section 5), the proposal structure (section 6), the traceability table (section 7) and the AI evidence (section 9).

## Scope and rules

- Problem chosen by the team (brief section 2): **Course completion / dropout** – low course completion rates; students
  stop taking part in the middle of the semester.
- Setting: the brief's starting scenario (section 3) – a university expanding online and blended learning, with LMS,
  academic records, assignments, quizzes, grades and surveys available, and no agreed analytical question yet. The
  team acts as the university's Data Analytics Team.
- Required chain: Stakeholder Needs → Business Goal → SMART Goal → KPI → Analytical Questions → Analytics Type →
  Candidate Technique Family → Data Requirements → Decision Supported.
- Milestone 1 analyses no dataset, trains no model and proves no cause. Data requirements stay at concept level;
  open datasets may be named only as possible sources. Every baseline and target is a labelled assumption.
- Real-world context on course non-completion (published studies, institutional reports, accounts from students,
  lecturers, advisors and program managers) grounds the simulated stakeholder personas. It is background for the
  personas and for the problem context, never a fact about the scenario university.
- Generative AI acts as simulated stakeholder, sparring partner and reviewer (brief 4.1), never as a source of facts.
  The team's own question list drives each interview, one question at a time, with follow-ups; statements are
  labelled stakeholder opinion, assumption or scenario information.
- No causal wording for observational associations. No algorithm is fixed.
- Length: 6–10 pages of main content, appendices excluded. English source, Vietnamese twin.

## 1. Project Context and Problem Statement

- Context, problem, scope, why analytics is needed (brief section 6 row 1, section 3).
- Real-world background on course non-completion and mid-semester disengagement in online and blended courses.

## 2. Stakeholder Analysis and Requirement Findings

- Stakeholder map: every role relevant to course completion (at least Program Manager, Course Lecturer, Student
  Support Officer / Academic Advisor), their initial assumed goals and possible decisions (Task 1).
- Interview approach: personas grounded in real-world accounts, question design (5–8 open questions per role over
  Business Goals, Success Vision, Challenges, Decision Making, Risk Anticipation), follow-ups, statement labels
  (Tasks 2–3).
- Synthesis: Organize → Identify Themes → Prioritize → Refine → Validate; synthesis table, conflicts and constraints,
  validation table (Task 4).

## 3. Business Goal, SMART Goal and KPIs

- One Business Goal as an organisational outcome, its stakeholder origin, importance and decision (Task 5).
- SMART Goal with assumed baseline and target; 1 primary KPI and at most 2 secondary KPIs with rationale (Task 6).

## 4. Analytical Question Ladder

- At least one question each at Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive, Prescriptive, logically linked (Task 7).
- Five-criteria check table; at least one Before → After example.

## 5. Proposed Analytic Approach

- Analytics type, candidate technique family and expected output per question; no final algorithm (Task 8).

## 6. Preliminary Data Requirements and Feasibility

- Per question: data and variables, possible source, granularity and time, availability, privacy and ethics note
  (Task 9); access, quality risks, privacy and ethics of learning analytics.

## 7. Decision Supported and Expected Value

- Who uses the results, which decision, decision level (strategic/tactical/operational, programmed/non-programmed,
  one-way/two-way door) and why the level sets the depth of evidence; expected value (Task 10).

## 8. Generative AI Usage and Team Reflection

- AI roles used, suggestions accepted or rejected with reasons, what still needs real evidence (brief section 6 row 8).

## 9. Traceability Matrix

- One table: Business Goal, SMART Goal / KPI, AQ, Analytics Type, Candidate Technique Family, Data Needed, Decision
  Supported (brief section 7).

## Appendices

- Interview questions per stakeholder; key transcript excerpts from every interview; the validation round; AI critique
  → team decision → reason table; reviewer prompt and the feedback used (brief section 9).
