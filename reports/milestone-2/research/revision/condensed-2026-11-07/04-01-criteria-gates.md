## 4.1. Criteria and Must-Have Gates

Every candidate was scored on the ten criteria of the brief: AQ coverage, population fit, unit and granularity,
outcome availability, feature availability and timing, temporal coverage, documentation and provenance, license and
access, quality feasibility, and ethics and representativeness [1]. Each criterion takes 0 (not met or not
stated), 1 (partly met or uncertain) or 2 (well met). A score of 2 requires a fact recorded from the candidate's own
documentation. A criterion scores 1 when the documentation states part of what is needed, as for OULAD's outcome,
whose Withdrawal value is documented while its other values are not.
Appendix II gives each score with the fact behind it.

The scores support the discussion, but the gates decide. A candidate that misses a must-have gate is rejected
whatever its total, because the brief warns that a high score cannot make up for a missing target, missing timing
for a predictive question or a missing right of use [1]. The gates of Section 2.1 apply these warnings to this
project: G1 secures the outcome and the date of each withdrawal, G2a and G2b the timing of tasks and activity, G3 a later run for testing
AQ3, G4 the right of use, and G5 privacy and the links between tables. Facts that a candidate's documentation does not
settle, but its files can, were marked as conditions for the structural verification of Section 6.
