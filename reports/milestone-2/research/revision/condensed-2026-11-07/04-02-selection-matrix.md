OULAD is selected because it is the only candidate whose documentation meets the timing and license gates and
records a dated withdrawal; whether its result values also separate pass from fail was a condition checked in the
files. It records a final result per enrolment with Withdrawal and a dated unregistration [3], dated assessments and
submissions, and daily clicks per student [3], under a CC BY 4.0 license [2]. Every other candidate fails G1
and G2a. UCI 697 labels students by degree at the end of three or four years and holds only semester aggregates
[4], [5]. XuetangX has dated activity but a binary dropout label and no stated license [8]. OULAD's total of 12 is
modest because its documentation leaves several facts open. The selection was therefore made conditional on the
files confirming them: the separation of pass from fail (G1), enough runs per module for testing (G3), and a student
identifier that persists across runs (G5). Section 6 confirms G1 and G5 and shows G3 met only in part.

Three limits come with the choice. OULAD describes UK distance-learning modules from 2013–2014, and its modules were
selected partly because many students failed them [3], so its failure and withdrawal rates are likely to exceed a typical program's. It holds
no delivery mode, year of study, attendance, leave or transfer status. The authors also advise analysing module runs
by start month, February or October, separately [3], which limits how many comparable runs each module has. No
supplementary dataset is selected: no candidate holds the missing contact, capacity or attendance data, and the only
other source of dated activity, XuetangX, lacks a license and a pass or fail outcome [8].
