## 7.2. Data Gaps and Controlled Revisions

No question is fully covered, but each keeps its core, so the gap rules of the brief lead to narrowing rather than to
dropping a question [1]. Partial coverage allows a question to be kept or narrowed with its limits stated, and a
prescriptive question without action evidence stays a future decision requirement with a lower claim [1]. The team
applied these rules as follows.

AQ1 and AQ2 keep their outcome, module, module-run and timing parts. They lose the comparisons by delivery mode and
year of study and the attendance sign, which OULAD does not hold. AQ3 keeps its target, its two cut-offs and its test
on a later module run, with two limits. Before the end of week 2 almost no module run has a task due, so the week-3
list rests on activity alone. Each module and start month has at most one earlier run to train on. CCC and the
February runs of EEE and GGG have no earlier run of the same start month, so they can test a model trained on other runs but cannot
train one of their own. Its comparison between first-year and
later-year students is replaced by a check across deprivation band and disability, which OULAD records. AQ4 keeps its
ranking under an assumed weekly capacity, and the contact record, the outcome after contact and the follow-up KPI
remain requirements for the pilot; no claim about the effect of a contact will be drawn from OULAD.

The Business Goal, the SMART Goal and the decisions are unchanged. The eight controlled revisions, C01 to C08, are
summarised in Section 1.2 and given in full, with the old and new content, the data evidence and the effect of each, in
Appendix V.
