## 2.2. Requirements by Analytics Level

The four questions climb the analytics levels, and each level needs a different kind of evidence rather than simply
more data [1]. Table 2.2 sets out the minimum data for each level and what that data can show.

**Table 2.2. Data requirements by analytics level**

| Level | AQ | Minimum data | What the data can show |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Descriptive | AQ1 | Outcome, course and semester dimensions, dated tasks, the denominator of students enrolled after add-drop | Rates and the timing of first missed tasks |
| Diagnostic | AQ2 | AQ1 data plus dated signs and comparison groups, with background attributes to check consistency across groups | Associations, not causes |
| Predictive | AQ3 | A labelled outcome, predictors timestamped before the prediction point, enough semesters for testing on a later one | Accuracy on a later semester at a fixed list length |
| Prescriptive | AQ4 | Actions (contacts), constraints (capacity) and outcomes after action | A ranking under constraints; without action and outcome data, no claim that a contact helps |

The prescriptive level is the hardest to supply from open data. It needs records of actions and of what followed
them; where these are missing, the brief requires a recorded data gap rather than a recommendation dressed as
prescriptive analytics [1]. In the scenario, the university keeps no contact record yet, so the action part of AQ4 is
declared a gap before the search begins, and weekly capacity is treated as an assumption of the baseline, not as data.

The requirements also carry ethics conditions. AQ1 needs no sensitive attribute, and its results go to the program by
group, never by name. AQ2 and AQ3 may use background attributes such as disability or the deprivation of a student's
area only to check that associations and accuracy hold across groups, never as reasons to list a student. For AQ4,
the contact record and any referral for financial or personal support are personal data, used only for support under a
stated purpose and never for grading.
